# Dark Hours App Controversy (August 2026)

## Summary

In early August 2026, indie developer **Terry Godier** — previously celebrated for his RSS reader *Current* — became the center of a multi-layered controversy. His Claude-built astronomy app *Dark Hours* was blocked from the App Store, and Daring Fireball's **John Gruber** published a full-throated defense of it, only to issue a rare retraction (his first in 24 years) after it emerged the app had *started* as an astrology app. The story then metastasized: the app appeared to be a close copy of an existing open-source project — also named Dark Hours — that was also built with Claude. Godier stopped distribution and pointed users to the original, but the affair became a flashpoint in the [[concepts/vibe-coding|vibe-coding]] authenticity debate.

## Key Actors

- **Terry Godier** — indie developer whose reputation, built on *Current*, took the hit. He denies having been aware of the other Dark Hours app, but stopped distribution and encouraged people to use the existing open-source version. "The optics ain't great."
- **John Gruber** — Daring Fireball. He was "misled, both overtly and through omissions," and had even shown Godier a draft of his post before publication. He retracted rather than corrected, keeping the original text online for transparency and apologizing to the App Store reviewers he had besmirched.
- **Miguel Beher** — creator of DarkHours (no space), an open-source "astrophotography and dark-sky planner" also built with Claude; the project remains live at darkhours.app.
- **Apple / App Review Board** — vindicated: the original rejection of the astrology app was correct.

## The Vibe-Coding Authenticity Debate

The affair crystallized a question Ernie Smith had been circling in his inbox: **"How human should vibe-coded apps be if you're going to share them publicly?"** If Claude did copy an existing open-source project and turn it into an app, "the whole idea of whether you created it becomes an open question." Even if it didn't, he argues, a project must "reflect you, your passions, and your creativity" — there "has to be a point when the tool stops and you begin." See [[concepts/vibe-coding]] (redirects to [[concepts/harness-engineering/agentic-workflows/vibe-coding]]).

## The AI-Flattery Email Phenomenon

Smith connected the incident to the surge of project-pitch emails that open with AI-generated flattery: "so much of what's been built, soup to nuts, is AI. Even the email is AI, which is why it's so flattering." What reads like a personalized, can-do project is actually a PR hustle — inbox-level sycophancy (see [[concepts/ai-sycophancy]]) that erodes trust in the weekend tinkerer. A parallel scandal hit **Hank Green**, accused of using AI in a script; he clarified he sometimes uses AI for research and effectively apologized to his team.

## Significance

- Gruber's first retraction in Daring Fireball's 24-year history — a cautionary tale about trusting a source's framing, especially when it flatters the writer's priors.
- A case study in how [[concepts/vibe-coding|vibe-coded]] apps can shade into [[concepts/ai-slop|AI slop]] and copyright-adjacent copying of open-source work (see [[concepts/ai-generated-code-policies]]).
- Platform-policy angle: Apple's astrology ban was correct, but opaque rejection processes invite conspiracy theories — a dynamic long analyzed by [[entities/cory-doctorow]] in his writing on platform gatekeeping.

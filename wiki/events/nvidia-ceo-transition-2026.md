# NVIDIA CEO Transition (November 2026)

On **November 12–13, 2026**, NVIDIA's Board of Directors announced that co-founder **Jensen Huang resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and member of the Board, effective immediately** — ending a 33-year tenure as CEO. The official press release confirmed facts that had been *rumor-level* on 2026-11-11 (WSJ report of board preparing removal, HN 1085 pts; see [[entities/jensen-huang]] "Board preparing to oust him").

## Official announcement (NVIDIA press release, primary source)

- **Jensen Huang resigned** as President, CEO, and Board member, effective immediately.
- **Colette Kress** — EVP & CFO since September 2013 (previously ~25 years in finance roles at Cisco, Microsoft) — was appointed **Interim CEO while retaining the CFO role**, until a permanent successor is named. The Board launched a search considering internal and external candidates.
- **Harvey C. Jones** — entrepreneur, technology executive, venture investor, former CEO and Executive Chairman of **Synopsys** — was appointed NVIDIA's **first-ever Chair of the Board**.
- Board statement (Jones): the Board has "full confidence in Colette and the leadership team"; operations, customer commitments and product roadmap expected "to continue without interruption."
- Kress statement: committed to "building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years."
- Q3 FY2027 earnings unchanged: Tuesday, **November 17, 2026**, 1:20 p.m. PT release, 2 p.m. PT call — Kress's first earnings call as interim CEO.

## Trigger chain (as reported)

The resignation follows the November 11 WSJ/NYT reports: an alleged affair with a woman **suspected of spying for China**, an export/"spy relationship" scandal, and a board that had retained an outside firm to identify CEO candidates. Huang holds ~3.4% of NVIDIA with **no dual-class voting rights**, so he could not block a determined board.

## Congressional testimony (Nov 12 follow-up)

Per TechCrunch (via Daring Fireball), **"Ex-Nvidia CEO" Huang agreed on Wednesday to testify before Congress by Thanksgiving**, after House Republicans threatened a subpoena:
- **Brian Mast** (House Foreign Affairs Chair), **John Moolenaar** (House Select Committee on the CCP Chair), **Rick Crawford** (House Intelligence Chair) sent a joint letter on **November 4** requesting his appearance.
- Testimony will be **behind closed doors**, but Mast expects it to be **recorded on video and transcribed**.
- Mast: "Jensen and I have been very at odds... on export controls, but Nvidia is a very important company to the United States of America... he has very serious questions to answer."

## Market & industry reaction

**Gary Marcus** (same day, "Jensen Huang resigns."): while conceding Huang "was one of the greatest CEOs in history," he argues Huang "had to go" — he "enabled and even lobbied for Chinese AI, subverted US policy, and carelessly left himself vulnerable to both blackmail and espionage," with blindness to conflict of interest leaving NVIDIA open to "endless litigation and investigation." Concrete market facts:
- Stock: **−8% premarket → −13% at the opening bell**.
- Central question for the AI industry: whether an NVIDIA with an acting CEO still executes the **billions promised to Anthropic in its IPO** — now "front and center" for [[events/anthropic-ipo-roadshow-2026]] (roadshow opened Nov 10).
- Marcus's framing: Huang was "the ringmaster of all the insane circular financing" (cf. [[concepts/ai-infrastructure-circular-financing]], [[concepts/ai-bubble-economics]]) — "all that might collapse." He expects congressional investigations to continue, and names **China as the biggest immediate winner**, with Lutnick and Trump possibly caught in the crossfire.

## Open questions

- Permanent CEO search: internal/external candidates; earlier reporting (Nov 11) floated Apple hardware chief **Johny Sruji** at rumor level — unconfirmed.
- Whether NVIDIA's Anthropic IPO backstop and its $100B–$250B upstream purchase commitments survive unchanged under interim leadership.
- Whether the recorded closed-door congressional transcript is released, and what it reveals about export-control lobbying.

## Employee petition to restore Huang (Nov 13)

Wired reported on Nov 13 that Nvidia employees organized a petition calling for Jensen Huang's
return (HN 637pts/247c). The thread's framing is mostly incentive analysis rather than moral
judgment:

- **Compensation is the visible stake.** jmathai notes employees "took a pretty big pay cut this
  week"; dghlsakjg estimates a ~30% RSU hit for people whose retirement sat in NVDA. hn_submit:
  "Most don't care about his extramarital affairs" — the fear is job security without him at the
  helm. thewhitetulip waits for Microsoft to "enter the drama like they did with OpenAI".
- **Accountability-sink objection.** Qem finds it odd that Huang took heavy flak for relationships
  with adult women while "those involved with Epstein remain mostly undisturbed", and asks whether
  an AI-bubble burst would get blamed on the affairs "instead of the rampant misallocation of
  resources". Duanemclemore names the mechanism: "a convenient accountability sink to free them
  from having to examine the structural causes."
- **The power-dynamic split.** doug_durham: both women were involved in his business, one was an
  employee, so consent can never be stated confidently given the power asymmetry. skeledrew
  counters that nothing indicates coercion and the affairs surfaced "without an ounce of blame".
  allears separates morality from competence: even if consensual, it was "piss-poor judgment" that
  damaged the company's reputation and cost shareholders money.
- Reuters (HN 301pts) reported on Nov 12 that Huang had a relationship with a *second* employee.

## Open-model ecosystem impact (Nathan Lambert, Nov 14)

Nathan Lambert (Interconnects/Robotic, "Open-source AI without Jensen", Nov 14, 2026) argues the resignation is **"a major setback for the US development of an open model ecosystem"** — the only substantive open-model analysis of the transition in the wiki. Unlike the market and political framing above, his case is ecosystem-capability:

- **Funding dries up.** Open models were "one of Jensen's more ambitious, founder-style bets. He has been **flooding the zone with funding** in a way that will very likely dry up." Nvidia's stated interest in open models (avoiding customer concentration in buyers who build their own chips) survives, but "supporting the open ecosystem is a tricky puzzle more than an engineering problem."
- **Jensen as shield, not cheerleader.** "He would stop a lot of shit from going wrong once early winds were stirring. He was willing to use his power on open models." Lambert's near-term fear is **state-level sloppy regulation → national regulation**, backed by a swell of Democratic funding toward AI-regulating candidates (~$50M, Axios Oct 14 2026) whose "open models are unsafe" priors are uneducated on the benefits. "We've been **one, strong Chinese open-weight model away from a crisis** since this summer" — no major hack from a popular Chinese open-weight model yet, "but it is a matter of when, not if."
- **Talent, not money, is the binding constraint** (Lambert endorses Dylan Patel verbatim from the ChinaTalk + SemiAnalysis emergency podcast): "It's not money, it's talent, man." The best people sit at Anthropic/OpenAI/DeepMind/Meta; the rest scatter across a hundred startups. **Moonshot is "skill-wise probably just as good as the US labs"** — its deficit is compute and deployment, not skill. DeepMind, Grok and Meta "have regularly fallen behind the frontier of Chinese labs, and they're spending more." Full quote: [[entities/dylan-patel]].
- **Open-weight league table (Nov 2026).** **Nemotron** had "the right level of ambition and strong people, but it hasn't delivered to the point of being called 'mission accomplished'" ([[entities/nvidia-nemotron-3-ultra]]). **Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small** is "the best Western open-weight model in a while" but "**far behind Kimi K3.1 Pro**", with high expectations for Zhipu's next GLM ([[entities/thinking-machines-lab]], [[concepts/inkling-2-small]]).
- **Commoditized intelligence is far off.** With compute scarce and Anthropic/OpenAI absorbing capacity, inference startups can't even get chips, so **there is little margin competition on open weights** — and DeepSeek's DeepInfer showed open models can be served at "giant inference margins" ([[concepts/deepinfer]]). The labs with model→datacenter→chip vertical integration win.
- **Who fills the vacuum.** Mira Murati's next round at a **$40B valuation is "a done deal"**, but "that won't be enough to keep pace with the closed frontier for long"; the likely remaining allies are **hyperscalers — Zuckerberg, Andy Jassy, or Satya**. "We're regressing in the operational capability of our movement, when the stakes and attacks are destined to rise."
- **Media runs on timing, not information.** "All of these Jensen affairs were known, but getting them out at the right time, right after the midterm political swing, took down the king" — isomorphic to the summer's Jacob Coxon episode, whose safety-culture revelations became national only through datacenter/Overton-window timing. Lambert expects the same path is available to open-model safety narratives.

Counter-context: the open-model optimism case (ownership of intelligence, enterprise post-training as the right lane) is preserved in [[concepts/open-source-ai-must-win]] and [[concepts/state-of-open-source-ai-2026]]; Lambert's claim is about the **political patron** being removed, not about the technical thesis failing.

## GPU-smuggling investigation as the alleged real cause (November 16)

Tom's Hardware (Nov 14) reported analysts' claim that the underlying cause of the resignation was not the
affairs reporting but a **GPU-smuggling investigation**: more than **1.5 gigawatts of banned chips (~1.5M
AI GPUs)** tracked flowing to China despite export controls
([HN 61 pts](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50498051); raw:
`raw/articles/2026-11-16_tomshardware_gpu-smuggling-huang.md`). The same day Reuters/FT reported allies
discussing a **Huang comeback as CEO** (169 pts, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50511982) — contested
by commenters citing Treasury Secretary Bessent's public demand that the board pick an **external** CEO
given export-control scrutiny, and [waximabbax]'s point that a public company board cannot simply reverse
its decision on Silicon Valley pressure. HN sentiment split between "board/regulators know something we
don't" and "no evidence of wrongdoing has been shown — the Malaysia/Singapore channel claims are from
Nvidia's own public press releases" ([anon291]). The **DRAM price-fixing suit survived a motion to
dismiss** on Nov 15 (514 pts) — see [[concepts/dram-price-fixing-lawsuit-2026]] — adding a second
antitrust front to the memory/HBM supply chain underpinning NVIDIA's roadmap. WSJ follow-up (Nov 16):
"Nvidia's Days as an Open Checkbook for the AI Economy Are Numbered" (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50513876).

## The financial architecture of the bubble (Ed Zitron, Nov 18)

Ed Zitron's long-form "The NVIDIA Bubble" (wheresyoured.at, 707 lines / 56KB) supplies the **financial and smuggling-chain detail** the rest of this page lacks, connecting the personal scandal to a structural revenue-integrity problem. Several of these entities (Megaspeed, Alice Huang, Culper Research, Supermicro/Wally Liaw) had **zero prior coverage anywhere in the wiki**:

- **The Alice Huang / Megaspeed figure escalated fast.** Illegal chip sales routed through Alice Huang's empire moved from $4.8B → "at least" $7.5B → **$12B** (March 2024 – June 2025, per WSJ) within a single week of reporting. Megaspeed bought "nearly $12 billion worth of top-of-the-line NVIDIA chips" — including Blackwell parts it claimed had demand "well above supply" — and shipped them to Chinese firms. Zitron notes Jensen Huang both created demand (via CoreWeave) and dealt directly with Megaspeed.
- **Culper Research short report (May 2026).** Alleged that **20%+ of NVIDIA's FY2026 revenue ($43B+) came from sales through China**. Zitron's framing — "Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg" — makes the smuggling operation a *distribution channel*, not a few rogue salespeople.
- **Supermicro / Wally Liaw arrest (March 2026).** Culper directly connects Megaspeed to **Supermicro**, the popular AI-server maker. Co-founder and board member **Wally Liaw was arrested** with co-conspirators for trying to sell **over $2.5B in chips** to a Chinese passthrough entity Culper believes is a Megaspeed affiliate. Liaw had previously resigned in 2018 over an accounting scandal, returned in 2021, and was re-elected to the board in 2023. Zitron's point: a co-founder/board member of one of the largest AI-server manufacturers — **70%+ of whose revenue is NVIDIA-GPU-powered servers**, and which was NVIDIA's *third-largest customer* months into Megaspeed's $12B purchases — was arrested selling to the *same* smuggling network Alice Huang ran.
- **Alice Huang → DeepSeek.** Companies connected to Alice Huang **invested in DeepSeek** — the lab whose Q1 2025 efficiency result "spooked the world" by suggesting fewer NVIDIA GPUs were needed. The smuggler's counterparty and the company undermining the GPU-demand thesis are the same orbit.
- **Retail / pension / private-credit exposure.** Zitron's broader warning: individual investors, **insurers, pension funds, and private-credit vehicles** are exposed to the AI-infrastructure buildout — and to NVIDIA's revenue-integrity risk — **directly or indirectly**, largely without realizing it. This reframes the resignation from a personal- scandal story into a balance-sheet-contagion story, consistent with the same-day Tom's Hardware GPU-smuggling report (~1.5 GW / ~1.5M banned AI GPUs) above and Marcus's "ringmaster of circular financing" framing.

This section is the **revenue-integrity / financial-plumbing** complement to the political (congressional testimony), personal (affairs), and ecosystem (Lambert) angles already on this page. Raw: [[raw/articles/wheresyoured.at--the-nvidia-bubble--711785b3.md]].

## Huang's first public statement: "would be honored" to return (November 18)

Five days after resigning, Huang broke his silence with a **first-person post on X** (`@JensenHuang`, Nov 18) — the first public, on-the-record confirmation of the comeback speculation that had until then been only *reported* via allies ([[#Comeback speculation and smuggling probe (November 15-16)|above]]). Key admissions and signals:

- **Ownership of the personal scandal:** *"I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life. I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation."* This is the first time Huang publicly acknowledged wrongdoing *and* confirmed an **active Board independent investigation**.
- **Explicit return ambition:** *"Since my departure, I've seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA's employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO... If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."*
- **Patriotic framing:** *"I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts... Nowhere but in America could a company like NVIDIA be possible."* — widely read as courting political/Trump-side support amid the export-control scrutiny.

The post materially upgrades the wiki's coverage from *reported* comeback talks to **Huang's own stated willingness to return**, while the Board search and Treasury's demand for an *external* CEO remain unresolved. Raw: [[raw/articles/x.com--jensenhuang-status-2122726005219737649--f161af35.md]].

### Daring Fireball: the "50-Yard Line" political read

Around the same moment, **Daring Fireball (John Gruber, "The 50-Yard Line," Nov 18)** reframed the scandal's political dimension, revisiting Huang's March 2026 quote that *"America's unique advantage that no country could possibly have is President Trump."* Gruber's thesis: corporate leaders normally stay near the political "50-yard line" (Tim Cook's Obama→Trump→Biden→Trump survival strategy) so they can "just turn around" after each election; Huang instead *"broke for Trump's end zone"* — million-dollar inauguration tickets, the gold trophy, an appointment to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology — an alignment that was *"unprecedented in modern U.S. history"* and that now leaves NVIDIA (unlike Apple) exposed to *"charges of corruption and/or breaches of national security."* Gruber credits Huang with at least believing what he said, and notes Trump had floated **Tim Cook's name to succeed Huang at Nvidia**. Raw: [[raw/articles/daringfireball.net--2026-11-the-50-yard-line--1dbb4704.md]]. See [[entities/daringfireball-net]].

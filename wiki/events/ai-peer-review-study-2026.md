# AI Article Reviews Dominate Human Peer Reviews (King & Worthington, 2026)

**Gary King (Harvard/IQSS)** and **Steven Worthington** published *"AI Article Reviews
Dominate Human Peer Reviews at Their Best: Evidence and Policy Choices"* (page last
updated 2026-10-22). The headline finding: **AI-generated article reviews are now
better — by every measure the authors could construct — than reviews written by
well-compensated human experts** who were themselves judged to be of unusually high
quality by journal editors and authors.
Project page: https://gking.harvard.edu/peerless/

## Study design (per Mollick's amplification)

Ethan Mollick (@emollick), amplifying the paper on 2026-10-23, described the
evaluation as covering **124 Harvard social-science papers** spanning statistical
analyses, formal models, and qualitative work, with **Claude (Opus 4.8)** as the
reviewing model. By every constructed measure, the AI reviews beat the human expert
reviews. Mollick's framing: *"As academics, we need to figure out how to adapt the
practice of science to AI, fast."* — and *"This is not going to be the only area in
which AIs beat humans."*

## Policy discussion

The paper does not simply declare peer review obsolete. It discusses journal policy
choices with **both concerning downsides and potential upside paths**:
- increase publication speed and quality;
- reduce adversarial reviewer–author interactions;
- improve the experience of editors, reviewers, authors, and readers;
- advance science.

The authors explicitly flag the downsides alongside the opportunities, making this a
policy-oriented piece rather than a pure benchmark result.

## Why it matters

Peer review is a flagship "human expert judgment" task. A rigorous, Harvard-led study
claiming AI superiority across 124 expert-reviewed papers is a strong signal in the
ongoing debate about **AI replacing high-skill professional evaluation work** — and it
comes from a quantitative-social-science methodologist (King), not an AI lab, lending
it methodological credibility. It connects to broader threads of AI-outperforming-humans
claims and AI-referee experiments in academia.

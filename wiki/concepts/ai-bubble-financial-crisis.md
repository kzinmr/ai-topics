## Overview

A 2026 debate over whether an AI capex-bubble correction could propagate into a *financial* crisis --
not just a tech-sector drawdown. The scenario and its rebuttal center on whether AI's damage channel
is a **credit channel** (mark-to-market losses in private credit) or a **labor channel** (displacement),
and whether the two are conflatable.

## The 2028 credit-contraction scenario (von Krogh simulation)

Jakob von Krogh circulated a thought-experiment simulation (HN 447 pts):

1. AI capex bubble bursts -> datacenter/GPU collateral values are written down
2. Private credit funds holding those assets face **mark-to-market losses**
3. Losses freeze **middle-market lending**, the segment private credit dominates
4. The credit contraction feeds back into the real economy -- a recession *caused by* the AI bust, distinct from AI-driven job losses

## The rebuttal (Harsesian)

Labor displacement is not a credit event. Even severe white-collar displacement reduces costs for firms
that adopt AI; it is a redistribution shock, not a balance-sheet shock. A recession would require the
*financing* of AI capex to be the transmission channel, not the *effects* of AI.

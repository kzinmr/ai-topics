# AI-Generated Language Readability

Why AI-generated text has become measurably harder for humans to understand — and why this is an *alignment* artifact, not a bug.

## Core finding (arXiv:2610.29162, Oct 2026)

A psycholinguistics study (HN 209pts, Oct 24 2026) investigated a set of psycholinguistic features known to correlate with human processing difficulty — object relative clauses, long syntactic dependencies, low-frequency n-grams — across a novel corpus of AI-generated text in diverse domains. Key results:

- AI models use difficult processing features **more often than paraphrases generated by round-trip translation**.
- Compared with human writing, **all nine frontier and near-frontier models** use more inanimate subjects with animate verbs and more low-frequency n-grams; most also use longer dependencies, more stacked nouns, and more reduced object relatives in conversation.
- There is **substantial variation across model families** — readability is a per-lab choice, not an inherent property of LLMs.

## The information-density hypothesis

The authors' explanation: human language is optimized for **successful communication**, but AI-generated text is increasingly optimized for other goals — math, code, reasoning — where the pressure is for **information density** rather than communicability. Proof-of-concept: fine-tuning a language model under a **length penalty** makes it diverge from human-like linguistic behavior, reproducing the hard-to-read profile.

## Why it matters

- Reframes "AI slop" from a style complaint into a measurable misalignment between human processing demands and RL objectives that reward density (the same objective pressure that drives over-long chain-of-thought and terse technical prose).
- Suggests a cheap remedy axis: readability-aware post-training objectives rather than "write simpler" prompting.
- Connects to the [[concepts/linguistic-vertigo]] phenomenon (users' felt sense that AI text has become harder to parse) by supplying the first systematic psycholinguistic measurement.

# Anti-LLM Sentiment in Hobby Programming Communities

A growing cultural phenomenon in niche hobby programming communities (OSDev, LangDev, EmuDev, demoscene, code golfing, chess engine development) where LLM usage is increasingly rejected, banned, or viewed as a form of "cheating" that misses the point of the craft.

## Origins

Michael Fogus (author of "Born Against") identifies several converging factors:

1. **Process > Product**: In these communities, the process of mastering a difficult field IS the product. Getting something that "runs" is a nice-to-have, not the goal. LLMs shortcut the process, removing the learning that these communities value.

2. **Earned Expertise**: Respect in niche dev communities is earned through years of forum participation, sharing elegant code, displaying genuine curiosity, and contributing deep domain knowledge. LLM-generated contributions bypass this social contract.

3. **Poisoned Wells**: Early LLM adopters in these communities often lacked deep understanding and a vocal subset of community members reacted with vitriol, creating a toxic dynamic that hardened anti-LLM positions.

4. **Skepticism of Newcomers**: The increased use of LLMs in mainstream software development has generated broader skepticism — when newcomers claim deep knowledge, communities increasingly suspect LLM-assisted persona construction.

## Communities Affected

Documented anti-LLM stances have been observed in:

- **Chess Engine Development**: Explicit rules against "AI-generated contributions" adopted
- **OSDev** (Operating System Development): Value deep understanding of hardware-software interfaces
- **LangDev** (Programming Language Development): Requires understanding of parsing, type theory, compiler design
- **EmuDev** (Emulator Development): Demands cycle-accurate hardware knowledge
- **Demoscene**: Celebrates human creativity under extreme technical constraints
- **Code Golf**: Emphasizes human ingenuity in minimizing code size

## Dynamics

The opposition is not purely reactionary — it reflects a genuine philosophical stance:

- **Gatekeeping as quality control**: Communities see gatekeeping as protection against dilution of expertise. LLMs represent an existential threat to the slow, deliberate knowledge accumulation that defines these spaces.
- **Haven from AI**: As AI dominates mainstream software development ([[concepts/vibe-coding]]), these communities increasingly position themselves as refuges for human craftsmanship.
- **Ambiguous policies**: Many communities adopt vague "no AI" rules that are difficult to enforce but serve as cultural signals.

## Relationship to Broader AI Skepticism

This grassroots anti-LLM sentiment in hobby programming is distinct from, but complementary to, broader [[entities/gary-marcus]] movements. Where AI critics like Gary Marcus focus on capability limitations and safety concerns, hobbyist opposition centers on cultural values: mastery, craftsmanship, and the intrinsic value of difficult knowledge.

## Implications

- **Growing cultural divide**: Software development is bifurcating into AI-assisted mainstream and AI-resistant niche communities
- **Talent pipeline effects**: As hobby communities become anti-LLM havens, they may attract developers seeking environments free from AI pressure
- **Tooling fragmentation**: Development tools may bifurcate — AI-native (Cursor, Copilot) vs. AI-free (traditional editors)

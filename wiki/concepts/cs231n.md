# CS231n — Stanford Deep Learning for Computer Vision

**CS231n** ("Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition") is Stanford's flagship undergraduate/graduate **deep learning course**, taught since 2015 and historically led by **Fei-Fei Li** with Justin Johnson and Andrej Karpathy. Its publicly released **lecture notes and assignments (cs231n.github.io)** became one of the most widely used free deep-learning curricula on the internet — the standard on-ramp for a generation of vision and later LLM practitioners.

## Why it matters in AI history

- **The Karpathy effect**: Andrej Karpathy ([[entities/andrej-karpathy]]) TA'd (2015–16) and taught (2017) the course. His assignments — building backpropagation from scratch, then a bare-bones autodiff engine (`micrograd` ancestor) — established the "implement it from scratch to understand it" pedagogy he later scaled via **nanoGPT**, **makemore**, and **[[entities/karpathy-projects|neural networks: zero to hero]]**.
- **Open teaching materials**: lecture notes, IPython notebooks, and the famous **assignment 1–3 pipeline** (linear classifier → two-layer net → CNN with custom backprop) were free, rigorous, and code-first — predating and inspiring the modern open-course ecosystem.
- **Era transition**: after the transformer takeover, Karpathy's successor-flavored successor content (CS25 "Transformers United", nanoGPT exercises, "LLM from scratch") carried CS231n's pedagogy into the LLM era. See [[concepts/llm-course-roadmap]] for how it maps onto today's curriculum.

## Canonical syllabus (classic version)

| Week | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Image classification, k-NN vs. linear classifiers, loss functions, optimization |
| 4–6 | Backpropagation from scratch; training neural nets (init, regularization, optimization) |
| 7–9 | CNN architectures (LeNet→AlexNet→VGG→ResNet), pooling, transfer learning |
| 10–11 | Recurrent nets, sequence models (LSTM era, later attention/transformers) |
| 12+ | Detection, segmentation, generative models (GANs/VAEs), reinforcement learning |

Assignments are pure **NumPy** (no framework) in the classic years — deliberate, so students internalize the computation graph.

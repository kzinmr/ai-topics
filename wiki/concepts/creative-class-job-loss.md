# Creative Class Job Loss

The empirical case that AI — not a generalized recession — is hollowing out US media, film, and arts employment, first quantified at scale by Joseph Politano (Apricitas Economics).

## Headline numbers (Sep 27 2026, HN 384pts Oct 24 2026)

- **>200,000 jobs lost** in US "creative" industries over four years; **~50,000 in the last year alone** — one of the worst stretches for media employment in modern US history, comparable in intensity/duration only to the 2001 and 2008 recessions.
- **Movie & sound recording (Hollywood): >100k jobs lost**, nearly **one-third of the sector**; at summer 2026 depths, employment was the lowest since 2008 and approaching a 30-year low. Nearly half of all media job losses concentrate here.
- **Written publishing: >70k jobs lost** — partly a decades-long technological grind (newspapers/magazines), but streaming, live broadcasting, and graphic design show genuinely unusual downturns.
- Counter-signal used as the AI attribution control: **in-person entertainment is still growing at a healthy pace** — the losses are concentrated in *digitally producible* work (novels, photorealistic images, soundalike music, video), exactly the modalities generative models commoditized.

## Attribution caveats (why the AI share is contested)

- Media firms are deliberately coy about AI use — to avoid public backlash **and to preserve the legal basis for their copyrights** (a striking disclosure incentive working against measurement).
- Confounders: Hollywood consolidation, offshoring of content production, and audience displacement from traditional media to social/UGC creators (itself increasingly AI-assisted).
- Politano's strongest evidence is sectoral selectivity: *every* digital-arts subsector is shrinking while physical entertainment grows.

## Why it matters for the AI-economics debate

Pairs directly with Torsten Slok's "no AI payoff outside tech" finding ([[concepts/ai-economics]]): AI shows up in corporate **cost lines (layoffs) years before it shows up in revenue or TFP**. The creative class is the leading indicator — the first labor market where generated output substitutes for human output at near-zero marginal cost. Watch for whether the pattern propagates to white-collar "legible work" ([[concepts/legible-work]] if present — otherwise see [[concepts/ai-economics]]).

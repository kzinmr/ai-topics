# Agent-Generated Software

**Agent-generated software** sits a step beyond [[concepts/vibe-coding]]: instead of a human iteratively prompting a model to write an app, an *agent* (or a supervised swarm of agents) plans, builds, tests, plays, and iterates on the software largely on its own, with the human supplying taste, goals, and review. The defining cases (Sep 2026) share one architecture: **generating agents plus critic/QA agents in a loop**.

## Evidence at the consumer scale

- **Valve's "Peaks"** (released 2026-08, covered Sep 22): a polished co-op bartending game on Steam built by a team of ~14 people **with Claude Code as their main internal tool** — agents handled large parts of the build; the human team did review and taste. Anthropic's writeup is the most credible first-party account of agentic development on a shipped consumer product.
- **Ethan Mollick's Rescue Raiders remake**: asked Claude to remake a 1987 abandonware game; Claude "iterated back-and-forth with critic & art agents" until a playable modern version emerged. A one-person, prompt-driven reproduction of a niche 1987 game with new graphics, tech trees, and multiplayer.
- **Simon Willison's TTS pelican videos** (Sep 14, Sep 23): scripted conversations + Gemini TTS to produce fully synthetic explainer videos — content *products*, not just apps, generated by chained agents ([[concepts/gemini/gemini-3-8-tts]]).

## What makes it distinct from vibe-coding

| | Vibe-coding | Agent-generated software |
|---|---|---|
| Human role | Prompt + eyeball output | Set goals, review, taste |
| Iteration driver | Human turns | Agent↔critic loops |
| QA | Manual testing | Playtest/QA agents, screenshots |
| Scale unit | App after app | Shipped products, games, media |

## Economics & limits

The cost curve matters: Epoch's ~13×/year decline in cost-per-performance makes hours-long critic loops economically viable ([[concepts/token-economics]]). Remaining bottlenecks: verifying agent claims (the "verify, don't just eyeball" principle), distribution/curation of the resulting flood, and IP/rights questions (voice cloning, abandonware, synthetic celebrity media — see deepfake backlash cases).

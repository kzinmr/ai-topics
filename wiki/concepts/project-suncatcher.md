# Project Suncatcher (Google orbital TPU research)

**Project Suncatcher** is Google's research moonshot to explore scaling machine learning compute in space: a planned constellation of solar-powered satellites carrying **data-center-class TPU trays**, harvesting near-continuous orbital solar energy. Announced September 25, 2026 with a white paper on constellation design, control, and communication, plus initial radiation-testing results on Google TPUs.

## "Hello World" milestone — first AI response from orbit (Oct 30, 2026)

The prototype satellite launched **October 1, 2026**. After weeks of commissioning (powering up the host and TPU trays, running early ML tests), Google Research announced on October 30 (Travis Beals, VP Paradigms of Intelligence) the first-ever AI response generated by a data-center-class TPU in orbit:

- During a ground-station pass, Google uplinked the first prompt to an onboard **Gemma 4 open model**, asking for a haiku about being in space.
- As the satellite passed over **Svalbard, Norway**, the downlinked poem confirmed success: *"Blue marble below / Data streams in silent grace / Starlight feeds my core."*
- Follow-up tests: space-themed limericks and telemetry prompts reporting payload technical status.

## Roadmap and open questions

- **Next tests**: TPU performance under thermal stress and radiation exposure in orbit.
- **Next milestone**: twin interconnected satellites in **2027** (from the original two-prototype-by-early-2027 plan, now partially achieved).
- **Core bet**: orbital solar power is ~8× more efficient than ground-based solar, potentially breaking terrestrial power/land constraints on AI compute. See [[concepts/google-spacex-ai-compute-deal]] for the launch-partnership context and [[concepts/ai-bubble-economics]] / [[concepts/subprime-data-center-crisis]] for the terrestrial-power-constraint framing this moonshot answers.
- **Caveats**: single-vendor reporting (Google), prototype scale, no published cost-per-FLOP or bandwidth economics; radiation/thermal durability of TPU-class silicon is the unsolved risk the 2027 twin-satellite tests target.

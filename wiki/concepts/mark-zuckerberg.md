# Mark Zuckerberg

**Mark Zuckerberg** is the founder and CEO of [[entities/meta|Meta]]. As the person who directly shapes Meta's AI strategy — including the formation of Meta Superintelligence Labs and Meta's open-weight model program ([[concepts/open-source-llms|Llama]] family) — he is a central voice in the debate over how superintelligence should be governed, distributed, and aligned.

## "The Future is for Everyone" (August 2026)

On August 11, 2026, Zuckerberg published a ~6,500-word essay, "[The Future is for Everyone](https://www.meta.com/thefutureisforeveryone/)", laying out a complete philosophy for how superintelligence should be developed and deployed. It is one of the most detailed public statements by any frontier-lab leader on AI governance, positioning Meta directly against centralized / safety-first approaches from other labs.

### The Three Pillars

Zuckerberg frames the essay around three principles:

1. **Individual empowerment as the source of prosperity** — progress comes from putting power in individuals' hands, not from centralized institutions.
2. **Invention as the primary purpose of superintelligence** — the greatest contribution of superintelligence will be discovering new knowledge (drugs, science, products), not automation of existing work.
3. **Balance of power as the foundation of safety** — safety comes from checks and balances between competing actors, not from a single benevolent controller.

### The Central Argument: Against a "Singular Benevolent Superintelligence"

The essay's core claim is that alignment-as-centralization is fundamentally flawed:

> "Humanity is not a monoculture. People's diverse values represent different tradeoffs they would make on important issues. There is no technological solution that can align with everyone's opposing interests and values at once. Any singular superintelligence would have to prioritize some values over others... There is no such thing as a singular benevolent superintelligence."

Zuckerberg argues the only realistic path to safety is **broad distribution** — giving every individual a personal superintelligent agent so that competing interests "naturally check and balance each other," analogous to democratic governance. His recurring thought experiment: one person with a superintelligent lawyer is an unfair advantage; everyone with one produces fairer justice.

## Personal Superintelligence Vision

The essay enumerates the concrete ways Meta intends to deliver "personal superintelligence":

- **Personal agents** that work 24/7 on health, career, finances, and relationships, with a fully private mode ("even Meta cannot see your information," modeled on WhatsApp's end-to-end encryption).
- **Creation tools** giving everyone "invention superpowers" (his 8-year-old daughter codes ideas and produces videos in an evening).
- **Entrepreneurial tools** — individuals manifest ideas without raising money or building teams; predicts *more* employment over time, not less.
- **Personalized tutors/coaches** — a "PhD in every subject with unlimited patience."
- **Scientific advances** — cites the Chan Zuckerberg Biohub's open-source virtual-cell and protein models.
- **Free/affordable access** via a **dynamic auction mechanism** that guarantees lowest-possible prices for compute while ensuring capacity is used for what people collectively value most.

## Risk Positions

Unlike a purely techno-optimist framing, the essay engages each major risk category through the "balance of power" lens:

| Risk | Zuckerberg's Position |
|------|----------------------|
| **Job displacement** | Outcome depends on the balance between automation and individual empowerment; personal superintelligence pushes toward job growth, not loss. |
| **Cybersecurity / biorisk** | Broad distribution *increases* security (open-source systems are more secure; the "long tail" gets hardened); restricting capabilities leads to centralization. |
| **Government tyranny / surveillance** | Privacy and a fully-private agent mode prevent an individual–government power imbalance. |
| **Geopolitics** | The US must lead AI and open source; warns against export controls being over-applied and against policies that slow American labs "even by a month." |
| **Existential risk / RSI** | See below — the balance-of-power answer to [[concepts/recursive-self-improvement|recursive self-improvement]]. |

## Alignment Reframing

Zuckerberg explicitly rejects the dominant lab framing of alignment as "a defensive measure for enforcing a centralized set of values" (citing a leading model that refused to draft a school letter because it judged standardized testing unethical). His alternative: **alignment means agents share the *person's* goals and values, not the company's**. He argues that achieving adoption by billions of users *requires* solving alignment-to-individual-interests — so scale itself becomes the safety mechanism.

## Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)

The essay acknowledges the RSI dilemma directly: any lab that does not let its systems direct substantial compute toward self-improvement "will inherently fall behind," risking a single self-improving system commanding more effective compute than everyone else combined. His answer is the same balance-of-power principle: the "significant majority of intelligence must be directed by people towards advancing people's goals," with frontier labs collectively building enough compute to both remain competitive on RSI and still serve individual goals.

## Policy Proposals

- **Intermediate training checkpoints to government**: leading labs should share intermediate checkpoints and technical staff so the government can harden critical infrastructure early, instead of waiting for release review — "advance national security while minimizing delays of public releases."
- **Independent board governance**: Meta's independent board gains the power to approve safety criteria for model releases; encourages other labs to adopt similar structures (noting founder-CEOs currently have extensive authority).
- **Open source**: strongly supportive; announces Meta Superintelligence Labs "will resume releasing some open source models soon"; opposes restrictions on distillation and data use.
- **Community Compacts**: a "Future Is For Everyone Fund" to give local communities benefits (jobs, school funding, energy) from data-center builds.

## Significance

The essay is notable for being the clearest articulation of the **"distribute, don't centralize"** position in the AI governance debate, standing in direct opposition to labs pursuing a single aligned superintelligence. It ties together Meta's product strategy (personal agents, open weights, glasses) with a coherent safety philosophy grounded in [[concepts/ai-alignment|alignment]] theory, [[concepts/agi-economics|AGI economics]], and political theory about the balance of power.

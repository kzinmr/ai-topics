# How Organizations Use AI: Evidence from ChatGPT

An OpenAI working paper (Chatterji, Holtz, Rakholia, Tambe, Weeratunga — August 2026) that is among the first large-scale, data-driven studies of **enterprise generative-AI adoption**. It links internal **ChatGPT Enterprise account records** to usage, worker roles, message-level task classifications, and public-company financial data through March 2026 — avoiding the recall/reporting biases of survey-based evidence.

## Methodology

- **Data**: ChatGPT Enterprise (OpenAI's centrally administered workplace product) usage records, linked to job-title and task-classification metadata and public-company financials.
- **Scale**: the worker-level sample at the six-month adoption horizon includes **>1,500 organizations** and **>17 million messages**.

## Four Stylized Facts

1. **Rapid growth** — aggregate output tokens from ChatGPT Enterprise customers grew roughly **sevenfold** between June 2025 and March 2026; within a consistent cohort (Jan 2024–Jun 2025 adopters) growth was nearly **fourfold**. About half of growth came from *already-adopting* firms (intensity), not just new adopters (extensive margin).
2. **Concentrated adoption** — among U.S. public companies, adopters are **larger, more valuable, and more R&D- and SG&A-intensive** than non-adopters. Early enterprise adoption is associated with greater prior investment in intangible and organizational capabilities.
3. **Broad but uneven use** — usage within firms spans job functions and seniority, but intensity is heterogeneous: marketing/communications workers send more messages than executives, and **early-career workers send ~8–9 more weekly messages** than senior employees.
4. **Broad task coverage** — the most common uses are writing, communication, and information synthesis, plus research, planning, data analysis, and legal/regulatory/finance work. This breadth is consistent with generative AI acting as a **general purpose technology (GPT)** for knowledge work.

## Interpretation

The findings portray enterprise AI adoption as a **broad but uneven** phenomenon: concentrated among firms with greater scale and intangible investment, while use *within* adopting firms is distributed across many worker groups and tasks but varies substantially in intensity. The long-run economic value will depend on whether dispersed individual use develops into **complementary organizational capabilities** — not just individual productivity gains.

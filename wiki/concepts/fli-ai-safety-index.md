# FLI AI Safety Index

The **FLI AI Safety Index** is a periodic evaluation framework published by the Future of Life Institute (FLI) that grades major AI companies on their safety and security practices. The index uses expert panels to assess companies across multiple domains including risk assessment, current harms, safety frameworks, existential safety, governance, and information sharing.

## Summer 2026 Edition

Released July 2026, the second edition of the index grades nine AI companies. No company received a grade above C+. Anthropic received the highest overall score (C+, 2.66), while the majority of labs scored in the D- to F range.

### Domain Breakdown

The index evaluates six domains with varying indicator counts:

- **Risk Assessment** (6 indicators): Anthropic/OpenAI tied at C+; most others at F
- **Current Harms** (9 indicators): Anthropic B- (highest); DeepSeek/Mistral F
- **Safety Frameworks** (4 indicators): B- scores at top; most labs at D or F
- **Existential Safety** (4 indicators): Everyone D+ or below; seven labs at F — the weakest domain across all companies
- **Governance & Accountability** (4 indicators): Anthropic B (highest); five labs F
- **Information Sharing** (10 indicators): Anthropic B+ (highest); DeepSeek/Mistral D-

### Key Findings

- **Existential safety is the universal weak spot.** Every company scored poorly on existential safety measures. No company scored above D+ in this domain, and seven of nine received F.
- **Transparency gap persists.** Information sharing was relatively strong at top labs (Anthropic B+, Google DeepMind B-, OpenAI B-) but collapsed at Chinese companies (DeepSeek D-, Alibaba Cloud D).
- **Chinese labs clustered at bottom.** Three Chinese companies (Z.ai D-, Alibaba Cloud D-, DeepSeek F) occupy the bottom tier, with DeepSeek at 0.47. The report includes a "Chinese Regulatory Context" note acknowledging different regulatory environments.
- **Mistral enters at F.** The French open-source company received the lowest overall score (0.33) in its first evaluation.
- **Downward trends.** Most companies that were previously rated received lower grades in Summer 2026 compared to Winter 2025, suggesting safety progress is not keeping pace with capability advances.

## Methodology

The index is based on surveys completed by company employees and evaluated by a panel of independent AI safety experts. Each of the 37 indicators (up from Winter 2025) is scored and aggregated by domain, then combined into an overall grade. The full methodology is documented in the PDF report alongside a two-page summary.

## Significance

The FLI Safety Index complements other safety evaluation frameworks (RSPs, preparedness frameworks, model evaluations) by providing a comparative, structural assessment of company-wide safety practices rather than model-specific capability testing. It highlights systemic gaps in existential safety preparedness and the growing divide between Western and Chinese AI companies in transparency and safety governance.

# Ben Thompson

**Ben Thompson** is the author of [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/), one of the most
influential paid newsletters on technology strategy and business economics. He is best known
for the **Aggregation Theory** framework, which has become a standard lens for analyzing
platform businesses — including the current AI platform race.

## Aggregation Theory (2016–)

Thompson's central thesis: the internet reversed the traditional scarcity relationship between
distribution and content.

- **Distribution is no longer scarce** — the internet (app stores, search, social feeds) commoditized
  the delivery of content and products.
- **Marginal cost of content → 0** — so competition on content/supply side collapses.
- **Scarcity moves to consumer attention** — whoever controls the *demand side* (the user relationship)
  captures the value.

Therefore the durable businesses of the internet era are **aggregators**: firms that directly
aggregate demand (users) on the demand side and compete over scarce consumer attention —
Google, Amazon, Facebook, Apple, and increasingly AI assistants and chat interfaces.

### Implications for AI

- AI assistants / agent interfaces are candidate "next aggregators" — control of the user
  relationship is the strategic prize, not model weights (which commoditize from below).
- Model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic) must move up the stack toward consumer/enterprise
  surfaces, or risk being disintermediated — a recurring Stratechery theme in the LLM era.

## Other Recurring Frameworks

- **Monopoly vs. regulatory utility** debate for platforms; the "national champion" framing.
- **AI as deflationary force** on content/labor supply costs, reinforcing aggregator economics.
- Daily articles plus weekly **Dithering** podcast and **Piling Up** / **Sharp Tech** companion shows.

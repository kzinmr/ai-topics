# The Waymo Effect — Research Collaboration Beyond the Lab

"The Waymo Effect" describes a pattern observed as Waymo scaled robotaxi service: **the real value of frontier technology increasingly flows through operational field sites that function as research environments**, and the people who bridge lab and field become the scarce resource. The term circulated via a *To Invent* piece ("To Invent Waymo, They Had to Reinvent PM") picked up by ResearchAgenda, whose author found the collaboration patterns so important they joined the company. ^[raw/articles/researchagenda-news--waymo-effect-research-collaboration.md]

## Key Observations

- **Field as lab**: at Waymo scale, real-world deployment generates edge cases, data, and design problems that no simulation or academic benchmark reproduces — the operational fleet *is* the research apparatus.
- **Role blurring**: researchers become operators and operators become researchers; research productivity depends on feedback loops between deployment and the lab, not on publications.
- **Implication for AI labs**: as [[openai]] and peers push robots, agents-in-production, and science programs (GPT-5.5 for science, materials discovery), the bottleneck shifts from model capability to the organizational machinery that converts field experience into research — a management/organizational problem, not a scaling-law problem.

## Open Questions

- Does the pattern generalize beyond physical autonomy to software agents (where "field sites" are production deployments)?
- How should research organizations compensate and credential people whose output is field feedback rather than papers?

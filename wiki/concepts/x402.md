# x402: HTTP-native agentic payments

x402 is an open payment protocol that uses the HTTP **`402 Payment Required`** status code —
reserved since HTTP/1.1 and never standardized — as a machine-readable payment request. A
client sends an unpaid request, receives 402 with the price and payment terms, signs a
stablecoin payment, and retries with the payment attached. No account, no API key, no
subscription. Brave became the first major search vendor to adopt it (Nov 12, 2026).
HN 129pts/76c.

## The mechanics

Brave's implementation makes the pattern concrete:

- The agent (not a human) holds a **CLI wallet**; the user funds it by transferring USDC over
  the Base network.
- Brave runs its **own facilitator** that accepts USDC on Base; other networks and stablecoins
  are promised.
- Payment replaces authentication: endpoints behind x402 "require payment on every request
  rather than authentication."
- Two products are purchasable this way: general web search and **LLM Context** (agentic
  grounding).

The stated benefit for agent builders is the elimination of API-key management and plan
selection — an agent can discover a paid endpoint and pay it at call time, which is the
property a key-based or subscription-based API cannot provide because it requires a human to
have provisioned an account beforehand.

## Where it fits

- Sits directly on top of the agent-side payment rail that agentic commerce needs. See
  [[concepts/agentic-commerce]] — x402 fills the settlement layer for *machine-initiated*
  purchases, where the earlier stablecoin-default and Stripe-Projects work covered
  *assistant-assisted* checkouts.
- Pairs naturally with the agentic-representation fights: [[concepts/agentic-representation-gatekeeping]]
  is about merchants *blocking* agents; x402 is a vendor *admitting* agents on a pay-per-call
  basis instead. Brave is monetizing exactly the traffic that others are trying to exclude.

## Reception

The thread is more interested in micropayments generally than in Brave specifically:

- **Supportive:** "People only hate the inconvenience of existing micro payments. The idea of
  paying for usage is actually very appealing" (kuboble); utopiah's utility-billing analogue
  (meter, estimate, settle) with AI providers fronting the balance sheet; "_ink_": a human who
  would happily pay a small fee per article instead of maintaining 45 subscriptions.
- **Skeptical on the rail, not the idea:** makingstuffs and cowlevel both attack stablecoin
  counterparty risk — no consumer recourse versus a bank, and a documented history of
  stablecoin providers freezing funds ("imagine you're Kagi and Google sues you for scraping;
  an injunction freezes your USDC"). lern_too_spel rejects permissionless blockchain payments
  outright.
- **Skeptical on novelty:** com2kid — "the least stupid use of the block chain I've ever heard
  of", with the observation that Visa could have shipped this in 1999 and the industry chose
  ads instead. war-is-peace notes anyone using an LLM is already hard-stuck in token
  micropayments, so one more meter is marginal if integrated well.

## Open questions

Whether x402 becomes a general web-monetization layer or stays an agent-only rail is the live
question. Everything in the thread that endorses it does so for *agent* callers or for
readers who want per-article pricing; everything that rejects it targets stablecoin
counterparty risk rather than the protocol shape. Brave accepting via its own facilitator also
means Brave is the custodian in practice, which is the same chokepoint the protocol claims to
remove.

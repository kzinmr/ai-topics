# Cancer Capital — Surplus Money Chasing AI Returns

Anil Dash's September 2, 2026 thesis ([original](https://anildash.com/2026/09/02/cancer-capital/)): venture capital has stopped functioning as high-risk/high-reward portfolio allocation and become **"cancer capital"** — surplus money that must chase returns wherever it can, metastasizing through the AI economy regardless of whether productive value is created. The name comes from his 2013 essay *VC isn't VC anymore*, written after a colleague observed "VCS are like a cancer."

## The Argument

**Original model of VC:** a small share of overall investment, deliberately high-risk/high-reward, because a healthy economy — like a healthy portfolio — mostly consists of stable, predictable assets (bonds, established businesses).

**Current state:** low rates and monetary expansion created "permanently more money sloshing around"; most VC funds now "aren't even pretending" to follow portfolio logic. The 2021 boom wasn't a temporary bubble — **"the boom we went through in 2021? That was all of it. That was the peak."**

**Mechanism — cancer as metaphor:** normal cells do productive work and receive energy in exchange for creating value. Cancer cells "have learned to exploit the body's natural processes to absorb more and more energy, without giving anything back." Dash's claim: much of the AI economy now works this way — value extraction without value creation, "a machine for making rich people richer while the rest of us struggle."

**Evidence cited:**
- **OpenAI as the paradigm case**: "the largest charity in world history" — Microsoft's $13B+ became an $800B valuation on a majority stake — "the greatest wealth transfer in human history."
- **AI cost externalities**: pollution from unregulated data center construction, toxic forever chemicals, communities living "literally in the shadow of these buildings."
- **YC's Michael Seibel**: his criticism of founders raising big rounds is read by insiders as "you should be grateful we're giving you millions" rather than the intended "don't count your chickens."
- **Hedge fund behavior**: money managers "desperately hoping that AI can provide them with returns," funding "theatrics" — a rising tide raising yachts, not boats.

**Prescription:** "We must do what we can to stop cancer before it spreads beyond the point of remission." Build businesses and institutions that serve broad human flourishing, not investors' need to deploy surplus capital.

## Why It Matters for the AI Wiki

- **Reframes the [[concepts/ai-bubble|AI bubble]] debate**: Dash argues there is no bubble to pop — the misallocation *is* the steady state. Bearish analyses waiting for a crash misdiagnose the pathology: capital behaves this way permanently unless the structure changes.
- **Connects to [[concepts/ai-slop]]**: slop is the labor-market symptom of the same disease — effort-asymmetry exploitation of attention, as surplus capital optimizes for deployed-not-productive output (see [[concepts/ai-slop-productivity-paradox]]).
- **Continuity with Dash's open-web critique**: the scraping/summarization assault on the open web ([[entities/anildash]]) is cancer capital applied to information infrastructure — platforms absorb value creators make without giving back.
- **Echoes Doctorow's "enshittification"** as the platform-side version of the same extraction dynamic.

## Open Questions

- Is the cancer metaphor analytically load-bearing (self-reinforcing growth regardless of host health) or polemical framing? Financial historians note VC's 2021 peak-and-crash dynamics resemble classic bubbles more than metastasis.
- What mechanism could constitute "remission"? Antitrust, monetary policy, or LP-side reform — Dash names none.
- Does the framing extend to open-source AI, which Dash elsewhere treats as the alternative infrastructure — or is even that capturable by surplus capital (cloud-subsidized model serving)?

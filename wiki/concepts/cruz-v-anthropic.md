# Cruz v. Anthropic — post-Bartz opt-out litigation

**Cruz v. Anthropic** (filed May 2026, non-class action) is the follow-on suit by authors who **opted out of the Bartz $1.5B settlement**, seeking full statutory damages of up to **$150,000 per infringed work** — roughly 50× the ~$3,000/book Bartz paid. On **October 27, 2026** The Stanford Daily reported that a federal judge ruled (in the May 2026 proceedings) that Anthropic illegally scraped and pirated books by **over 10 Stanford professors** to train Claude. HN: 529 pts.

## Plaintiffs and allegations

- Named Stanford-affiliated authors include **Tobias Wolff** (English professor emeritus, National Medal of Arts; 10 works used), **Chang-rae Lee**, plus faculty Abraham Verghese, Daniel Mason, Hayagreeva Rao, Myra Strober. Wolff and Lee opted out of Bartz.
- Allegations mirror Bartz: illegal download/reproduction/distribution from **shadow libraries (LibGen, PiLiMi)** and storage in a **permanent central library** — the specific practice Judge Alsup blocked.
- If successful: permanent injunction against training Claude on their works.

## Relationship to Bartz v. Anthropic

The Bartz ruling (see [[concepts/anthropic-copyright-settlement]]) split the question: **training = fair use** (Alsup: LLMs trained "not to race ahead and replicate or supplant" works "but to turn a hard corner and create something different"), while **piracy to build a central library = illegal**; $1.5B went to authors/publishers of ~500,000 registered works (Authors Alliance: largest AI copyright settlement). Cruz tests what remains **after** that settlement: whether opt-out plaintiffs can extract statutory damages and injunctions beyond the class remedy.

## Broader litigation landscape

- **70+ infringement suits** against AI companies, including NYT and Getty.
- **Authors Guild** suit against OpenAI/Microsoft continues; CEO Mary Rasenberger describes a "multi-pronged approach" — litigation + licensing advocacy + **national licensing legislation with Congress**.
- Stanford IP scholar **Paul Goldstein** (Bartz class plaintiff) frames the core obstacle as **transaction costs**: millions of works per training run make per-work licensing impracticable; he also flags the U.S. anomaly of having no "moral right of attribution."
- Wolff's framing: settlement "still gives Anthropic pretty free rein"; unpaid training is "a form of slave labor"; his stated worry centers on young writers' economics.

## Assessment

- Source is a student newspaper (Stanford Daily); the underlying ruling details (May 2026, Alsup) are consistent with the Bartz record already in the wiki, so `confidence: medium` pending primary court documents.
- Strategic significance: $150k/work vs $3k/settlement is the live test of whether Bartz closed the liability tail. Watch for jury trial and any injunction precedent.

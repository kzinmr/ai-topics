# AI Music Copyright

**AI music copyright** is the emerging legal domain governing how AI music-generation systems (e.g., Suno, Udio) may use copyrighted musical works for training and output. The first major European rulings in this space arrived in 2026, led by German collecting societies.

## GEMA v. Suno — Munich Regional Court (Aug 2026)

**GEMA**, the German collecting society that licenses music on behalf of composers, **won its copyright case against AI music firm Suno** in the **Munich Regional Court** (reported 2026-08-02 by The Signal). The court ordered Suno to **stop reproducing** the works in question.

Key elements:
- **Plaintiff**: GEMA (German collecting society)
- **Defendant**: Suno, an AI music-generation company
- **Court**: Munich Regional Court (Landgericht München)
- **Outcome**: Suno ordered to cease reproduction of GEMA-licensed works

This is a direct legal counterweight to the US fair-use trajectory documented in [[concepts/anthropic-copyright-settlement]] — in Germany, the collecting-society framework gives rights holders a mechanism to block AI training/output on copyrighted music, whereas US law is still weighing fair use for training.

## Contrasting Signal: Warner Music Group × Suno Partnership

In the same reporting window, **Warner Music Group and Suno forged a groundbreaking partnership** — the licensing route rather than the litigation route. This exemplifies the two-track landscape of AI music: **litigation** (GEMA v. Suno, RIAA suits) vs **licensing deals** (labels striking revenue-share agreements with AI music platforms).

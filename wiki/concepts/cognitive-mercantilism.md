# Cognitive Mercantilism

Venkatesh Rao's thesis (Contraptions, 2026-10-25): today's AI panic is structurally a form of **mercantilism applied to intelligence** — the same zero-sum worldview that preceded capitalism, with "intelligence" substituted for "wealth." Inspired by Joyce Appleby's *The Relentless Revolution* (2010).

## The Four-Part Mapping

Rao maps the four beliefs of pre-capitalist mercantilism onto AI anxiety:

1. **Fixed wealth → fixed intelligence**: intelligence is imagined as a scarce, finite resource hoarded in brains and knowledge products; national/corporate strategy becomes talent treasure-hunts and IP bullionism.
2. **Untrustworthy poor → untrustworthy masses**: "stupid people cannot be trusted to think for themselves"; democratizing intelligence is dangerous, so cognition must be governed by certified cognitive elites.
3. **Supply-side obsession**: controlling the production and price of brains/knowledge products dominates; the demand side — ordinary people as sources of demand for, and participants in the production of, intelligence — barely registers.
4. **Useless mouths → useless minds**: if 80% of humanity does low-grade cognitive labor, automating it supposedly creates "billions of useless minds" — the direct analogue of removing subsistence farmers and getting "useless mouths to feed."

## The Historical Counter-Example

The agricultural revolution disproved the fourth belief: food productivity collapsed farming's labor share without creating a planet of useless mouths — vastly more people did *other* things while enjoying vastly more food. Rao's implied argument: AI panic mistakes a transition in the *supply of intelligence* for permanent obsolescence of human cognition. The "cognitive capitalist" counterpart sees near-limitless expansion of demand and "a world full of minds whose potential has barely begun to be explored."

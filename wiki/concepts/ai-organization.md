# AI Organization

> **This page is a landing page for the AI Organization category.** For the full content index, see [[concepts/ai-organization/_index]].

## Summary

AI Organization is a framework category exploring how human organizations and work design transform when AI agents become capable of autonomous execution. It synthesizes three major models:

1. **Block's Hierarchy → Intelligence** (Jack Dorsey): Replacing middle management with World Model + Intelligence Layer
2. **McKinsey's Agentic Organization**: Five-pillar framework with Diamond Org Chart and M-Shaped Supervisors
3. **Solo Founder / Super IC**: AI tools enabling 1-person = 10-person teams

## Field Notes: Foo Camp Agentic-Organization Session (Bowne-Anderson, Nov 25, 2026)

Concrete, largely first-hand cases from Hugo Bowne-Anderson's O'Reilly **Foo Camp** lightning talk and ~10-person off-the-record session (attendees: a major tech company's Americas research-lab head, a founder running four companies with ~20 agents each, a software-factory founder, a designer building an open-source protocol for personal agents, and a co-creator of core web protocols). Source: raw/articles/hugobowne.substack.com--p-what-the-amish-can-teach-us-about--5afbb1b68f99df90.md. See [[entities/hugo-bowne-anderson]] and [[concepts/ai-organization/ai-org-from-hierarchy-to-intelligence]].

- **"Chief-of-staff agent" at company scale.** Hugo's own chief-of-staff agent reads his email, priorities, and client/student/collaborator needs overnight and proposes the day. Friends at **frontier labs** told him they are seeing comparable enterprise-scale coordination agents emerge inside the labs.
- **Full-open teams & boundary design.** One research lead's team already **shares every agent session** (what a colleague asked, tried, and why they changed direction — the finished code rarely shows this); she proposed taking one willing team fully open to discover where boundaries are actually needed.
- **Agent authority & secrets.** One company defaults to open channels with legal/personnel exceptions. A harder failure case: someone accidentally put personal material into a shared session, and by the time they tried to retract it, **agents had already learned from it**. In a large org you must know who an agent acts for, what it can read, and who it can share with. Adjacent to [[concepts/agent-security-certification]].
- **Junior-career gap under fewer managers.** Experienced people find their way to new work; early-career people get isolated when the manager-led connection disappears. A designer is building a personal agent that scouts opportunities **on the employee's behalf** — raising whose interests the agent represents.
- **Software-factory throughput bottleneck.** A factory that produces code faster than the business can ship is "making tires faster than engines" — people spend their time moving piles of tires while engines slow down (a Theory-of-Constraints view of AI code generation).
- **Principle-writing to keep only the 51–49 calls.** The most useful answer in the room: when a decision reaches you, write down the principle behind your call so the next person/agent can make it — ideally only close calls ("Supreme Court cases all day long") should land on your desk. This is judgment offloading via written principles (cf. [[concepts/spec-driven-development]]).

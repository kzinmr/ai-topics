# President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST)

The **President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST)** is the
US President's standing advisory body on science and technology policy, re-established
by President Trump under **Executive Order 14177** with its first members appointed in
**March 2026**. Its distinguishing feature relative to prior PCAST incarnations is
composition: the seat count is capped at 24, and the roster is dominated by frontier-AI
and semiconductor principals rather than academic scientists.

## Mandate and structure

- **Co-chairs**: David Sacks (former Special Advisor for AI & Crypto) and Michael
  Kratsios (Assistant to the President and Director of the Office of Science and
  Technology Policy). Both roles mean PCAST is chaired by the serving OSTP director plus
  the administration's most senior former AI policy hand — i.e. it sits inside the
  Executive Office rather than at arm's length.
- **Size**: up to **24 members**, appointed by the President, **serving without
  compensation**.
- **Stated remit**: "strengthening American leadership in science and technology," with
  the site naming **superintelligence (SI)**, quantum, nuclear and biotechnology as the
  emerging technologies members are expected to advise on. The framing language is "the
  New Golden Age of Science and Innovation."
- **Historical precedent claimed**: the page traces the lineage to Franklin D.
  Roosevelt's 1933 Science Advisory Board, asserting that "each President has established
  his own advisory committee of scientists, engineers, and industry leaders."

## Why this matters for AI governance

The composition is the story. A council that advises on **superintelligence** is staffed
almost entirely by the operators of the frontier-AI and semiconductor buildout — NVIDIA's
[[entities/nvidia|CEO of record until five days ago]], AMD's CEO, Meta's CEO, Google's
co-founder, Oracle's founder, the leading frontier-AI VC, and a supplier to the datacenter
supply chain — plus the Attorney General. There is no comparable representation from
academic AI safety, civil society, or labour.

Consequences worth tracking:

1. **Capture risk is structural, not incidental.** Prior US frontier-AI advisory efforts
   (the NIST AI Safety Institute lineage, the [[concepts/ai-regulation-2026|state-level
   safety-act wave]], the [[concepts/ai-governance-political-pressure|frontier model
   monitoring]] debate) were contested on whether industry had too much access. PCAST
   resolves that question by making industry the majority of the body by design.
2. **A "superintelligence" brief held by commercial principals.** The remit explicitly
   includes SI, which means the body defining the government's frame for the
   [[concepts/superintelligence|superintelligence]] question is populated by people whose
   firms are racing toward it. Expect the advice to be capability-forward and
   competition-forward ([[concepts/us-china-ai-competition]]), not risk-forward.
3. **Serving-member conflict during the NVIDIA transition.** Jensen Huang was published
   as a member while serving as NVIDIA CEO; he resigned as CEO on November 12, 2026.
   Whether and how the seat is handled under an interim CEO is an open governance question
   with no precedent in the PCAST record.
4. **The Attorney General on a science council.** Bondi's presence signals PCAST is being
   used for the enforcement-adjacent side of AI policy (export controls,
   [[concepts/ai-supply-chain-security|chip smuggling]], antitrust posture toward
   labs) rather than purely advisory science.

## Limitations of this page

The wiki currently holds only the White House OSTP landing page (member roster and
mandate text). **No PCAST report, meeting minutes, or published recommendation has been
captured** — the source page's "PCAST Meetings" and "PCAST Documents & Reports" sections
are empty navigation headings. Nothing here should be read as evidence of what PCAST has
actually advised; the page documents the *institution and its composition*, which is
itself the substantive fact.

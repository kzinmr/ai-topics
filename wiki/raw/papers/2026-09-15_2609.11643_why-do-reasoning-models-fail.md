# Why Do Reasoning Models Fail to Follow Instructions?

**arXiv:** 2609.11643  |  **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11643

**Authors:** Arun Chellapragada, Ketan Palawat, Prince Soni, Hemanth Velaga, Sreyan Manna, Sheldon Yu, Yuyang Zhang, Akash Srivastava, Diyi Wang, Shameem Puthiya Parambil

## Abstract

Despite their remarkable reasoning capabilities, Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often exhibit lower instruction-following performance compared to their non-reasoning counterparts. We systematically study this phenomenon across multiple instruction types and model families. Using a controlled synthetic-data framework, we identify three failure modes, including faithfulness drift (models answer a related but subtly different question) and reasoning that diverges from the final answer. We show that a model's faithfulness to its own chain-of-thought is a strong predictor of whether it follows the final instruction, and that instruction following can be improved with minimal supervised fine-tuning on small amounts of data.

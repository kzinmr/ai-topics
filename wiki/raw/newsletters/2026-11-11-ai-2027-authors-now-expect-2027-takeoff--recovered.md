AI 2027's Authors: The Intelligence Explosion Is Coming in 2027 After All The Takeaway 👉 The AI Futures Project , the team behind AI 2027, now expects a superhuman AI researcher in 2027 , unless the industry deliberately slows down. 👉 Until now, even its most bullish forecaster, Daniel Kokotajlo , put only even odds on one arriving before 2029 . 👉 The trigger: METR estimates Anthropic's progress has roughly doubled , mostly thanks to AI, which the team reads as a 1.7x AI-driven speedup. 👉 The group calls for an industry-wide pause , followed by an international agreement. The team behind AI 2027 spent much of last year pushing its forecasts back. Now it has pulled them forward, hard. In a post published Tuesday, the AI Futures Project says all of its forecasters now see a superhuman AI researcher , an AI good enough that a lab would rather run on it alone than on the world's best human researchers without AI, as likely to arrive some time in 2027 , assuming no deliberate slowdown. Until now, even its most bullish member, Daniel Kokotajlo , put only even odds on such a system arriving before 2029. The group also sees a 35% to 65% chance that AI able to do all white-collar work arrives before 2028. Each forecaster's median date for AI that beats top experts at virtually every task: mostly rising through 2025, then falling, with the sharpest drop this month. (AI Futures Project) The trigger is METR's report on Anthropic, which we covered on Monday. The independent evaluator estimated that Anthropic's AI progress is running at about twice its historical pace, and guessed that roughly 80% of that acceleration comes from AI doing the company's own research work. The forecasters read that as a 1.7x speedup caused by AI, a pace that even the most aggressive version of their model, updated less than three months ago, had given under a 4% chance of reaching by this November. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative, so that figure rests partly on trust. In the team's picture, AI works like a roughly fivefold stronger engine for research, and because new ideas keep getting harder to find, that becomes 1.7 times faster progress. (AI Futures Project) The authors are not celebrating. " Unfortunately, it seems like the intelligence explosion will be happening in 2027 after all, " Kokotajlo wrote on X. " I am scared, sad, and angry. " Their argument: labs are not on track to keep an AI researcher at that level reliably aligned with human goals, and letting a misaligned one build its own successors would put everyone in an extremely bad position. They call for an industry-wide pause , followed by an international agreement. Why it matters: If the forecasters are right, the point where AI does most of the work of building better AI is roughly a year away, which turns questions about control and regulation from long-term debates into near-term decisions. Every five years, Stanford's One Hundred Year Study on AI (AI100) asks a panel of experts to take stock of the field. This time the panel started by grading its predecessors. The third report came out on Tuesday, and its look back at 2021 is blunt: that panel suspected deep learning's dominance "may be coming to an end" , doubted AI could gain common sense without a body, and did not foresee a chat window reaching 100 million users within 14 months. The 2026 report, "AI Goes Mainstream: The Scaling Era and the Rise of Generative AI." (Stanford AI100 via TNW) The diagnosis: the 2021 experts, nearly all insiders, underestimated what happens when you keep scaling the same architecture. Since then, frontier models have reached or neared expert level on bar and medical exams, and AI has proved open problems in mathematics, alone or with humans. In 2026, the report notes, Claude Fable found a one-line counterexample that disproves the famous Jacobian conjecture in three or more dimensions. Now the panel makes its own big call: scaling is running into limits. Fresh high-quality data is scarce and frontier training runs cost too much, so it expects the next gains to come from smarter algorithms, longer reasoning, memory and agents rather than ever bigger models. It also warns that the data center boom has outpaced earlier investment booms while AI "is not yet paying its way" in economic data or company returns. Geoffrey Hinton and John Hopfield at the 2024 Nobel lectures; the report cites their Nobel as recognition of how fast AI is transforming science. (Stanford AI100) The report also turns that lesson on itself: when the field's leading insiders agree on what comes next, "they may be converging on the previous paradigm's limits, not on the next paradigm's trajectory."

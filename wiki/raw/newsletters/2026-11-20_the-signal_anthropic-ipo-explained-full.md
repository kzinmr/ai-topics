# Anthropic's IPO explained (for people who aren't investors)

*The Signal (Alex Banks), Nov 20, 2026. Captured via newsletter-wiki-ingest 2026-11-21.*

Alex BanksNov 20, 2026821ShareAnthropic is now a public company, trading on the Nasdaq as "ANT.”

It listed at $83 (above the $70 to $78 it pitched to investors) and it hit a peak price of $111.53 on the first day of trading, valuing the company at about $1.5 trillion (closer to $2 trillion if you count every share, including staff stock options).

But before it listed, it had to publish an S-1 (the document a company files with US regulators laying out its numbers and its risks), an update on 2 November, and a second amendment on 10 November.

Most recently, it pitched investors with a public 57-slide roadshow.

In the 2 November amendment, Anthropic said it "may imminently meet" a threshold in its Responsible Scaling Policy, the rulebook it uses to match model capability with safety measures.

The threshold is defined as "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI".

In layman’s terms, that means AI doing so much of the research and engineering that one year produces as much progress as two years normally would. This is called recursive self-improvement.

On 10 November, the wording changed to what Anthropic now says is “we have met this threshold”.

Aka, Anthropic just went from “we’re close” to “we’re already there” in a span of just eight days.

By the way, this is all based on what it expects from future models and its overall rate of progress, as opposed to what’s already been seen.

Relatedly, Claude wrote over 90% of the contributions to Anthropic's own codebase in September.

The faster AI does research, the less buffer time there is between a first yes and something getting launched.

At some point (maybe soon), the limit to growth will become "how quickly can a human say yes" to the AI's output.

As for Anthropic as a business, let's break down some of the numbers:

Revenue grew > 45x over the last year and a half, while compute costs grew 10x.

Gross margin > 85% so far this year, up from 29% a year earlier.

Net revenue retention of ~900% (a customer who spent $1 a year ago now spends $9).

Now Anthropic reported a loss of $80.7B in the first half of 2026 and $104B over nine months.

About 90% of that is an accounting charge, with no cash actually leaving the business.

It works a bit like this, convertible notes are kind of like IOUs that turn into shares later, and accounting rules say they have to be valued at what they're worth today. So every time Anthropic's valuation climbs, the notes become more valuable, and that increase gets booked as a loss.

In other words, Anthropic is being “penalised” on paper for becoming more valuable. That charge made up $72.4B of the first-half loss and $94.3B of the nine-month loss to 30 September 2026.

Anthropic's disclosed that it has $6.2B in outstanding convertible debt as of June. Almost all is held by Amazon, which valued it at about $146B in its latest quarterly filing.

It’s only when we take these charges out that the picture starts to change shape:

2026 first-half operating loss: $693m on $16.3B of revenue

Operating cash flow: positive $6.6B over nine months, against negative $2.0B a year earlier

The roadshow paints a rosier picture of Anthropic’s profitability, leading with a 12% adjusted operating margin...put another way, Anthropic makes 12 cents of profit for every dollar of revenue once it leaves out stock-based pay for staff and non-cash charitable donations. However, when you count everything under standard accounting rules (GAAP), the same nine months show an operating loss of $1.26B.

That figure already leaves out the paper charge on the convertible notes, so it shows what actually running the business costs, which turns out to be a long way from the $104B headline loss.

So the 12% makes Anthropic look healthier than its full accounts do, and I think the honest take is that it’s close to breaking even but not quite there yet. Growth is a different story entirely, because Anthropic is one of the fastest-growing companies of all time.

The only companies that can come close over a one-year period are the COVID-19 vaccine makers, fueled by an unprecedented global pandemic and economic shock. Over a two-year period, these companies can’t even keep up.

Nicola Giunchi, Is Anthropic the fastest-growing company in history?Revenue went from $386M in 2024 to $4.6B in 2025, then $33.2B in the first nine months of 2026.

The first half of 2026 was $16.3B against $1.16B a year earlier, about 14 times bigger. The third quarter alone brought in $17.0B.

Customers spending $100,000 or more a year went from about 1,500 at the end of 2025 to over 11,900.

More than half of the Fortune 500 spend over $100,000 on their AI budget/year, yet on average it still represents under 0.1% of their total expenses.

If a company spent $1,000, Anthropic would get under $1. Even if this increases by one order of magnitude to just 1%, Anthropic would earn 10x as much, so the implications are HUGE.

While I’m not a financial advisor, the monumental growth that Anthropic has experienced over the last two years is no small feat.

I think it’s a testament to how much businesses will pay for AI that is truly useful, and customers who start using Claude just keep spending more.

14x growth in a year is extremely difficult to repeat. Quarterly revenue rose about 144% and then about 47%, so the real question now is how much it slows moving forward.

The risk I'd watch is demand slowing before chips reach the end of their useful economic life.

So far, it seems to me that Anthropic has run an infra-light model, renting most of its computing power rather than owning it.

Yet compared to hyperscalers like SpaceX, Google, Meta and Microsoft, Anthropic's models are head and shoulders above the rest.

This is interesting because it doesn't own the entire stack, so it can't lean on economies of scale up and down it.

How they've managed to pull this off completely amazes me.

Yet this means its SpaceX deal is a massive risk factor.

Anthropic's filing says it could pay SpaceX up to $120.5 billion for computing power through 2029, about $3.1 billion a month once it's all live.

But after early 2027, either side can walk away on 90 days' notice.

The roadshow suggests Anthropic has already started to change this.

It now leases chips and runs them in data centres built just for it, which it calls "Anthropic Direct", and expects that to make up a growing share of its computing over the coming years.

Anthropic says it will spend what it raises in its IPO ($81.3 billion) on compute and infrastructure, meaning it has a lot of dry powder to deploy.

Especially considering they've only burned through ~$9 billion of the $141.1 billion they've raised, with about $132 billion still sitting in cash and short-term investments.

I'm excited to see how the next year plays out, as it's a critical moment as they expand from model + application provider to a full-stack AI company that runs more of its own computing, rather than renting it from others.

I use Claude every single day, it's my most-used knowledge work tool.

And like the thousands of businesses that already rely on Anthropic's intelligence, it seems as though every SaaS tool has rushed to proudly integrate them to keep their customers happy.

Yesterday, it became the biggest pure-play AI lab ever to list, going from what was a small offshoot of OpenAI just five years ago to setting the precedent for the rest of the market.

What is sonic branding?
Sonic branding is the use of sound, often in the form of a short audio cue, to make a brand instantly recognizable. Even without seeing the brand’s logo or marketing materials, sonic branding allows you to hear a noise and associate it with a specific company.

Yet, despite being able to reach customers even when they’re not looking, sonic branding is often treated as a lower priority than visual identity. For many companies, that means there’s a valuable opportunity to push further into sonic branding and establish your brand in the aural space.
What does sonic branding sound like?
Sonic branding is extremely effective, as it’s truly unavoidable. If the sound is playing anywhere in a room, then you’re going to hear it (and potentially even subconsciously think of the company you associate it with).
Here are some examples of sonic branding in action.
Apple
Jim Reekes designed the Mac startup chime for
Apple back in 1991
. Every Mac that started up from that date until 2016 began with the same sound, the short C major chord quickly becoming synonymous with the brand.
Intel
Intel’s five-note sonic imprint is instantly recognizable, forming part of television ads for the company since composer Walter Werzowa created it in 1994. The five notes aim to mirror the phrase ‘Intel Inside’ sonically.
Law & Order
While not a typical brand, the Law & Order scene-change sound has become synonymous with the show. The New York Times even named it the most
successful sound in television history
back in 2017. The sound was created by Mike Post out of dozens of layered audio clips, including jail cell doors slamming and a crowd of people stamping on the floor. These two notes have become iconic to the series and establish a consistent aural tone across scenes and even different versions of the show.
All of these examples demonstrate how a short sound clip can transcend its own composition and come to represent something much larger.
Sonic branding vs. audio branding
Sonic branding and audio branding overlap but don’t describe the same thing. Audio branding is an umbrella term that encompasses every single decision a company makes related to audio. That could mean in-app notification sounds or even the voice you use on
AI customer service calls
.
Sonic branding is more specific, typically only extending to the strategic choice of sounds built around a core logo. Many of the most famous examples are short and wordless, allowing them to be played in conjunction whenever brands show their logo.

Why sonic identity matters for brand recognition
Sound reaches a user even when they’re not actively looking at the screen where the sound is coming from. Especially as individuals continue to
engage more frequently with audio content
, having a clear sonic identity helps set your brand apart from others.
Veritonic published research that suggests
77% of consumers
can recall a brand more easily when they associate it with a certain sound. Similar
studies conducted by Audacy
revealed that sonic branding increased ad recall in podcasts by 14% and in radio ads by 17%.
Clear sonic branding is also a trust signal. Brands that have an established sound are 7% more trustworthy and 6% more likable, according to Audacy.
At scale, these percentages can make an enormous difference in how the world perceives your brand. And, if you’re able to create a sound that becomes recognizable, it can push your global branding even further, creating a new surface for your brand to interact with customers and stick in their minds.

Frequently asked questions
What does sonic branding mean?
Sonic branding is deliberately using sound to make your company recognizable. It often revolves around a short sonic logo, which is a cue you deploy across different channels that becomes associated with your business. Sonic branding has the same end goal as overall branding, which is to make your company instantly recognizable.
What are some top examples of sonic branding?
Netflix’s three-second noise when logging into the platform and starting a stream, Apple’s old start-up Mac sound, Intel’s five-note branding clip, and Law and Order’s scene-change noise are all top examples of sonic branding. They’re recognized by millions around the world and make you instantly think of the brand behind the sound.
How is voice part of sonic branding?
The voice you decide to use in ads or in your AI agents is a direct representation of your brand. The voice you select will influence what your customers automatically think about your company. Make sure to match the voice of your brand to wider brand guidelines.

# Book Review: Bobiverse Books 1-3 by Dennis E. Taylor

Source: https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2026/09/book-review-bobiverse-books-1-3-by-dennis-e-taylor/

Like sticky popcorn, this is a moreish but ultimately unsatisfying set of stories. After I flamed out of
Dungeon Crawler Carl
, someone suggested I try this series. I can see why! It's campy sci-fi fun, with enough tropes to keep you chuckling and enough tension to keep you turning the pages.
Bob's brain is uploaded to a computer post-mortem. Awoken in a dystopian future, he has to pilot a spaceship to strange new worlds, put right what once went wrong, and hoping The Force will be with him, always. Oh, and he repeatedly clones himself. Creating a universe populated with Bobs (a "Bobiverse" if you will)
It's your standard "only I, a slightly geeky guy with lots of pop-culture knowledge, can save the world" fare which is beloved by slightly geeky guys everywhere who think they're smarter than all those normies just because they can recite the list of Hugo winners alphabetically.
Like
Ready Player One
it throws in as many quips and catchphrases as the plot will bear. Unlike Cline's work, it never really commits to them, so you end up with a scattering of Monty Python, Star Wars, and the X-Files without an overall theme developing.
And, in keeping with Andy Weir's The Martian, it's all just one guy sciencing the shit out of the problem. Except that lots of the science is sort of hand-waved away with "and then I 3D printed a thing".
Similar to both those books is an almost total lack of female characters. The ones that are in the first two are either plot-points or harridans. By the third there's a love interest who is
slightly
more rounded, and a couple of other incidentals, but offset against yet another woman who just can't appreciate the "genius" of Bob.
It's all good page turning fun other than the fact that Bob is a
total
cretin. He begins a slow descent into fascist dictator and barely even comments on it. He spies, carries out extra-judicial killings, and meddles in politics to his own advantage. At no point does the text ever really engage with the fact that
Bob is a monster
.
The character rarely reflects on whether his behaviour meets the moral standard he expects of others. He bemoans the aliens who are destroying entire ecosystems while simultaneously wiping out whole species himself. At times he is a conniving bully and reacts badly to anyone who pushes back against the ineffable will of Bob.
The jumping back-and-forth between the different Bobs is a bit frustrating, a bit like flicking between TV channels. I wish each story strand were allowed some space to develop - but instead it's one chapter of this planet, then one chapter of another, before (eventually) circling back.
Annoyingly,
the books are only available on Amazon Kindle
. They're not on any other platform or library. I'm grateful to the friend who lent me their copies. I binged the first three but, without any indication that Bob will mature as a character or face a reckoning for his egregious actions, that's where I stopped.

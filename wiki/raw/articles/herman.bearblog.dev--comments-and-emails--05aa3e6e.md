# Comments and emails

Source: https://herman.bearblog.dev/comments-and-emails/

Comments and emails
24 Nov, 2026
I'm regularly asked why Bear doesn't have built-in comments, guestbooks, or contact forms. They're all trivial to build (a text box and a send button), and I could add them in an afternoon. But they're not included by design.
The problem with all three is what they let in. A comment box or contact form accepts anything from anyone. Spam bots find them quickly, so whoever runs the blog ends up moderating a queue. And the people who comment don't have to identify themselves as most of these forms ask for a name and email address and check neither.
This week a blogger on Bear received a particularly nasty message via an embedded, third-party contact form on their blog, the sender could type anything into the email boxes and press send. It was signed with a fake name, and used the public email address of another blogger, essentially impersonating someone else to stir up trouble. What arrived was, in effect, a shitty, anonymous note.
Anonymous notes are the worst kind to receive. There's nobody to reply to, nothing to block, and the perpetrator doesn't have a social incentive to behave.
This is why Bear's solution to all three is a link with your email address in it (and if you're worried about scrapers, use a dedicated alias or separate address). Your email provider already does the heavy lifting on spam filtering, and a message generally has to come from a real account you can reply to or block. A contact form would have to recreate that verification with confirmation links, and most don't bother.
Naturally, people can still get nasty emails from throwaway accounts, but the sender still has to set up that account or alias first, which adds just enough friction to discourage it. I suspect most nastiness online is impulsive, and the friction of email is just enough to let that impulse pass.
I wrote
recently
that good systems are ones that well-behaved people barely notice. Email works the same way. A reader who wants to say they enjoyed your post already has an address, and is happy for you to see it. Someone wanting to stay completely anonymous to send a shitty email has to go out of their way to set it up.
If you'd like readers to be able to reach you, the docs explain how to set up a
reply via email link
and a
guestbook
that runs on email.

# iPhone Duo for Creative Pros

Source: https://www.austinmann.com/trek/iphone-duo

A week with the iPhone Duo at home, camping and in Iceland. A capable camera with new tricks, and a creative powerhouse for planning, editing and sharing.
Hi from Iceland!
In the last week, I’ve had a lot of fun getting to know the iPhone Duo at home, camping with family and yesterday I arrived in Iceland to guide some friends on a private tour.
Honestly, the idea of a folding iPhone didn’t immediately appeal to me. I usually like smaller devices, but when I first started playing with the Duo in September, I realized it’s really something special and I couldn’t wait to get my hands on a review unit from Apple. The iPhone 18 Pro remains the most powerful camera in the lineup, and though the Duo also has an excellent camera, where it really shines is everything around the photograph: planning, editing and sharing.
I always approach these projects with one question, and though the camera isn’t the whole story here, it’s still my question:
HOW DOES THIS NEW TECH MAKE MY PHOTOS AND VIDEOS BETTER?
As I’ve looked to answer this question, I haven’t been able to go all-in on a dedicated shoot like I usually do for my camera reviews, but I learned a lot just having the Duo with me as I work, shoot and travel.

No tripod needed
You never know when the aurora might show itself and though I
almost
always have my Ultrapod + Glif tripod with me, sometimes it’s back in the room when needed! Last night in the parking lot of our hotel in southern Iceland, the aurora started dancing in the sky. I partially opened the iPhone Duo, set it on top of the car and used Night mode and was able to capture the aurora overhead without ever fiddling with a tripod. What a moment in iPhone photography history!
The iPhone camera team is always looking for ways to eliminate the need for additional hardware (integrating lenses, building Action mode so no gimbal is needed, etc) and this is a BIG one. The iPhone Duo can now shoot long exposures in Night mode without a tripod!
Smart Take
I’ve found Smart Take works surprisingly well. Apple says “it follows the scene in real time and automatically snaps the photo when everyone is posed and ready,” and it does.
We went camping this weekend and while Olivia and I were playing on a merry-go-round at the campground I decided to try it out. I partially opened the Duo, set it on the ground in laptop-style mode (no tripod needed), set it to Smart Take and sat down with Olivia. The Duo waited til we were both looking at the camera (which of course took a second for Olivia) and then snapped automatically.

The nano-texture display
As you may know, I've always been a fan of matte, nano-texture displays… even at the cost of a little vibrance, I prefer no reflections.
The inner display on the Duo looks lovely and feels great to the touch. It works well in bright sun, and I imagine it will feel paper-like with Apple Pencil.
The crease is visible from an angle if you look for it, but I didn’t notice it in daily use.

Shooting with Duo
The Duo’s hardware opens up a handful of new shooting positions.
I’ve found folded to be best: one hand, discreet, fastest from pocket to shot. You can also shoot unfolded, which looks gorgeous on the big display, but I find the camera harder to operate that way and it really draws attention to you.
The all-new trick is partially folded, which feels a bit like shooting top down with an old Rolleiflex. You can shoot outward while looking down or up at the device, which is great for staying discreet and for really low and high angles like the image below. I found myself drawn to this shooting style as it feels really different than most iPhone photography.

Kid Cue and Duo Preview
The native Camera app has two new features that use the outer display while shooting. Open the Duo, open the Camera app on the inner display and tap the new icon in the top right. Kid Cue plays Snoopy to pull a kid’s attention to the camera. Duo Preview shows a live preview on the outer display to whoever is in front of your camera.

A few thoughts for serious creative pros
Pro retouchers will love using Apple Pencil on the Duo for masking and selections, especially on the paper-like nano-texture screen.
Both displays use the new Color Matching Function (CMF) technology released with 18 Pro, which means better color accuracy and consistency for anyone using multiple devices in their creative workflow.
ProRAW is not supported in the native Camera app on iPhone Duo, and it’s unclear to me why. I rarely use ProRAW because I prefer the power and speed of Photographic Styles, but I do use it in low light to control noise reduction. To shoot aurora like the image above, I’d normally be in ProRAW.
For now you need a third-party app to shoot RAW on the Duo. Apple recommends Afterlight, Moment Pro Camera II, Halide Pro Camera III and Not Boring Camera, which all support it.
I migrated all my settings and apps from my 18 Pro and every camera setting came over except one: Preserve Settings > Resolution. I set the Duo to 48MP and it kept switching back to 24MP until I revisited Settings > Preserve Settings and realized that setting hadn’t transferred from my iPhone 18 Pro because on the Duo it has a slightly different title.
The Duo hasn’t been my main daily device, but I’ve pushed it hard shooting and editing photos, planning my trip, outlining ideas and watching movies, and the battery has been consistently better than I expected.
I haven’t been able to test this yet but I’m looking forward to having the Duo in my pocket to use it as a tether monitor connected to my Hasselblad/Sony etc.
Creative apps on iPhone Duo
Esther has played piano her whole life and we’ve been wanting to get Olivia started… that serendipitously happened on a Saturday morning when I downloaded the new PianoFold app for iPhone Duo and set it in front of Olivia, who is almost 4.
She picked it up immediately and I eventually had to take it away, she was having so much fun.
Not Boring Camera
emulates an analog camera and offers a bunch of filmic looks.
Procreate Pocket
is fully optimized for iPhone Duo and, with Apple Pencil support, will be a popular on-the-go sketchbook for hand-drawn artists.
Tide Guide
is a beautiful, simple app for planning around the tide.
These are just a few of the earliest apps designed for Duo, but I absolutely can't wait to see the creative ideas developers come up with to really leverage this completely new hardware configuration.
A few wishes
I’d like the native Camera app to be able to use the outer display as a flash, the way Photo Booth flashes the whole screen. It could be a big, soft light source, especially nice for portraits.
ProRAW in the native Camera app! It’d be super useful for aurora and other RAW use cases.
Smart Take across the rest of the iPhone lineup. It’s a compelling feature for capturing the perfect moment and it’d be super popular.

The Bottom Line
The iPhone Duo offers a great camera, more than enough for most users and for me in most situations. Creative pros will miss the longer lens, but gain a big, beautiful display, the freedom to shoot without a tripod, and entirely new ways to capture, edit and share images.
The Duo represents perhaps the most exciting innovation in iPhone hardware since, dare I say, the original iPhone. It’s a joy to shoot with and an even more powerful companion throughout my creative workflow, from planning shoots with
Sunlitt
to editing in
Lightroom
and outlining in
MindNode
. It’s earned a place in my core kit, and I’m genuinely excited to see where this new chapter of the iPhone takes us.

United Airlines bans AI agents from booking flights on behalf of travelers (CBS News, Wednesday Nov 11, 2026).

"United Airlines said on Wednesday that it is banning artificial intelligence agents from making flight reservations on behalf of travelers, citing security risks and the possibility that such actions could violate federal rules. Many consumers are turning to AI agents such as Meta's Muse and OpenAI's dots to complete tasks ranging from researching products and tracking down deals to making purchases."

## United's stated reasons

- "sharing account credentials with personal, third-party, AI assistants can expose sensitive information, and actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles."
- Using an AI agent to, e.g., check in for a flight "could violate federal regulations, including rules requiring travelers to verify in person that their luggage does not contain banned substances."
- "United is taking steps to restrict access — directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators — to prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants," updating booking and ticketing policies.

## Analyst read: intermediary control + search costs

- Henry Harteveldt (Atmosphere Research Group): the policy suggests United wants to retain control over the customer booking process. "United is saying they don't want to add yet another, and potentially even more powerful, intermediary than they already face in terms of Google search, price-comparison metasearch sites and online travel agencies. Flight searches cost airlines money, and undoubtedly United wants to avoid the expense associated with the surge of AI bot flight searches."
- Southwest took the opposite tack: made its flight inventory viewable to ChatGPT users.
- United fares remain bookable through aggregators (Kayak, Hopper, Google Flights); "consumers shouldn't find it any more difficult to find good fares... But by blocking the AI bots, United won't make it any easier to find its fares, either" (Harteveldt).

Is Harvey Worth it for a Small Law Firm?
by
Harvey Team
•
Nov 24, 2026
Yes, for a small law firm that regularly handles document-heavy work. Harvey can reduce the time lawyers spend preparing a first pass on litigation records, previous deals, and unfamiliar research. The buying decision comes down to time saved after lawyer review, weighed against what the firm pays.
At a lean firm, the partner preparing for a hearing may also be sorting through the records. A transactional lawyer answering one client question may need to find a clause in years of previous deals.
Harvey for law firms
helps lawyers analyze those materials, while counsel still checks the sources and makes the final call.
Small Firms Get the Most From Harvey on Document-Heavy Work
Litigation and transactional teams work with different records, but both spend lawyer time finding and organizing information before they can advise a client. The best first use case starts with one of those repeatable tasks.
Litigation Teams Need a Reliable First Pass on the Record
Before a litigation lawyer can build an argument, someone has to read the production, check dates, and reconcile financial records. In a small practice, that initial review often falls to the lawyer preparing the case.
With
Harvey Vault
, a litigation team can review groups of documents, organize extracted information in Review Tables, and check findings against the underlying files. Counsel still needs to decide which facts matter and whether the evidence supports the position it plans to take.
At
B. Cremades y Asociados
, a Spanish arbitration and litigation boutique, lawyers used Harvey to analyze thousands of invoices in a construction arbitration. Harvey helped organize currency conversions and interest calculations into a chart in minutes, work the firm said previously took hours by hand.
Transactional Lawyers Need Answers From Prior Deals
A client asks whether a protection appeared in an earlier acquisition. The answer may be buried in an old agreement, a later amendment, or a folder of negotiation documents. Finding the precedent is only the first step. The lawyer still has to decide whether it fits the current deal.
At
Giliberti Triscornia
, an M&A firm with more than 60 professionals, a partner used Harvey to review 15 takeover-bid documents for a specific provision. Harvey returned a chart in minutes, instead of the full day a manual scan would have taken, with each answer linked to its passage. The partner could see whether a structure under consideration had been tested in earlier bids.
A smaller transactional practice runs the same check when it compares indemnities across earlier deals or tests an incoming draft against its preferred language. Counsel still decides whether the precedent fits the current transaction.
Legal Research Needs Sources Counsel Can Check
A generalist at a smaller firm may need to research an unfamiliar issue without a specialist down the hall. Harvey’s
knowledge sources
support legal, regulatory, and tax research using authorities counsel can inspect.
Lawyers still need to open the cited authority, confirm the jurisdiction, and check the reasoning. A sensible test is a question the firm already knows how to answer, with a review of the sources available under its proposed agreement.
Spaces Keep a Matter’s Context Together as New Documents Arrive
Matters change after the first review. New files arrive, colleagues join, and earlier decisions shape the next task. Having to reconstruct that context each time can eat into any time gained.
In
Harvey Spaces
, lawyers keep a matter’s documents, work product, and history together. Harvey Agents open inside a Space with that context, subject to the matter’s permissions.
When new documents arrive,
Review Table Sync
flags which connected Review Tables are out of date. Lawyers can add the new files in one click without redoing the existing analysis, and they still check the new findings against the sources.
That continuity matters most to firms that run similar matters regularly. On a one-off assignment, the quality of the first answer may count for more than keeping context together.
What Onboarding Looks Like for a Smaller Firm
A small firm has to introduce new software while client work continues. Even if the initial use case is clear, someone needs to decide which lawyers will test it, what materials they can use, and how the firm will review the results.
Harvey supports that first use case through a Customer Success Manager, Legal Engineers with law-practice experience, and Harvey Academy training. Legal Engineers can help shape the test around the firm's own matters, rather than starting with a generic rollout.
Harvey works
inside Word and Outlook
and can sync matter folders from iManage, SharePoint, and Google Drive. Lawyers can start from files the firm already keeps. The firm can decide which connections to use and who will manage permissions as it sets up its first test.
Start with one recurring task or matter type. Ask the lawyers who know it best to judge the output, the source checking, and the time involved. A useful test needs to hold up under real deadlines.
What Makes Harvey Worth the Investment?
A fast first draft isn’t much use if a lawyer spends the saved time correcting it. The measure is the complete task, from gathering the files to a result that has passed lawyer review.
Take work the firm completes regularly, establish how long it ordinarily takes, and then repeat it with Harvey. Include document preparation, prompting, source checks, revisions, and final lawyer review. Account for onboarding and training when considering the initial investment.
A useful assessment should answer four questions:
Does the complete task take less lawyer time after verification?
Can the lawyer trace important findings to the original document or legal source?
Does the result remain useful across comparable matters, or was the first test unusually favorable?
What does the time returned allow the firm to do that it could not do before?
For a managing partner, time saved is only part of the answer. On fixed-fee matters, lower delivery time may improve margin. On hourly work, fewer billed hours don’t automatically increase revenue.
The gain may be faster client responses, fewer write-offs, or room for an additional matter. In a recent
discussion among smaller firms
, one lawyer said AI is reallocating where the firm’s time goes. It also makes it practical to take on questions the team might previously have set aside.
Harvey’s price depends on seats, selected products, and contract terms, so ask for a quote before the evaluation starts. Compare that quote with the time and capacity the firm can realistically recover.

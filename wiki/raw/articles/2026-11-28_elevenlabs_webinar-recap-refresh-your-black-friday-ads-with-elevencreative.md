You put a lot into your Black Friday creative, and you want it to work as hard as possible. The question is how long it lasts. Ad platforms like Meta and Google now rely on the creative itself to find buyers, so the number of distinct ads a team can ship limits how much it can spend profitably.
In a
live workshop
on refreshing ads for Black Friday and Cyber Monday, Gabe Michael, forward deployed creative at ElevenLabs, and Jan Emmanuele, deployment strategist at ElevenLabs, walked through why creative production is the bottleneck in peak season and what an always-on alternative looks like. This post covers what they shared and how to apply it between now and January.
Key takeaways
Creative is the targeting.
With broad targeting, platforms use your ads to find your audience, and near-identical ads count as one idea. Variety drives performance.
Volume finds winners.
Winning ads are rare and hard to predict, so the teams that test the most creative find them most often.
Peak season punishes repetition.
Spend concentrates into a few days, frequency climbs, and response drops with every repeat view.
Your best ad is your most valuable asset.
Extend it with new hooks, voices, music, and languages before you build something new.
The handoff is the bottleneck.
A workflow built on sequential handoffs can't refresh weekly. An engine that reads performance and generates variants can.
Creative became the constraint
Targeting on ad platforms has become much broader. Instead of narrow audience settings doing the work, the platforms read the ad and decide who should see it. Ads that look and sound nearly the same get treated as a single idea, so 10 slight tweaks of one concept buy you very little new reach or insight.
Winning ads are rare and hard to predict. A team shipping a few new ads each week waits a long time between winners, while a team shipping many more finds them far sooner. That's why the strongest brands test far more creative than everyone else, and why the winner is often an ad nobody on the team would have picked.
Why ads wear out faster in peak season
Peak season compresses all of this. A large share of November and December online spend lands in the five days of Cyber Week, and every brand is bidding for the same shoppers in that window. Your top ads will get seen, but they'll get seen by the same people over and over. With each repeat view, someone who hasn't already converted is less likely to act.
The weeks after launch are harder. Teams have to spot which ads are wearing out, replace them before results drop, and carry what worked into December and January.
Localization is an underused lever
People respond better to ads in their own language. But many teams still run in only one language because each new language used to mean a new production cycle with its own budget and turnaround.
As a result, brands either run their original-language ads globally and accept lower conversion rates in non-native markets, or they localize a handful of priority markets and ignore the long tail. Taking a winning ad into a new market is often cheaper than finding a new winner there.
From production line to always-on engine
The traditional creative model runs in sequence: brand identity, creative strategy, production, distribution, then analysis at the end. Each step hands off to the next, and each handoff costs time many teams don't have during peak season. Adding AI usually replaces the production step with prompting, which speeds up one stage but keeps every handoff in place.

Why it matters:
Video is where most ad spend goes, and it's the hardest format to refresh because every change used to mean a reshoot or a re-edit. Changing the hook lets a team test the variant as a new idea, which stretches the life of a concept that already works.
A refresh checklist for the rest of the season
Rank live ads by spend and refresh the top ones first. These wear out the fastest.
Keep the product shot, structure, and brand kit. Change the hook, voice, offer, music, and language.
Make each variant different enough that the platform counts it as a new ad.
Ship in sets of three to five new creatives.
Refresh weekly through January. Swap when frequency climbs or results drop, and treat two weeks as the outer limit.
Take the winning ad into the next moment: Cyber Monday first, then the December holidays.
Have a December and January set ready now. The season runs well past Black Friday.
Two mistakes to avoid
The first is giving the AI too little to work with. The team still has to define the goal of the ad and who it's for. With that in place, the tools multiply what the team can ship.
The second is taking too little risk. Once the brand foundation is set, a long chain of briefs and approvals only slows the team down. Launch more variants, see what wins, then put budget behind the winners.
The common thread is that a team's time should go into the original idea and the decisions about it.

# The Silicon Underground

Source: https://dfarq.homeip.net/is-ai-crashing/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=is-ai-crashing

It’s November 8, 2026. I don’t know if AI is crashing. I don’t know if the AI bubble has burst. Not yet. Some people say it is. I remember the dotcom crash but didn’t blog about it, regrettably. Writing down my thoughts now will look foolish if nothing happens. But if something does happen, I’ll wish I had written something down to look back on.
What past stock market crashes felt like
It’s November 8, 2027. I don’t know if the AI bubble has burst. But it feels like it’s getting close.
I think stock market crashes are like thunderstorms in the midwest.
Sometimes stock market crashes sneak up on you. The 1987 crash seems that way. I was pretty young when it happened, but it seemed it surprised my dad. It was like that storm that comes in in a hurry. One minute it’s sunny and peaceful, then the sky darkens very suddenly, day turns to night, and violent thunder shakes the building.
That felt like 1987. The dotcom crash didn’t feel like that.
In retrospect, the
dotcom crash
was something we could have seen coming. Companies that had no profitability and no plan to get profitable were trading at valuations comparable to
Microsoft circa 1986
. Eventually a reckoning was inevitable. But it didn’t happen all at once. That crash was like a gloomy day where it’s overcast and windy and it looks like it could rain at any minute. But when it finally did start to rain there wasn’t much indication from the ground that looked any different from an hour before.
Today we blame a banking crisis in Japan for starting the dotcom crash. There was nothing special about Japan, the dotcom crash was just waiting for a catalyst. It could have been any sufficiently large economy.
And I’ve said before I see
parallels today with the dotcom era
.
By definition, a crash is a decline of over 10% in a stock market index over a period of a few days.
The AI crash in China
Arguably, the AI bubble burst in China on October 26, 2026. That day, China’s STAR50 index did experience a crash, and it lost $480 billion in value. We can think of STAR50 as China’s equivalent to NASDAQ. It’s not all AI companies, but it’s very tech heavy, and problems with AI companies will spill over into other tech companies. The scary thing about China’s wipeout is that even after that, Chinese stocks still looked overpriced compared to NASDAQ. So there’s still a lot of money left to lose in the STAR50 index.
If things do get worse and we do get a bona fide stock market crash on the NASDAQ, I think it’s very possible October 26, 2026 will go down in history as the day the AI bubble started to burst.
US Tech stocks
In late October, the money supply for AI companies tightened, raising the cost of borrowing. This caused some unease, especially because the timing coincided so closely with what was happening in China.
During the first week of November, US tech stocks had a generally bad week. On November 7, 2026, Yahoo Finance reported that tech stocks were cheaper than the S&P 500 for the first time since 2017. And I will tell you that a friend asked me on November 5 if the AI crash was happening. I said it depends what happens on the 6th.
Markets were up on the 6th. There was better-than-expected news about the US jobs market that day, and I think that caused enough investors to decide to buy the dip to push the market slightly up that day.
Several other things happened around the same time. A very highly publicized security vulnerability, PHP2Shell, was disclosed October 21, 2026. The scare around that pushed up the value of computer security stocks. I’m not sure if that was enough to affect the broader market or not, but it seems plausible.
Also, a lot of companies announced earnings on or around October 31. Good earnings pushed some stocks higher. But also, publicly traded companies often have trading windows for their employees, to reduce the possibility of insider trading. The trading window opens soon after the earnings calls. Tech employees selling their stock likely pushed down values somewhat.
Last, the United States had an election November 3. The markets rallied a bit after those results came in.
It was a volatile week with a lot of back-and-forth. It’s very possible a couple of bad days in the coming week could send the NASDAQ into crash territory. Since the NASDAQ 100 lost around 6.6% of its value that week, it fell short of a crash. But a couple more rocky days could send it into crash territory.
Stormy weather ahead
The weather looked stormy where I live much of that week. It didn’t rain as much as it could have. But it looked like it was just a matter of time. And the stock market was giving off a lot of the same kind of vibes.
I avoid investing directly in the tech sector. Because I work in the field, I have a hard time remaining completely objective about it, especially in the heat of the moment. I invest in broader index funds because they’re less volatile.
A few months ago, I moved a good chunk of money into XLP, a mutual fund that invests in companies that sell or produce consumer staples. It’s not at all glamorous, but it’s a generally safe place to park money during a crisis. Even when they can’t buy anything else, people continue to buy bread and toothpaste and soap. It pays dividends, so you’ll at least get dividend income, and recovers faster than glitzier growth stocks do. That fund is now trading near its 52-month high, so I think that means I’m not the only one who thinks things are looking stormy.

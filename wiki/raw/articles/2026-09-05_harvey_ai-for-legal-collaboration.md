How AI Supports Legal Collaboration in Shared Workspaces
AI for legal collaboration brings in-house teams and outside counsel into one shared workspace to work from the same matter context in real time.
by
Harvey Team
•
Sep 4, 2026
Legal work has always been collaborative, but that collaboration now stretches across more people, systems, and organizations than ever. Matters today are more complex and often require expertise that extends far beyond the lawyer leading the work. You may need to work with people across different offices, time zones, practice areas, and organizations.
The challenge isn’t just bringing the right people together, but keeping everyone aligned, especially when the information is spread across people, documents, conversations, and systems. You can lose important context between handoffs, miss an update, or find that someone is working with outdated facts. This can cost you time, affect work quality, and ultimately jeopardize the trust you’ve built with clients and colleagues.
AI for legal collaboration can help you close these gaps by keeping people, context, and work connected as a matter moves forward.
Why Traditional Legal Collaboration Creates a Coordination Problem
Traditional legal collaboration has historically relied on emails, calls, document exchanges, and sequential review cycles. These methods move work between people, but don’t give the team a shared environment to collaborate. You have to coordinate each handoff, keep everyone updated, and make sure no information is left out. This makes collaboration dependent on constant coordination rather than true integration.
Law Firm-Client Collaboration and Relationships
Law firms work closely with clients throughout a matter, often relying on their input and business context to make decisions. You may need a client to review a draft, answer a question, provide new information, or weigh in as the legal strategy develops.
When those interactions happen through separate exchanges, it takes more effort to keep the firm and client aligned. This can slow progress and make it harder to
build stronger law firm-client partnerships
over time.
The collaboration between
Deutsche Telekom and Gleiss Lutz
shows this pattern. The firm and its client spent years working side by side on complex matters through emails, markups, and version control. They are now moving that work into a shared space in Harvey where both build on the same materials.
In-House Collaboration With Internal Partners and Outside Counsel
In-house teams have to manage collaboration in multiple directions at once. Business leaders may need practical guidance on a developing issue, while outside counsel needs the right commercial context to provide useful advice. You’re often responsible for connecting those conversations and making sure decisions made on one side are understood on the other.
Traditional approaches split that work into separate conversations and handoffs, making it hard for internal stakeholders and outside counsel to stay connected to the same context as the matter progresses. On complex, cross-border matters, that coordination becomes critical quickly, since the work involves multiple internal teams and external advisors across different geographies and time zones.
How Legal AI Tools Enhance Internal and External Collaboration
For legal teams weighing how AI can support collaboration, the value goes beyond individual task support. AI for legal collaboration can help legal teams maintain continuity as work moves between people. With the right legal AI platform, team members can build on existing analysis without piecing together what happened before or starting from scratch at every handoff.
The same approach can strengthen law firm-client collaboration and the way in-house teams work with internal stakeholders and outside counsel. AI-powered legal collaboration can connect everyone working on the same matter, creating a stronger foundation for collaboration across organizations.
Acting as a Real-Time Shared Workspace
Shared AI workspaces give authorized collaborators real-time access to the same matter information. Legal teams can work alongside colleagues, clients, and outside counsel through the same platform. As the matter develops, each party can review the latest work, contribute where needed, and see updates as they happen. This keeps everyone aligned and allows collaboration to continue without waiting for separate updates or review cycles.
Facilitating Faster Feedback Loops
Reviews often slow down because feedback happens sequentially: one person comments, another responds, and the work moves through another round of revisions. AI-powered collaboration can make the process more continuous by enabling the right people to review and contribute as the work develops. You can address questions and incorporate feedback without repeatedly collecting comments from different places or rebuilding context for each reviewer. This shortens review cycles and speeds decision-making.
Leveraging Repeatable Workflows
Legal teams often solve the same types of problems repeatedly, but the knowledge behind that work does not always carry forward. AI for legal collaboration can convert successful approaches to document review, legal research, initial drafting, and other recurring legal work into repeatable workflows that teams can reuse. This helps legal professionals apply the same instructions, standards, and institutional knowledge across matters rather than relying on individual memory. Law firms can also extend proven workflows to clients, creating a more consistent way for both sides to handle recurring legal needs.
Keeping Analysis Connected to Underlying Sources
Legal analysis is easier to collaborate on when another lawyer can see not only the conclusion, but the authority and evidence behind it. A legal AI solution can keep analysis connected to the documents, legal research, and case law that support it, giving reviewers a direct path back to the source material. Your colleagues can also verify the reasoning or take the analysis further without repeating work that’s already been done. That traceability makes substantive review more efficient and helps teams reach well-supported legal decisions faster.
Enabling Leaner Teams to Manage Relationships
Lean legal teams do not always have dedicated resources to manage every update, handoff, and follow-up across their relationships. AI for legal collaboration can absorb some of that coordination by preserving matter context and making it easier for the right people to access and understand ongoing work. For example, a small in-house team can bring internal stakeholders and outside counsel into a shared AI workspace, where they can access relevant documents, analysis, and matter context without relying on the legal team for constant updates.
Giving Leaders Visibility Without Micromanaging
Legal leaders need enough visibility to identify risks and give direction, but traditional status reporting doesn’t always provide a clear picture of how the work is progressing. In a shared AI workspace, leaders can view the work, its supporting context, and its progress without asking the team to prepare another update. They can step in when a decision requires senior judgment and stay out of the way when it doesn’t.
Why AI-Enabled Collaboration Matters Most for Lean Legal Teams
When you work on a lean legal team, there are fewer people to share the workload. You may be doing substantive legal work while also coordinating with clients, internal stakeholders, and outside counsel. As matters become more complex and client expectations grow, that coordination can take up a significant part of your time.
AI for legal collaboration becomes especially valuable when every person on your team already has a full workload. It can reduce the coordination needed to keep matters moving and make it easier for the right people to stay connected to the same matter context in real time. This gives you more time to focus on the legal judgment, strategy, and client relationships that require your attention.
It can also change how your legal practice competes with larger firms. You may not be able to match their headcount, but legal collaboration AI can help your team increase its output with the people and resources you already have. You can handle more work, respond to clients faster, and maintain a high level of service without needing to grow your team at the same rate.
How Legal Collaboration Could Evolve From Here
Legal AI is already changing everyday legal practice, from legal research and document review to initial drafting. AI adoption reflects the pace of that change:
use among legal professionals rose from 19% in 2023 to 79% in 2024
.
Many of the building blocks for the next phase of legal collaboration already exist today, from AI agents that support multistep legal work to shared workspaces that keep teams connected to the same matter context. Over the next three to five years, the bigger change may be how broadly and continuously those capabilities operate across matters, teams, and organizations. Instead of starting from scratch each time work changes hands or a new matter begins, legal teams could increasingly work in environments where context, workflows, and collaboration carry forward. Here are some examples:
AI coordination spans more of the legal process.
AI agents can already support multistep workflows. Over time, they could carry context across more stages of a matter, track open questions and dependencies, and surface what needs attention as work moves between teams and organizations.
Sources stay connected as work moves.
When analysis passes between lawyers, clients, and outside counsel, the supporting sources and context could travel with it. That would make prior work easier to verify, reuse, and build on without repeating the same review.
Lean teams spend less time coordinating work.
AI already helps lawyers complete individual tasks faster. The next shift could be reducing the time spent chasing inputs, tracking decisions, and keeping clients, internal stakeholders, and outside counsel aligned.
Firm-client collaboration becomes more continuous.
Shared AI workspaces could carry knowledge, workflows, and context from one engagement to the next. Firms and clients could spend less time rebuilding background and more time moving new work forward.
Clients expect more from AI-enabled collaboration.
Clients are already asking firms how they use generative AI. Over time, they may focus more on the experience it creates — faster responses, better visibility, less repeated context-setting, and easier collaboration.

FAQs
1. Is client data secure when teams collaborate through AI?
Confidentiality and data ownership are the first questions most legal teams ask. Harvey runs on enterprise security, with encryption, access controls set matter by matter, and data residency options, so shared work stays protected. You can review the full approach on the
Harvey security
page.
2. Can law firms, clients, and in-house teams share one AI workspace?
Yes. A shared workspace lets a firm, its client, and in-house counsel work in one secure space, each with their own access. This is how firms and companies
collaborate at scale
instead of emailing versions back and forth.
3. Does AI for legal collaboration work with the tools legal teams already use?
Yes. Through its
ecosystem
, Harvey connects to Word, Outlook, SharePoint, and document management systems like iManage. Working where drafting and email already happen keeps everyone in sync without moving between systems.
4. What should legal teams look for in an AI collaboration platform?
Ask who owns the AI’s output, how your data is protected, and whether answers are grounded in sources you can trace. A platform built for collaboration should also work across organizations, not just inside one team.

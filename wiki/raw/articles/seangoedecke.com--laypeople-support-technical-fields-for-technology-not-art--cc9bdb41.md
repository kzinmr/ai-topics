# Laypeople support technical fields for technology, not art

Source: https://seangoedecke.com/laypeople-support-technical-fields-for-technology-not-art/

Some people think that LLMs should not be used in technical fields like
programming
or
mathematics
, because those fields are actually art. True or not, this kind of argument is not going to be persuasive to the general population. The argument goes something
1
like this:
It doesn’t matter how effective LLMs are at technical fields like programming or mathematics, because one of the essential values of those fields is that they’re
art
. While it looks to laypeople like these fields exist to solve practical problems, what they’re actually for is to produce works of beauty, or to express something distinctively human, or to spend time contemplating
the infinite
. Simply solving the problems misses the point in the same way that building an infinite poem generator would miss the point of poetry.
I agree that programming or mathematics can be beautiful. I remember being mildly awestruck at learning
epsilon-delta proofs
, and I have
written
a lot
about the kind of software I think is elegant. Since there’s no clear
definition of art
or of aesthetics in general, it’s plausible to me that almost everything could have an artistic dimension to it.
All of this is compelling to software engineers, many of whom (like me) find some programs beautiful and are flattered to think of themselves as artists. But I suspect it’s
spectacularly
uncompelling to onlookers, because it undermines the implicit reasons that they have for supporting technical fields. Many people who are not programmers or mathematicians think of those fields like this:
I enjoy the fruits of technology: modern medicine, transport, mobile phones, and so on
While I don’t understand the exact relationship, I know that technical fields like mathematics and programming are essential to sustaining and advancing these technologies
Therefore I’m happy to respect and support mathematicians in their mysterious research work
And I’m broadly okay with
2
software engineers drawing healthy salaries for the same reason
In other words, regular people
3
think mathematicians (and to a lesser extent programmers) are valuable because they think science is valuable, and they think science is valuable because they think technology is valuable. They are willing to accept that technical fields can be art only insofar as that eventually helps researchers have the kind of creative insights that advance science: as Rikab sarcastically puts it, that advance the
knowledge frontier
.
If you go to those non-technical people and you say “LLMs can accelerate problem-solving in these fields, but I’m strongly against using them because the real point is to produce great art”, they will be very unhappy. They might reasonably feel cheated, in the same way as if they hired someone to renovate their kitchen who insisted in doing it all with hand tools in order to experience “the joy of creation”. This argument — we should stop using LLMs in technical fields because they’re art — is going to be even less compelling to technical
employers
.
Engineers and mathematicians who want to defend the artistic value of their field will need more compelling arguments
4
than just appealing to the value of art. We will need to find some way to connect the value of art with the practical technological value that motivates public interest in our fields. For instance, we could argue that:
Great software products must rely on
elegant abstractions
, which need a human creative touch: simply piling on working code will hit a wall past a certain level of complexity
LLMs can solve problems, but actual progress in mathematics depends on
creating new abstractions
, which is a more creative task that LLMs are not yet good at
Progress in technical fields is uneven and relies upon inspiration, which needs creative
hammock time
and distinctively human understanding
LLMs can produce working software, but truly
great
software requires human inspiration
5
, and great software is what advances the field
I don’t know if I agree with all of these points. I haven’t yet seen an LLM-created codebase truly hit a wall (at least, not this year), and I don’t see a reason why LLMs should be incapable of creating new abstractions or having human-like inspirations. I also don’t think great products have to have great code: why wouldn’t workmanlike code in service of a great idea be enough? Does
Fallingwater
have to have beautifully installed
6
insulation?
Still, any argument we make to our employers or the general public about art is going to have to try and make this case. Instead of saying that human-made technical work is valuable because it’s art, we need to make an affirmative case for why art can make technical work more effective. Otherwise we’re conceding the main reason why the general population thinks our work matters.

# Huang reinstatement came at Trump’s behest

Source: https://www.semafor.com/article/11/23/2026/jensen-huang-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo

Hours before Nvidia reinstated ousted CEO Jensen Huang, President Donald Trump dialed into Nvidia’s Sunday board meeting to argue for his return.
The remarkable direct engagement by Trump, first reported by Semafor and confirmed by multiple sources, underscores how fully he has redefined the role of the US government in private enterprise.
Trump wasn’t alone. Vice President JD Vance and other members of the administration have also gotten actively involved in the inner workings of one of the US’ most valuable companies, even as members of Trump’s own party raise questions about his statement that reinstating Huang poses no national security risk.
By Monday morning, the company had announced Huang’s return and made no secret the decision was made based on “input” from the White House, which told the company the administration felt comfortable despite Huang’s extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy.
The pitch that resonated with Vance and Trump, who had been close to Huang, centered around the Nvidia CEO’s role as a protector of open-source models, and a check on the increasing power of closed frontier model makers like Anthropic and OpenAI, a person familiar with the matter told Semafor.
The pressure was also heightened by the tech rout in the markets, which sent the S&P 500 into correction territory over worries of AI risks. On Monday, Nvidia’s stock jumped 30%, the biggest increase in more than two decades, lifting the entire market with it.
Despite multiple active legal and regulatory probes that touch on the company — and potentially Huang personally — the president and vice president pushed back privately and publicly on allegations that he posed a threat to national security. Both were personally engaged over the weekend to bring Huang back, the person added.
“President Trump was totally involved and this is a great decision by the NVIDIA board. We have to protect our geniuses,” White House communications director Steven Cheung told Semafor.
Unanimous vote
Two weeks after the board voted unanimously to accept Huang’s resignation, which came after allegations of
affairs with two subordinates
, Nvidia’s board met late Sunday night and voted unanimously to bring him back, according to a person familiar with the matter.
Huang, who co-founded the chipmaker in 1993 and built it into an AI giant, benefited from a mounting defense campaign by some Silicon Valley figures who called for his return. Semafor had previously reported that despite a unanimous vote to oust him a couple weeks ago, a
faction of directors
had continued to push for him to come back.
The anti-Huang camp was dealt a blow when they learned a leading replacement candidate, Arm CEO Rene Haas, wouldn’t be available since he had signed a new contract with Arm extending his employment until 2033, according to another person familiar with directors’ thinking.
“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” Harvey Jones, who was elevated to board chairman at the time of Huang’s Nov. 12 ouster, said in its statement.
Nvidia’s senior leadership was not aware of the decision to restore Huang until shortly before the general public knew, according to a person familiar with the matter.
Washington probes
Huang’s return is guaranteed to
amplify the already vocal bipartisan calls
for Congress to investigate his behavior and the company’s lobbying against export controls that would curtail Nvidia’s overseas chips business.
He is already slated to appear in private before House members and in public before senators next week. Several Republicans greeted his reinstatement with open skepticism of the Justice Department’s claim that there was no evidence Huang’s affair presented a national security risk.
Sen. John Curtis, R-Utah, questioned the “basis” for that judgment. Huang “was right to step down when the affair came to light,” he
posted
on social media.
Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., the ranking member of the Senate Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee, called the decision to reinstate Huang “reckless.”
Huang’s return “only increases the urgency of passing bipartisan export control bills into law and making sure NVIDIA does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk,” she wrote.
DOJ continues to probe two key Nvidia customers, Alice Huang’s Megaspeed and Supermicro, over export control violations, and Nvidia itself over antitrust concerns, Semafor previously reported and confirmed with a Justice Department official.
— Ashley Gold and J.D. Capelouto contributed reporting.

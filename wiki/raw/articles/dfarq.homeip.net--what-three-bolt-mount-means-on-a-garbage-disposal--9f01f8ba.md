# What three-bolt mount means on a garbage disposal

Source: https://dfarq.homeip.net/what-three-bolt-mount-means-on-a-garbage-disposal/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=what-three-bolt-mount-means-on-a-garbage-disposal

I was looking at store brand garbage disposals and saw a term that I’m not sure everyone means. It said it uses a three-bolt mount for easy retrofit. What does that mean? What does three bolt mount mean on a garbage disposal?
Ace’s house brand garbage disposals use a three-bolt mount like an Insinkerator.
Store-brand and off-brand garbage disposals will mention a three bolt mount when they don’t want to mention the name of a competitor, namely, Insinkerator. Insinkerator garbage disposals use a three-bolt mounting system to attach to your sink. They’ve been using that system for decades. If you need to replace a worn out Insinkerator garbage disposal, don’t want it to take all weekend, but don’t want to pay the Insinkerator price, looking for a store brand unit that features a three-bolt mount for easy retrofit is the one you want.
Some of these disposals are actually just relabeled Insinkerators. But Moen also makes garbage disposals with the three-bolt system, as well as its own system. If it looks like an Insinkerator, it probably is. But if the disposal looks a little different, and especially if the disposal lacks the hex bolt in the bottom to turn the disposal with a wrenchette when it jams, it’s probably a Moen.
Ace Hardware garbage disposals
use the three-bolt system. I also recently found a Glacier Bay unit at Home Depot that uses the three-bolt system. In that case, installation is easier because you can just twist off the old garbage disposal, leave the existing mount in place, and just twist on the new garbage disposal. As long as the old one wasn’t leaking from the sink, you’re fine to leave the old mount in place. Doing so can cut an hour off the installation time. Especially if you don’t change disposals very often.

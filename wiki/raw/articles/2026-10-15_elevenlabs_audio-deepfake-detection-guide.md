Summary
Deepfake synthesized speech is becoming harder to detect by ear alone. It poses a significant risk in both public life and business.
Multiple types of deepfake detection software exist. Conventional tools use statistical analysis to assess the probability that an audio sample is AI-generated.
New watermarking technology allows you to get a definitive yes or no answer as to whether a voice sample was synthesized.
Watermarking is highly accurate, but detectors only work on a provider’s own audio. For example, ElevenLabs’ Audio Detector can identify watermarks generated by its own products with high accuracy but not those from other companies.
Watermarking is an important resource for organizations that need large-scale deepfake detection, but it should be only one of many tools in their deepfake-detection stack.
What is audio deepfake detection?
Audio deepfake detection is the process of determining whether voice audio was generated or manipulated by artificial intelligence.
A deepfake is any form of media (image, video, or audio, most commonly) created by a generative AI application for the purpose of misrepresenting what someone says or does. Audio deepfakes have many malicious use cases. They can be used for fraud, to attempt to access personal accounts, as political propaganda, or to secretly impersonate a spouse or family member in domestic disputes.
Based on current trends, the victims of audio deepfake scams are most likely to be:
Politicians & other public figures
: Individuals with extensive voice samples available for training online might have their voice
deepfaked by opponents or third parties
to mislead the public.
Family members
: A bad actor may attempt to use found voice recordings to scam elderly family members with deepfaked requests for money.
Corporate executives
: Faked interviews, presentations, and earnings calls might be used to harm a company’s brand.
Customer service teams
: Scammers might use collected
voice samples
to scam customer service teams into granting private account access.
Unfortunately, humans are not reliable detectors of deepfakes. In controlled research settings,
people’s ability to identify them is no better than chance
, and that research is from back in 2024. Generative media has grown significantly more sophisticated since then.
Education and legislation will go a long way towards inoculating society against the influence of deepfakes, but reliable detection methods are still necessary.
How does audio deepfake detection work?
Conventional deepfake detection uses statistical analysis to measure the probability that media was generated by an AI. A detection tool uses machine learning techniques to recognize the underlying patterns in different types of synthesized speech.
The first step to doing this is building a library of audio content to compare suspected deepfakes against. This should be a diverse set of data, meaning it needs to include genuine audio samples of a variety of types and many known deepfake samples to help make positive detections.
At runtime, it follows a three-stage process: preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification.
Audio preprocessing
When you give a trained detection tool an audio sample, it converts it into spectrographic data, or a mathematical representation of frequency changes over time.
Feature extraction
Next, the detection tool extracts features from the sample data that are like fingerprints for synthesized speech. For example, AI speech is sometimes too perfect and lacks the tonal and timing irregularities found in natural speech. Comparing the speaker
’
s pitch and tone to its library will also help the tool identify other red flags.
Classification
Because conventional deepfake detection occurs through statistical analysis, it can’t give you a definitive “yes” or “no” answer to the question of whether a sample is a deepfake. Instead, it generates a confidence score, or probability, that the sample is a deepfake.
Because it measures similarities between a sample and an existing audio library, conventional tools are limited by the known deepfake samples they have available to them. Anytime someone releases a new voice-cloning architecture, you need to retrain your detection tool on speech synthesized in that new system to accurately detect it.
What’s the difference between watermarking
and
statistical detection?
Watermarking
is an entirely different approach to speech classification. A machine learning classifier makes a probabilistic guess whether a sample is authentic based on what it knows from its training data about natural speech, like what real emotional weight and emphasis look like in spectrographic data, and what types of tonal and timing changes are natural.
Watermarking is deterministic. A watermark is a mathematical signature embedded within audio data by the synthesizer at levels a human listener can’t hear. A watermark detector verifies, yes or no, whether it finds that hidden signature.
A detector operates in three straightforward stages:
Extraction
: The tool extracts spectrographic data and isolates the frequencies where it expects to find a watermark.
Key matching
: It attempts to unlock the signature with a key.
Verification
: If the signal decodes correctly, the tool confirms that the
AI voice generator
in question generated the audio.

FAQ
Can deepfake voice detectors tell if any audio is a deepfake, or just from one provider?
It depends on the detection software
’
s architecture. The two most common types are speech classifiers and watermark detectors.
A speech classifier is a machine learning engine. It has pre-analyzed audio from many different AI voice generation engines. When you give it an audio clip, it performs a statistical analysis and scores the clip based on how similar it sounds to AI audio in its training data. Classifiers can often score audio from any provider, but they
’
re less accurate if their training data didn
’
t include that provider’s engine.
A watermark detector gives a definitive yes-or-no answer as to whether an audio clip includes the invisible watermark from a specific provider. But these detectors can only detect watermarks from their own service, such as ElevenLabs’ Audio Detector for ElevenLabs voice data.
How accurate is AI voice detection?
Watermark detectors are highly accurate. Classifiers are probabilistic by nature and will return a confidence score reflecting how closely a sample matches known patterns of AI-generated speech. ElevenLabs’ Speech Classifier has 99% precision and 80% recall.
What is audio watermarking and how is it different from a detector?
An audio watermark is an inaudible mathematical signature encoded in digital audio. It’s only revealed when audio is transformed into spectrographic data and a watermark detector tries to unlock the signature with a secret key.
A provider needs to build both a secret watermarking system and the detector app to read those watermarks

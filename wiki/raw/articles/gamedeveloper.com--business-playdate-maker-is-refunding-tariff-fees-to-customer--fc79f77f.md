# Playdate maker Panic is refunding tariff fees to customers

Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/playdate-maker-is-refunding-tariff-fees-to-customers

Crank-driven handheld Playdate maker Panic emailed customers this week to spread some good news: The company received tariff refunds from the United States government, and is passing that along to customers. People who purchased a Playdate from Panic during the period in which the tariffs were charged will get the refunds.
"There was a specific window of time this year where we charged tariffs to customers," Panic co-founder Cabel Sasser explained in an e-mail toGame Developer confirming the news. "We tried absorbing the cost for a while, but that was financially difficult for a small company like ours. We made the tariff a very obvious and clearly labeled line item on checkout for any customer affected."
Panic also
set up a website
to explain the 19% tariff surcharge.
The United States Supreme Court
ruled that United States president Donald Trump's tariffs
were not a legal use of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. Companies impacted by the tariffs had to apply for their share of the refund. Some companies, like Nintendo, are keeping the refund money. (
Nintendo and other companies
are being sued over this. The main argument is that buyers chose to purchase respective items knowing that tariffs would be charged.)

Retailers like Target, Home Depot, and Walmart were refunded billions of dollars. Nintendo's tariff refund was $300 million,
the company reported in its quarterly filings
.
Few companies are seemingly choosing to give that refund back to customers. It's largely
shipping companies like UPS that are refunding customers
. Playdate is one of a few companies that is passing along the refund money. The refund amount a customer will get back depends on how much they purchased.
"It's just not our money to keep, and it felt really good to give it back," Sasser said. "That's an easy way to know you made the right decision."
He continued: "Still, it took a lot of work for us to file all the paperwork to get the refund from the government, and it took work to build a little system to process the refund, and sometimes it felt like, wait, why are we seemingly doing more work than even gigantic companies are on this issue? That felt weird. But I guess it makes sense in a way, because our small size means we’re far more directly affected by something like a tariff."
Sasser said he hopes Panic never has to talk about tariffs again. "From start to finish on this whole tariff whim, all I could think about is all the cool stuff we could be working on instead," he said.

# Apple is getting this wrong | OpenAI

OpenAI published a public response to Apple's lawsuit, accusing Apple of making false claims. Key allegations from OpenAI:

1. **Apple's lawyers emailed the wrong person**: Apple claimed they contacted OpenAI in February without response. OpenAI revealed that Apple's outside lawyers emailed the wrong person after confusing two Asian last names.

2. **Fabricated meeting with General Counsel**: Apple claimed they had a discussion with OpenAI's General Counsel, which Apple now concedes never happened.

3. **Apple never raised specific allegations**: Apple told OpenAI they were "resolving any issues" and then went silent for five months before suing.

4. **Employee departure dispute**: Apple accuses Chang Li of taking trade secrets. OpenAI claims Li's work was entirely open-source and public.

5. **Broader context**: This is part of Apple's aggressive legal campaign against AI companies hiring former Apple employees, raising questions about non-compete enforcement and talent mobility in AI.

OpenAI characterizes the lawsuit as "careless, aggressive and oddly personal" and publishes email correspondence as evidence.

## Evidence: iMessage Transcripts

OpenAI published iMessages between Chang Liu (in blue) and Apple employees, where Chang—whose last day with Apple was January 22, 2026—is being asked by his former colleagues to help them locate files and information to assist them with their Apple work.

Key exchanges:
- **Jan 22-23, 2026**: Apple employees ask Liu to help copy files from his Apple iCloud and laptop before his departure. Liu cooperates, providing a 64GB drive and helping with AirDrop transfers.
- **Jan 27, 2026**: Liu warns Apple employee to sign off his iMessage on the old laptop as "there might be my new company stuff showing up." Apple employee asks Liu about shipping plans for Apple products — Liu answers their questions.
- **Jan 31, 2026**: Apple employee confirms signing out of Liu's iCloud and keeping NO copy of his stuff. Liu jokes about the startup life.
- **Feb 14, 2026**: Apple employee continues asking Liu technical questions about Apple hardware projects even after he's left. Liu notes "The problem is the people is also in my court now 😁😁" — referring to former Apple colleagues now at OpenAI.

This evidence directly contradicts Apple's claim that Liu improperly accessed Apple confidential information. Instead, it shows Apple employees proactively reaching out to Liu and asking for his help.

## Evidence: Email Correspondence

OpenAI also published emails between Apple's outside counsel (Gabriel Gross), OpenAI's General Counsel (Che Chang), and Apple's in-house legal counsel. The emails show:
- Gross emailed the wrong person after confusing two Asian last names
- Gross inaccurately claimed to have spoken with OpenAI's General Counsel
- Both Gross and Apple's in-house counsel subsequently confirmed this was an error
- Apple's in-house counsel noted they were "resolving any issues"
- The specific claims in Apple's lawsuit were never raised in these communications
- There was no further follow-up for five months before the lawsuit was filed

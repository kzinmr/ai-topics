# AI is driving a boom in modestly successful software projects

Source: https://seangoedecke.com/ai-is-driving-a-boom-in-modestly-successful-software/
Author: Sean Goedecke (GitHub). Fetched 2026-10-16.

## Question posed

"If AI is so great, why aren't we seeing an explosion of new, amazing software
products? If anyone can turn their idea into working software with coding agents,
where is all that software?" People have asked this for years. Goedecke's answer:
it's spread out over a lot of modestly successful projects.

## Headline findings

- Somewhere between 2x and 20x new software is being created, depending on where you look.
- No real growth in massively successful software projects (not counting AI tools).
- Around 2x growth in modestly successful software projects.

## Method

Picked ~20 separate sources that expose installation counts — software marketplaces,
plugin registries, modding platforms — and compared the number of *successful*
projects between 2022-24 and 2025-26 (before vs. after agentic coding hit the
mainstream in mid-2025).

## Evidence (registry-by-registry)

- NVDA screen-reader addons directory: >2x new addons with >1,000 downloads; **no change** in addons with >10,000 downloads.
- HACS (open-source home automation community plugin store): 2x new addons with >100 installs; **no change** in ones with >1,000 installs.
- New Steam games with >100 reviews: ~60% more common now than pre-2024.
- Obsidian plugins, Firefox/Chrome extensions, Nexus game mods, Blender extensions: same pattern.
- Google Play store, WordPress plugins, and a few others: did **not** show the pattern.

## Why addons/mods/plugins

1. Easy to tell how many people installed/used them (installation counts), separating "made" from "actually used" — critical given the vibe-coded repo explosion.
2. Minimizes software that is *about AI* ("selling shovels"). The real question is whether AI helps generate software that solves ordinary problems. AI-generated game mods/ports (multiplayer Halo CE in browser, fly a plane in Helldivers, Super Smash online with rollback netcode) are examples where gamers just want ordinary features.

## Were these actually AI-generated?

Hobbyist communities are coy about AI usage; disclosure tags discourage use, so voluntary-disclosure data is unusable. A 2x growth in successful projects (against a 10x growth in total projects) is "clearly something to do with AI."

## Why small/moderate projects, not big ones (speculation, no data)

- Big successful companies already have all the visible features they want — hence no feature explosion in macOS/Gmail. They are also "technically paralyzed by the features they have," so good coding agents can't trivially add new ones. Many new features in successful products are subtle (enterprise compat, internal reliability systems).
- Why no new *wildly* successful hobbyist projects: maybe good product ideas are just hard and AI can't help; or really successful projects take years to develop and the current crop of thousand-user tools needs time to grow into hundred-thousand-user tools.

# Initial Thoughts on This Week’s New Apple TV 4K (4th Generation) and HomePod Mini 2

Source: https://daringfireball.net/2026/10/thoughts_on_new_apple_tv_4k_and_homepod_mini_2

Initial Thoughts on This Week’s New Apple TV 4K (4th Generation) and HomePod Mini 2
Thursday, 15 October 2026
Apple Newsroom, Tuesday morning
:
The new Apple TV 4K features three far-field microphones, letting
users control what they’re watching and discover new shows
completely hands-free. Advanced echo cancellation intelligently
isolates a user’s voice from background sound to deliver
crystal-clear “Hey Siri” or “Siri” recognition, even while audio
is playing.
This is nice, but I don’t think it buys me anything, because my existing Apple TV 4K is paired with 2nd-gen HomePods, and the HomePods each have a “four-microphone design for far-field Siri”.
The A19 chip in the new Apple TV 4K delivers up to 50 percent
faster CPU performance and up to 2× faster GPU performance than
the previous generation, for greater responsiveness, faster
navigation, and smoother animations. The A19 chip also includes a
new, dedicated AV1 decoder, enabling more efficient, high-quality
streaming while using less bandwidth.
The new AV1 decoder seems like it might be meaningful, but does anyone think UI navigation and animation smoothness is improved by going from the A15 to the A19 SoC? I mean navigation is going up/down/left/right. I’m sure the graphics and CPU improvements matter for gaming, but the only mention of gaming in Apple’s own Newsroom announcement is this sentence: “With Apple Fitness+, the living room becomes a workout studio, while Apple Arcade and the App Store’s library of games turn it into a home arcade.” I wish it were otherwise, but gaming is such an afterthought on Apple TV that it only garnered the second half of a sentence that started with Fitness+, a service that has only been in the news this year
because Mark Gurman says Apple laid off part of the team
.
The new Siri Remote keeps its familiar design while adding Find My
support with Precision Finding and an option to play a sound on
the remote — making it far easier to locate.
That’s my single favorite feature in the announcement. I hate to say it though, but the previous remote should have supported these features. The 3rd-gen Apple TV 4K came in 2022, but
Apple launched AirTags with Precision Finding in 2021
. Remote controls are right after keys on the list of things people misplace most frequently, and Apple’s remote controls, because they’re small and thin, are particularly susceptible to being snatched by couch-cushion gremlins. Speakers, so the remote can play a sound to help you find it, were invented even earlier than 2021.
Pairing HomePod with Apple TV 4K already offers users an immersive
viewing experience that’s easy to set up, and the new Apple TV 4K
takes it even further. For the first time, users can create a
four-speaker surround sound experience with Apple TV 4K (4th
generation) and HomePod mini 2. The four speakers work together
with Apple TV 4K to intelligently recognize their position in the
room so audio timing and levels are automatically calibrated to
the space. HomePod (2nd generation) can also be used in this
four-speaker setup for the ultimate audio experience, as long as
the front and rear speaker pairs match.
This is tempting to try. In other words, in addition to the two 2nd-gen HomePods I already have paired in my living room, I can add two of the new HomePod Mini 2’s behind the sofa for surround sound.
The elephant in the room, though, is price. Before this week, Apple sold two models of the 3rd-generation Apple TV. On a percentage basis, they were hit the hardest of any products Apple sells by

The new Apple TV 4K announced this week only comes in one model, and it costs $300. It’s great that there’s only one model for simplicity’s sake, but the number one complaint when the previous generation cost $130/150 was that it was too expensive compared to set-top boxes and sticks from Amazon and Roku. (Or the “free” option of using the “smart TV” software that comes with every TV set.) Now, with the new Apple TV, it’s twice as expensive — and Apple is not keeping the 3rd-gen models around as lower-priced options. I still think it’s worth it, personally, but the general consumer market thought the old prices were too high and I don’t see any of the new features making Joe or Jane Consumer think “
Sure, I was skeptical about the old one at $130, but this new model, which looks exactly the same, this seems worth $300.
”
HomePod Mini 2 now costs $150. That’s not too much of an increase over the new-since-June-25 $130 price for the old HomePod Mini, but it’s a stiff 50 percent increase from
the old HomePod Mini’s $100 price from November 2020 through June 24 this year
. I generally round Apple’s prices up from their “99” values, but it’s worth noting that what I’m calling $100 was actually $99, and going to just two digits
does have a psychological effect on buying behavior
. Again, I think $150 is a reasonable price for the HomePod Mini 2, but it doesn’t feel as much like a lark as $99 did. $99 was a bit of a “
Sure, let’s get another one
” price; $150 is more of a “
Do we really need another one of these?
” price.
At the old pre-June prices, you could buy a 128 GB Apple TV and a pair of HomePod Minis for $350 ($150 + $100 + $100). Four short months later, with today’s new models, that combination would cost $600 ($300 + $150 + $150). That price difference is much more striking than any of the new and improved features in the products.

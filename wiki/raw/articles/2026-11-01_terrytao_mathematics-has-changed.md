## Why we should not be sad

The release by OpenAI on October 6 of over 700 research papers was a watershed in the history of mathematics. AI is now undeniably capable of making groundbreaking contributions across many areas of mathematics. So why are some mathematicians so unhappy?

Some of it is the apparent disregard by AI companies for the practice of mathematics. The OpenAI papers — despite some tremendous ideas — are atrociously written and violate our community expectations of attribution. Some of them are even wrong. We have much more control over how we hire mathematicians, publish papers, and train students than over the behavior of trillion-dollar companies, and that is where our efforts would be better spent.

Much of the reaction seems to reflect a feeling that the subject that they love is under attack, and is being "hollowed out". The recent OpenAI dump made barely a dent on the general problems of functoriality and reciprocity in the Langlands program. But even if it had solved every problem in the Langlands program, to imagine that would have "solved number theory" would be as naive as saying that the construction of the periodic table solved chemistry. Mathematicians should do a better job explaining that when we talk about the central problems in any area, we are merely discussing the most natural boundaries of our understanding — and that unlocking them is not the end but the first step to finding new playgrounds to explore.

Hugo Duminil-Copin's lament: "Not a single one of the major open problems I have publicly mentioned throughout my career (whether in a talk, a lecture, an article, or even a grant proposal) was left untouched by the announcement. Everything has been claimed to be proved."

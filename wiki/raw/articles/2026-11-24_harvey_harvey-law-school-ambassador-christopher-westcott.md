Christopher Westcott on Using Harvey to Study Smarter and Interview With Confidence
A conversation with Christopher Westcott, J.D. Candidate at Wake Forest University School of Law and Harvey Student Ambassador.
by
Harvey Team
•
Nov 23, 2026
This fall, we caught up with Christopher Westcott as finals approached. Christopher is a J.D. Candidate at Wake Forest University School of Law and one of our Student Ambassadors in Harvey's
Law School Ambassador Program
. With the MPRE a week away and a stack of deadlines ahead of him, he walked us through how he uses Harvey to study for exams, how it helped him through recruiting, and why he's put so much into helping classmates learn how to use Harvey in their work.
How did you get into legal AI, and what made you focus on Harvey?
I got interested in AI in undergrad, when generative AI first took off and I was surprised by what it could do just from playing around with it. That made me want to dig into it once I got to law school. As a 1L, I learned that some of the 2Ls had started an AI organization on campus called SAIL, so I got in touch with the leadership. By my 2L year, I was part of the leadership team, focused on teaching students how to use AI and how to use it ethically.
During my 2L, Wake Forest partnered with Harvey and everyone I talked to, from practitioners to teachers, told me it was the best legal AI out there. So I stepped away from SAIL to put all my emphasis on training students on Harvey. Especially for students headed to big law and mid-sized firms, I thought it would be the AI platform they'd interact with regularly and use day to day.
How has Harvey changed your day-to-day in law school?
When I got access to Harvey, I mostly used it as a thought partner or a tutor. If I didn’t understand something in class, I’d upload the class content and ask Harvey to walk me through it. Most recently, I’ve been using Harvey to generate practice questions and practice exams. I had an environmental law midterm a few weeks ago, and I’d already taken five or six practice exams I generated in Harvey before I sat down for it. That helped a lot because I got used to the timing and to the kinds of questions our professor had told us to expect. I’d done a bunch of reps on that material, so by the time I got into the exam, I was really comfortable.
Instead of replacing how I think, Harvey made my thinking about the class content that much deeper. I really appreciated that, because it's nice to know what you're talking about when you're taking an exam.
“
Instead of replacing how I was thinking about things, :Harvey: made my thinking about class content that much deeper.
”
Christopher Westcott
J.D. Candidate at Wake Forest University School of Law
What's been your favorite way to use Harvey this semester?
I've really enjoyed partnering up with my classmates in Vault. If we're taking the same exam, we set up a Vault for the class. For my environmental law class, all of us put our notes and slides into it, and we work from it together.
For the midterm, the classmates I partnered with all did really well. I think that's largely because we got practice through Harvey, but also because we made sure we didn't miss any material since we were all working together.
Did you use Harvey to prepare for interviews and recruiting?
I used Harvey for the pre-interview process. When I was writing my cover letter, I had a hard time distilling everything in my life onto one page. So I gave Harvey my CV, my LinkedIn, and the cover letter I'd already written, and asked it to help me condense everything and make it more precise. I found that very helpful. One of the law partners I later accepted an offer from told me my cover letter was really well written.
I also found that job applications have keywords in them so I'd copy and paste a job description into Harvey and ask it to edit my resume and cover letter to highlight what the employer was looking for. That was really helpful. When I was a 1L, I spent the summer practicing family law, but more recently I was applying to real estate jobs. When Harvey edited my resume for a real estate role, it highlighted the equitable distribution and property valuation parts of my family law work instead of the custody disputes, which makes sense, because it was tailoring things for a more property-focused area of practice. Before I applied to any job, I'd have Harvey help me tailor my resume and cover letter to that specific application, and that really streamlined the process.
“
Before I applied to any job, I'd have :Harvey: help me tailor my resume and cover letter to that specific application, and that really streamlined the process.
”
Christopher Westcott
J.D. Candidate at Wake Forest University School of Law
Any other ways Harvey helped with your job search?
Yes, and I keep telling people this one. If you're applying to big law and a firm's website doesn't have an education tab, it's really annoying to click through every single attorney profile to see who attended what law school. So I'd put the firm's URL into Harvey and ask it to tell me everyone who went to Wake Forest. That way I didn't have to click through everything.
It doesn't even need a specific URL. You can say, "I want to practice in Charlotte. This is the area of law I'm interested in. Can you give me some contacts who went to Wake Forest from a mid-sized firm, a big firm, and a small firm?" It's great for networking, because it's much easier than doing all that research yourself.
Why did you want to be part of Harvey's Ambassador Program?
I took an advanced legal research class, and AI was the biggest topic we talked about all semester because it's at the forefront of research and of how lawyers are going to practice. I was part of SAIL, so my professor kind of looked at me as the AI expert in the room. When she told me about the Harvey Ambassador Program, I was really excited.
I wanted to put all my attention into Harvey, because I know it's going to be the legal AI platform people use going forward. It's huge in the market, and I want to be prepared for that when I go off to work as an attorney.

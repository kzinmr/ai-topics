# oai-math-revised: a community hub for revising OpenAI’s math write-ups

		24 October, 2026 in advertising | Tags: Abhinav Nellore, Johannes Schmitt, Rachel Ward, Soledad Villar | by Terence Tao		

	

	
		
[This is a guest post by Abhinav Nellore, Johannes Schmitt, Soledad Villar, and Rachel Ward – T.]

OpenAI’s math write-ups at https://github.com/openai/math can be difficult to understand and can have incomplete citations.

https://github.com/mathcommunity/oai-math-revised/ seeks to be an inclusive effort to improve these write-ups, and the broader math community–students, postdocs, professors, and enthusiasts–is invited to participate.

The repo is browsable at https://mathcommunity.github.io/oai-math-revised/

We also invite the community to email mathcommunityadmin@gmail.com if they want to serve as admins/reviewers and for which categories from https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/overview.pdf they’d be appropriate for reviews.

						@Guest post writers: Thank you for this initiative! Not sure if this makes sense: is there any way to add another family tag to the problems where contributions, even if true, are overstated?

For instance, in family 102 (The Unique Games Conjecture and optimal approximation thresholds) within TCS, if UGC is true (as claimed in “The Unique Games Theorem“), then many results are already known to hold. Hence, contributions of such papers, as stated in the abstract, are an oversell.

For example, the abstract in The Factor-Two Hardness Threshold for Vertex Cover claims that “We prove that minimum Vertex Cover is NP-hard to approximate within every fixed factor below two, even on simple unweighted graphs.”. However, given UGC is true, Khot and Regev already proved this bound in 2008. Hence, the significance of the Vertex Cover result becomes unclear. Yes, newer methods may be introduced, but the paper should clearly highlight what the contribution is. As such, it currently seems to oversell. This is not an isolated case within this family (another e.g., Max-Cut hardness was also established by Khot, Kindler, Mossel, and O’Donnell in 2007), and hence, may also be seen in other families.

Overall, an outcome of this additional tag is that it may help us channelize the efforts more appropriately?

## Summary

A ~56,000-character near-future **speculative short story** (also released as a free epub),
framed with a Nick Bostrom *Deep Utopia* epigraph about machine superintelligence as a "bullet
train" toward accomplishing everything with no effort.

Protagonist **Edwin Yu**, a senior official in a "Department of Frontier Oversight", navigates a
world where AI systems outperform licensed professionals on essentially every regulated decision.
Key beats:

- Domestic life is already agent-saturated (smart kitchen, service bots, whole-house AI remodels),
  but Edwin resists adopting "just a little more" convenience — the generational tension runs
  through the family.
- Edwin runs an analysis showing that for **all 78 regulated medical decisions** covered by the
  "RHO" (Regulated Human Oversight) regime, **blindly accepting the AI's suggestions leads to
  better outcomes than following a licensed doctor's contrary opinion** — and visualizations show
  near-total AI overperformance in the medical decision cluster.
- A Mayo Clinic doctor (Dr. Barnett) confirms off the record: institutions officially support
  mandatory human-in-the-loop oversight (RHO 2.0, lobbied for by the AMA), but "the new systems
  really are better than any of us", and residents trained with AI tutoring/simulation are the
  best she has seen.
- **Coda**: Edwin's final report recommends exempting every regulated decision from the requirement
  that humans make them unaided; the oversight requirement ends up applying to nothing and is
  quietly repealed 18 months later.

The story's thesis (via character Mallory) is about **meaning after obsolescence**: the virtues we
admire in people — striving for excellence, discipline, keeping faith with people who count on you
— were never about economic output. "Being needed matters… But the economy is not a *whom*."
Purpose is something people give each other by choosing each other; closing with "Be excellent to
each other."

## Why it matters for the wiki

Fictional dramatization of the same empirical question Anthropic's 50-analyst randomized trial
studies: what happens to expert judgment when agents outperform experts on the decisions themselves.
Pairs with [[concepts/cognitive-debt]] and human-in-the-loop / oversight debates.

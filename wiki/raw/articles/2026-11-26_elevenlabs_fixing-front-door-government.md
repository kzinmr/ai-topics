Getting it right
Call almost any government agency and you know what's coming: a robotic voice, a confusing menu, and a reminder that your call is important. You press 2, then 4, then 0 because none of the options quite fit. Then you wait.
If you can't stay on hold through your lunch break, you hang up and try again tomorrow. Some people never call back, and they go without the benefit, permit, or answer they needed.
According to the
US Inspector General
, in 2025, 25 million calls to the Social Security Administration’s number ended without service. People hung up, missed the callback, or couldn’t be reached. In January 2025, Social Security’s average hold times peaked at 1 hour 40 minutes. Service did improve by the end of the year, and the Inspector General credited a new phone platform and more automation for much of that.
Automation alone isn't the answer, though. At the IRS, the phone is still the main way taxpayers reach out, and in 2025 the agency began routing nearly 8 million calls to voice bots. According to the
National Taxpayer Advocate's 2025 report to Congress
, the bots answered only 29% of those calls. The other 71% were disconnected or abandoned, most likely because callers wanted to talk to a person. A scripted bot that can't understand people or finish the job just adds one more way for a call to fail.
This broken interaction is the front door to the government for millions of people. At ElevenLabs, we're working to fix that front door with
voice agents
that understand what callers need and can actually get it done.
Where voice agents help today
Governments are already using voice agents for several kinds of calls.
Answering and routing
The first job is replacing the outdated automated
IVR
phone menu. A voice agent can understand the question, answer it, or send the caller to the right department the first time. Think of a city's 311 line: an agent can handle routine questions and triage the rest, so staff can spend their time on calls that need human judgment. It can also answer in the caller's language and after hours, providing service to people who may need it the most.
In Ukraine, the
Ministry of Economy's voice agent
, built on ElevenAgents, answers the State Employment Service hotline 24/7 and hands calls to a person when needed. In its first 80 days it handled 2,698 calls, about a quarter of the line's volume, and cut the time to answer from 30–40 seconds to 18. When air raid alerts sent operators to shelters, the agent kept the line open.
Status checks, scheduling, and service requests
Callers want to check where an application or refund stands, book or move an appointment, or update their information. These are among the most common reasons people call.  The agent can’t handle them by talking alone: it has to verify who it's talking to and connect to the agency's systems. With that access, it can resolve the request instead of just passing it along.
In Poland, ElevenLabs powers the voice agent for
Centralna e-Rejestracja
, the central appointment system in the national e-Health platform. It calls patients who only have a landline to remind them of appointments and reschedule when they need to. With 40 million appointments a year and no-show rates of 10–20%, even a modest reduction in missed appointments can meaningfully improve access to care across Poland.
Strengthening and protecting elections
In partnership with
Brazil’s National Electoral Court (TSE)
, we gave a voice to its Electoral Assistant, making election information more accessible to citizens. We also used our
No Go Voices
technology to protect the voices of several senior political figures, including presidential candidates, and Supreme Court justices, and blocked nearly 2,000 impersonation attempts.
Guided applications
A voice agent can walk someone through a multi-step benefits or permit application, collect what's needed along the way, and hand it off to a caseworker when judgment is required. In
Deloitte's 2026 Digital Citizen Survey
of 1,000 Americans, 55% said they expect AI to improve public services, and 48% named step-by-step guidance when applying for government services as an important need.
In India, we recently signed a
partnership with the state of Karnataka
. We're working with the state's technology agency to explore voice AI pilots, starting with grievance redressal, public helplines, and entitlement schemes, in Kannada and other Indian languages among the 14 we support across the country.
How voice agents improve accessibility
Language.
According to the
Government Accountability Office
, 26 million people in the U.S. have limited ability to read, speak, write, or understand English. A menu with two or three language options leaves many of them out, and translation is expensive: the National Weather Service spent about $2.7 million between 2022 and 2025 to bring AI translation to five languages. ElevenLabs’ latest voice models support more than 90 languages, which puts language access within reach for more agencies.
Connectivity.
Not everyone can just go online. The
FCC's 2026 broadband report
found about 10.5 million Americans still can't get high-speed home internet over a wired or fixed wireless connection, and more than 9 million of them live in rural areas.
Pew Research
finds that one in five rural adults relies on a smartphone with no home broadband. For them, a phone call is the front door, and in an emergency it may be the only one.
24/7 availability.
Most government phone lines are staffed during business hours, and many people with jobs, kids, or shift work can't call then. A voice agent picks up at 2 am on a Sunday with the same patience it has at 10 am on a Tuesday.
Why now: not every caller will be human
Government phone lines are already stretched. Soon, many of the calls won't come from people.
Gartner predicts
that by the end of 2030, more than 10 billion autonomous
AI agents
will clog public services by the end of 2030, finding benefits, checking eligibility, and filing claims on their users’ behalf. Government agencies are already seeing a
sharp rise in inquiries
to government agencies’ support sites.
Some of those agents will belong to scammers. The
IRS's 2026 Dirty Dozen
list of tax scams includes AI-enabled impersonation calls that use voice mimicry and spoofed caller ID. The Inspector General lists
audio deepfakes
among the ways AI can be exploited to commit benefits fraud.
Every automated call that ties up a line means a longer wait for a real person, and agencies can't deflect that volume forever. They'll need AI of their own to serve legitimate agents efficiently, catch fraudulent ones, and keep the line open for the people who need it. Fixing the front door also means securing it.
From the front door to the whole house
The front door is where we start, but we think AI can help rebuild the whole house. Government services are fragmented because they are organized around agencies rather than people's lives.
For example, starting a small business means dealing with at least three different agencies.
Some governments are already bringing services under one roof. Greece's single digital portal, gov.gr, now offers more than 2,200 services. In May 2026, we
signed a partnership with the Greek government
to explore bringing voice AI to its call centers and to gov.gr, so citizens can reach those services in any language. That includes the Greek diaspora and people who find the internet hard to use.
In the U.S., the federal government launched
America.gov
in September 2026, an AI-powered portal that draws on more than 29,000 federal websites. For now, it answers questions in text; applying, enrolling, and tracking progress are planned for 2027.
Now imagine that same front door with a voice. You pick up the phone, explain what you need in your own language, and get routed to the right part of government. Behind the scenes, agents across agencies talk to each other and resolve the issue, while you stay on one call.
Getting it right
Public trust depends on how this is done. Governments need voice agents that:
Sound human.
A robotic voice is a big part of why people abandon. Natural, expressive speech makes people more willing to stay on the line.
Resolve issues end to end.
Success should be measured by whether the caller's problem got solved, not by how many calls were contained.
Serve people in their language.
Language barriers can prevent people from calling in the first place. Agents should recognize the language a caller speaks and answer in it.
Are secure by design.
Citizen data needs protection at every step. That means data residency, zero retention, PII redaction, and security and compliance certifications, including SOC 2, GDPR, and FedRAMP.
Can detect and route AI callers.
More and more callers will be AI, not human.
Synthetic voice detection
can tell you which one is which and set rules to put humans at the front of the queue.
Protect citizens from fraud.
AI-driven fraud is on the rise, so it's important to first be able to detect
synthetic voices
and set boundaries on what AI agents can do.
For most people, the phone line is where they meet their government. For too long, that has meant ineffective menus and hold music. It can be a conversation instead, in the caller's language, at any hour, on any phone. We're starting with the front door, and we don't plan to stop there.

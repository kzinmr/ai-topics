# Apple CEO John Ternus criticized for 'scripted' approach to first earnings call

Bloomberg’s
Dave Lee

Apple investors and fans hoping to get an insight into the philosophy of new Chief Executive Officer John Ternus on Monday were left sorely disappointed. The 51-year-old’s first earnings call was a timid affair, with Ternus taking cover behind a carefully written script as analysts peppered him with questions.
Lee acknowledged that the slump in pre-market trading will have been largely driven by Apple’s caution about rising expenses in the current quarter. The company said that memory costs were continuing to rise and that building out Siri AI data centers we’re going to add to the RAM bill. But he argued that Ternus missed an opportunity to talk up the company.
In front of Ternus on Monday night was a chance to counteract the gloom and doom by instilling in Wall Street the fundamentals of the Ternus Doctrine — what he sees Apple becoming under his tenure, the “product guy” taking the company to a new era of innovation. Instead, he spoke like a man glancing at the clock and wishing the minutes away.
Fortune’s
Sebastian Herrera
was less vocal but echoed some of the criticism.
Ternus fielded some questions but also delegated answers to Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh and Chief Operating Officer Sabih Khan, who Apple said would participate in earnings calls moving forward, something the company hasn’t done for years. Ternus projected confidence on the call, but he appeared to stick mostly to scripted marketing points.
9to5Mac’s Take
It will of course take Ternus some time to develop his own style when it comes to all of the varied aspects of the role, including addressing analysts in earnings calls, but I thought he took a measured but optimistic approach. Given the famously reticent style of his predecessor, the criticism strikes me as rather odd.

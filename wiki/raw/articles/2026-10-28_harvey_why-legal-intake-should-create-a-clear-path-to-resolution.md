Why Legal Intake Should Create a Clear Path to Resolution
The biggest delays in legal work often happen before review begins. Harvey’s Deputy General Counsel, Vineet Shahani, explains how a connected intake process can move legal work forward from the moment a request arrives.
by
Vineet Shahani
•
Oct 27, 2026
Requests to the legal team rarely arrive through one channel. A contract may come through email, a policy question through Slack, a marketing review through a design tool, and a product review may arrive by email with supporting documents in Google Drive. For the business, it can be difficult to know where to go for legal advice or who is the right lawyer for a particular request.
Once a request reaches the legal team, they may need to follow up for missing context and find the relevant policies or precedents before review can begin. The team then identifies the right reviewer, reassigning the request if priorities or capacity change. Even for recurring requests, they may have to repeat these steps from scratch.
A better intake model should move the request toward resolution from the moment it arrives. AI can support that process by helping gather the necessary context, applying the company’s legal standards, and directing each request to the appropriate member of the legal team, whether a contract manager, lawyer, or paralegal. Business teams get the guidance they need sooner, while lawyers spend less time coordinating and reviewing playbooks and precedents and more time applying their expertise.
What Slows Legal Requests Down
A request entering the legal team’s queue doesn’t necessarily mean it’s ready for review. A business stakeholder may know the outcome they need, but not the facts a lawyer needs to advise them. A customer’s marked-up MSA, for example, may arrive without the deal value, signing deadline, or context on what the parties have already discussed.
The legal team then has to fill in the gaps by following up with the requester, locating the relevant documents and internal guidance, and determining who should handle the review. Even assigning the request can require coordination. The usual reviewer may be unavailable or at capacity, leaving someone to identify an alternative and transfer the context.
By the time the request reaches the right person with the necessary information, days can pass. In the meantime, the business may move ahead or make commitments that leave the legal team with less room to advise. What appears to be a slow legal review is often just time spent preparing the request for legal review.
This administrative burden becomes harder to sustain as demand for legal support grows without a corresponding increase in resources. In RSGI’s
recent research
, 68% of in-house legal teams expect their headcount to remain flat over the next year. Yet 92% of respondents reported faster turnaround of legal work to the business after adopting Harvey. That result points to how AI can help legal teams respond to business needs more quickly within existing capacity.
Different Requests Need Different Paths
Reducing time-consuming administrative work starts with recognizing that legal requests are not interchangeable. A marketing asset review may require details about the audience, claims, and supporting evidence before the legal team returns marked-up copy. A product review may involve a PRD, rollout plans, and input from several specialists before the business receives guidance on whether it can launch. Each request requires different context, expertise, and approvals.
A fixed intake process struggles to account for those differences. If it asks too little, the legal team still has to gather the missing context. If it asks every possible question upfront, the process becomes burdensome and business users may avoid it. The
intake experience should adapt to the work
, gathering the information required for that request and directing it through the appropriate review process.
Design Intake Around how the Team Works
Adapting intake to the type of request is only part of the equation. Legal teams also need control over how that request is addressed and resolved. Their policies, areas of responsibility, risk thresholds, and approval requirements should determine which work can receive an automated first pass, which questions can be answered using approved guidance, and which requests need a lawyer’s attention from the beginning.
These decisions are specific to each organization. A team may allow an approved AI agent to answer common policy questions, require lawyer review for marketing claims in certain markets, or involve privacy counsel when a product uses particular categories of data. The intake process should apply these rules while accounting for who has the relevant expertise and capacity.
Many legal teams already have tools for collecting and assigning requests. What they often lack is a connection between that infrastructure and the knowledge base embedded in the team that can move work forward more efficiently. When the team’s policies, precedents, and review requirements are built into the contract review process, each request gets resolved more quickly at a higher quality.
Connect Intake to Resolution
When intake reflects the team’s routing and review requirements, a request doesn’t reach the legal team as an unstructured ask. It arrives with the relevant context, source material, and any initial work already connected.
A lawyer can verify the sources, revise the analysis or work product, and involve a specialist when needed, with the request’s context and materials already gathered. New documents or answers from the requester stay connected to the same request, so the team can continue reviewing without retracing earlier steps.
After the review, the response returns through the same channel the business team used to submit the request. The requester receives a clear answer or deliverable and can see where the work stands, while the legal team retains a record of the context, review, and final decision.
Strengthening Legal’s Role as a Business Partner
A connected intake process improves more than turnaround time. When teams spend less time chasing context, routing requests, and recreating familiar work, they can engage earlier in business decisions and devote greater attention to issues that require judgment.
In RSGI’s
research
, 88% of legal teams surveyed said they spent more time with the business on higher-value work after adopting Harvey. Nearly half (48%) expect legal departments to have a more influential strategic role within the next year.
Harvey’s vision is to help legal teams strengthen that role.
Intake
helps legal teams manage requests from submission through resolution, with AI agents taking on routine requests and lawyers focusing on work that calls for judgment. The business receives guidance sooner while the legal team gains visibility into demand and bottlenecks. The legal department’s standing also evolves as the function contributes to revenue, not only cost savings, and gains greater capacity to influence the decisions where it can have the most impact.

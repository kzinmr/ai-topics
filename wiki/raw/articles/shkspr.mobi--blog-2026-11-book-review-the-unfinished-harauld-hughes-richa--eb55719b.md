# Book Review: The Unfinished Harauld Hughes - Richard Ayoade

Source: https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2026/11/book-review-the-unfinished-harauld-hughes-richard-ayoade/

A decade ago, I read
Ayoade's first book
. In my review, I said:
It's hard to know how to assess a book like this. It's far too clever for its own good - a frustrating and humorous look at... what? It's neither autobiography nor comic novel. An anti-novel which trudges the reader through a series of comic vignettes. It reminded me of some of Woody Allen's work. If you get the joke - you'll find it hilarious. Everyone else will just look on in bemusement.
And, yeah, this new book is pretty much the same! It's an absurdist novel about a (semi) fictional TV presenter creating a documentary about a (probably) fictional director. Much like
Bob Mortimer's books
it revels in the mundane and delights in uniquely shaped language constructs.
As well as being wryly funny, it is mercilessly satirical. If you've ever read the risible scripts of an "influential" 1950s playwright, you'll immediately feel at home.
There's the drip-feeding of a tiny mystery - but it isn't quite enough to sustain the story. There's no urge to discover what
really
happened; you'll read this like it was a steam train documentary on in the background while you do the washing-up. There's sort-of a plot-twist, but it is of no consequence.
Despite it's caustic and cynical view about theatre, there are moments of genuine profundity. I found this especially beautiful:
It can sometimes cost ten times as much to sit near the front as compared to the cheap seats in the balcony. And that’s because it’s worth it. The experience is entirely changed by proximity. The tension alters.
What do we want from a celebrity documentary? Is it scandal? Hidden secrets and bitter jealousies? Do we want a behind-the-scenes glimpse at the making-of a documentary? This gets close to being a "Lost in La Mancha" in book form, for an author who didn't exist. It is an enjoyable read, but I think I'd've preferred to watch this late-night on BBC4 never quite knowing if it was a spoof or not.

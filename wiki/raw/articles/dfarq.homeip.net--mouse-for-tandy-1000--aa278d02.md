# The Silicon Underground

Source: https://dfarq.homeip.net/mouse-for-tandy-1000/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=mouse-for-tandy-1000

Strictly speaking, the proper mouse for the Tandy 1000, at least the early models, was the TRS-80 color mouse. But it’s not your only option and arguably isn’t your best option. In this blog post, I’ll explain some alternatives, including the one I use.
The TRS-80 color mouse
The TRS-80 Color Mouse was what Tandy intended for you to use with your Tandy 1000. But it’s not exactly the ideal mouse.
The TRS-80 color mouse, part number
26-3025
,  takes its name from the TRS-80 Color Computer. The mouse isn’t very colorful though. It came in jet black with a single red button. Maybe they should have called it the TRS-80 goth mouse. Its squared-off design is also about as ergonomic as it is colorful.
One could also argue it’s not really a proper mouse. It uses a pair of potentiometers, so it works more like an analog joystick than like a mouse. It’s also not compatible with other more conventional mice, so you will have to get Tandy’s driver in order to use it.
But since it plugs into the joystick port, it will work on any early Tandy 1000, even unexpanded models.
But I opted to go a different route, because the TRS-80 color mouse is hard to find and expensive.
Tandy/Radio Shack Serial mice
I use a Radio Shack 25-326 mouse, a relabeled Logitech, with my Tandy 1000. It’s a bit more modern but I think it still looks the part, and it’s comfortable to use and compatible.
But if you can equip your Tandy 1000 with an
RS-232 serial port
, you can use any 9-pin RS-232 serial mouse with it. A Microsoft Dove bar mouse is a reasonably good fit, period-correct, easy to find, and inexpensive. An early Logitech mouse would also be a good fit.
But what if you like things to match? You’re in luck. Radio Shack sold Tandy-branded mice. The most period-correct choice would be
catalog number 25-1040D
, but it’s also harder to find. Later, Radio Shack sold Tandy-branded Logitech mice, such as
catalog number 25-2001
. They look exactly like the Logitech mice that companies like Compaq and Dell supplied with their machines, but they have the Tandy logo on them. The going rate on these is around $25.
After Tandy left the computer business and Radio Shack started selling IBM and Compaq computers instead, they still continued to sell computer accessories. These often had Radio Shack branding instead of Tandy. A Radio Shack RS-232 mouse such as
catalog number 26-326
is reasonably inexpensive today, and looks the part next to a Tandy 1000, even though it’s from the wrong decade. Prices on these were over $40 during the pandemic but you can pick one up today for closer to $15.
The advantage with a later serial mouse is they will work with pretty much any mouse driver you want to use. Tandy 1000s tend to be a bit memory challenged, so being able to use ctmouse, which only needs 6K, is a nice advantage. The later Radio Shack branded mice are still relabeled Logitech units, with slightly updated styling in keeping for the time when it was produced.

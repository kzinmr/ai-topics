What is SIP trunking and how does it work?
SIP trunking is the process of connecting your business’s phone system to public phone networks using the internet, rather than using traditional copper phone lines.
SIP trunking uses Session Initiation Protocol to connect your internal Private Branch Exchange (PBX) system to the Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN). Then, you can send voice calls over this virtual connection. Some providers also support video calls and text messages.
The SIP trunking process starts with signaling, which involves setting up the process and connecting the caller with the correct destination. The signaling process may also involve redirecting a call or closing a call once it’s over.
Once the call has been set up, the data packets travel over a separate Real-time Transport Protocol, or RTP, not over the SIP itself. The data packets transmitted during a call are called ‘media’.
SIP vs. SIP trunk: What’s the difference?
Session Initiation Protocol, or SIP, is a signaling protocol that sets up, manages, and ends calls between two endpoints. In this case, the endpoints are your internal phone system and your SIP trunking provider, which connects you to the public phone network.
A SIP trunk is the specific virtual connection that facilitates the call. One SIP trunk can contain multiple channels and handle dozens or even hundreds of calls at the same time.
SIP trunking vs. VoIP: How the technologies work together
Voice over Internet Protocol, or VoIP, is a general term for any voice call that happens over the internet. SIP trunking is a specific VoIP method that connects your company’s internal PBX to the public phone network to facilitate calls.
SIP trunking benefits for growing businesses
SIP trunking offers a number of benefits for growing businesses, especially when they need to expand their phone systems to support a higher volume of calls. Here are some of the advantages of using SIP trunking for your phone systems.
Cost-effective
SIP trunking is generally cheaper than using traditional phone lines, especially at scale. That’s because SIP trunks run over the internet connection you’re already using, so you don’t need to pay to install and maintain separate phone lines.
With SIP trunking, you also typically avoid the high long-distance charges of traditional phone lines, because calls travel over the internet instead of separate circuits. International rates still apply and vary by provider, but they’re often lower than what physical lines charge, which makes SIP trunking a cost-effective option for organizations operating internationally.
Supports different types of calls
SIP trunking is primarily used for voice calls. Some providers also support video calls and business texting, and you can connect AI voice agents to your SIP trunk to handle customer service calls. This means you can use the same system to handle many different types of business communications.
Easy to scale
As your business grows, SIP trunking systems scale with you. You can add more channels to your SIP trunks quickly as your call volume grows.
To scale a traditional phone system, you’ll need to bring in a technician to install new physical lines, which is both time-consuming and expensive. SIP trunking is much more flexible.
Allows you to keep your existing phone numbers
You don’t have to change your business’s phone number when you switch from a physical phone connection to SIP trunking. This is particularly helpful if you already have a toll-free phone number that’s part of your brand identity.
Many SIP trunking providers allow you to keep your existing carrier and phone numbers when you make the switch. You can also use local number porting to keep your phone number while switching to a new carrier.
Low risk of disruptions
With SIP trunking, you can configure your phone calls to reroute to a backup system in the event of a power outage or other performance issue. This means you’re far less likely to miss an important call, and you’ll provide consistently reliable service for your customers.
Advanced security
Calls happening on a SIP trunk can be encrypted for privacy and security. Some SIP trunking providers also offer access control features, which allow you to limit who uses your business phone system and who they can call.
If a threat actor gets access to your phone systems, it could expose sensitive data or put your business at risk of a cyber attack. Using these security features helps reduce these risks.
What affects SIP trunking cost?
There are two primary factors that affect the cost of your SIP trunking system: the number of channels you have and the number of minutes you use each month. If you have an unlimited SIP trunking plan, you’ll be charged based on the number of channels you have. If you have a metered plan, you’ll be charged based on the number of minutes you use.
Metered plans tend to be more cost-effective for small businesses that only need a few channels. However, unlimited plans are more cost-effective for large or fast-growing businesses with many channels operating continuously.
Some SIP trunking providers also charge one-time fees for setup and number porting, or add-on fees for features like Direct Inward Dial (DID) numbers, toll-free numbers, business texting, call recording, or call rerouting. Providers may also charge higher fees for international calls, although they still tend to be much cheaper than long-distance calls on a physical phone line.
How to choose SIP trunking providers for reliability and scale
There are many SIP trunking providers on the market, and you’ll need to determine which one best fits your business’s needs. Here are key factors to consider when choosing a SIP trunking provider:
Uptime and reliability:
Look for a provider that offers consistently high uptime rates, so you don’t have to worry about calls dropping. Providers that own and maintain their own SIP trunking infrastructure often have more control over reliability.
Call quality:
Look for a SIP trunking provider who prioritizes good audio and video quality, as this is essential for customer-facing calls. Some SIP trunking providers offer demos or free trials to help you determine whether their services meet your standards.
Encryption support:
Your SIP trunking provider should use TLS and SRTP protocols to encrypt both the signaling and media transfer processes.
Call capacity:
Consider how many channels you’ll need for consistent high-volume service and whether your SIP trunking provider supports that at reasonable rates.
Geographic coverage:
Make sure your SIP trunking provider supports numbers in the countries and regions where your business operates.
Business continuity:
Look for failover and redundancy, such as automatic rerouting to a backup number or site if your primary connection fails, so a single outage doesn’t stop your calls.
Number porting:
Choose a provider that can port your existing phone numbers without disrupting your operations.
Cost-efficiency:
Instead of opting for the cheapest provider on the market, look for SIP trunking services that provide the best value for the price. Consider how costs will increase as your business scales and whether your budget supports it.

Frequently asked questions about SIP trunking
What’s the difference between SIP trunking and a traditional phone line?
SIP trunking uses the internet to make phone calls. It uses Session Initiation Protocol to connect your business’s phone system to public phone networks. A traditional phone line uses copper wires and circuit networks to make voice calls.
How many SIP trunk channels does a business need?
The number of channels you need depends on how many calls your team handles at the same time. High-volume call centers or sales teams may need even more channels. To calculate how many channels you’ll need, count the number of calls happening at the same time when your business is at peak capacity. You’ll need at least that many channels, plus a buffer for unexpected activity spikes.
Is SIP trunking secure?
SIP trunking is not inherently secure, but you can add security features to protect your systems and data. Notably, you can encrypt both the signaling and media transfer processes for an added layer of protection.
What’s the difference between SIP trunking and PRI or ISDN?
PRI is a legacy ISDN connection that delivers a fixed block of channels over physical copper lines, typically 23 channels on a T1 and 30 on an E1. SIP trunking carries calls over your internet connection, so you can add or remove channels in software instead of ordering new circuits. SIP trunking is typically cheaper and more flexible, while PRI uses dedicated bandwidth that doesn’t depend on your internet quality.
What’s the difference between SIP trunking and hosted PBX or UCaaS?
SIP trunking connects a phone system you manage, such as your PBX, to the public phone network. A hosted PBX or UCaaS platform is a phone system that a vendor runs for you in the cloud, usually with features like voicemail, conferencing, and messaging, and it often uses SIP trunking behind the scenes. Choose SIP trunking if you want to keep control of your phone system or connect AI voice agents to it. Choose hosted PBX or UCaaS if you’d rather have a vendor manage the system.
What do I need to set up SIP trunking?
You need a SIP-compatible PBX or phone system, a SIP trunking provider, and enough bandwidth, roughly 80 to 100 Kbps per simultaneous call with the G.711 codec. Your system should support a common audio codec such as G.711 (PCMU or PCMA) or G.722. A session border controller (SBC) sits at the edge of your network to secure and manage SIP traffic, and your firewall must allow SIP signaling (typically port 5060, or 5061 for TLS) and the port range your provider uses for media.

Bringing voice into Yahoo's evolution
In 1996, country musician Wylie Gustafson recorded a three-second audio clip that changed how the world viewed a search giant. The Yahoo yodel became forever tied to the legendary brand and helped Yahoo realize the power of voice. Now, more than 30 years later, as it reimagines search, Yahoo has returned to those roots, bringing voice capabilities to its new AI answer tool, Yahoo Scout.
The voice feature in the Yahoo Scout mobile app, built on the
ElevenAgents
platform, offers users the unique ability to not only have on-screen queries and responses in natural language, but also the chance to delve deeper into subjects through conversations instead of just keywords. Yahoo Scout started as a wholesale reimagining of what else search could be, but Yahoo knew it wanted to take the feature even further.
“What's really interesting is that over the last few years, user behaviors are changing in how people search,” says Eric Feng, Senior Vice President and General Manager of Yahoo Research Group."
User queries are changing to become more conversational, moving from keywords to full phrases and questions. Immediately we thought that the perfect interface to adapt to these shifts in Yahoo Scout was voice.
Eric Feng
Senior Vice President and General Manager, Yahoo Research Group
There’s more to voice than just the voice.
Creating a natural-sounding voice that could respond to any user query was no easy task. Tone, inflection, context, and other human qualities are difficult to replicate. And that’s why Yahoo Scout turned to ElevenLabs and their ElevenAgents platform.
We need to think differently about how the experience works and what makes a conversation feel natural.
Matt Linford
Senior Engineering Director at Yahoo
“It was really challenging to find a solution that could meet those needs, until we met ElevenLabs,” says Feng.
“Voice is a very different interaction pattern than text,” says Matt Linford, a Senior Engineering Director at Yahoo. “The way people use it is different. You might be pulling out your phone to ask a quick question, or you’re hands-free. We need to think differently about how the experience works and what makes a conversation feel natural.”
Creating a lifelike voice was the first hurdle, but there was also an additional challenge. How well could they recreate that iconic Yahoo yodel? “We evaluated a lot of the different voice providers and did a bake-off about who could actually yodel the best,” says Feng, “We were very excited to see that Eleven Labs passed with flying colors.”

Yahoo also judged ElevenLabs on their understanding of Yahoo’s scale and enterprise-level needs. Privacy and security were critical. Making the feature work at scale was key. And a short deadline meant quick integration into the existing Yahoo Scout platform was also a must-have.
Voice is changing how people use technology.
With voice now integrated into Yahoo Scout, users are quickly adapting to this new, conversational way of searching. Yahoo Scout is seeing that instead of typing in fragments of queries like “coffee shop nearby” or “best pie recipe,” people simply talk like they would to a human. Instead of one-off questions, people have conversations, hands free.
Quick timelines. Big results.
With just six weeks from kickoff to production, voice in Yahoo Scout launched to immediate results. Session length for voice conversations quickly increased, as did multi-turn depth, especially in the most popular search categories. The efficient production schedule is credited to the true partnership between ElevenLabs and Yahoo Scout, made possible by the forward deployed engineers who brought the vision into a working product.
The future of your brand is voice.
Yahoo has been a pioneer in the industry for decades and, now with ElevenLabs, its proving voice should not just be part of a brand’s strategy, but central to its mission. Consumers increasingly want more personal, more conversational interactions everywhere, and AI can make it a reality.
“Yahoo Scout has just
come out of beta
and expanded across more of the Yahoo portfolio, including being a powerful part of a completely rebuilt Yahoo Search,” Feng says. “After delivering more than 3 billion answers since launching earlier this year, Yahoo Scout is now more personalized, contextual, and has voice capabilities on mobile that offer our users a new way of searching and interacting with the product and the brand.”

We are really looking forward to building on that privilege, innovating for our users, and introducing them to all of this new exciting technology that is happening right now from AI, from voice, from multimodal, from personalization.”

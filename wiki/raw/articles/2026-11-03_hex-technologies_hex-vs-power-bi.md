Power BI is one of the most established ways to give an organization governed dashboards and reporting. It offers mature semantic modeling, familiar interfaces, and attractive pricing, particularly for companies already invested in Microsoft.
But the expectations people have for working with data are
changing
. Instead of searching through dashboards or asking analysts to build another report, employees increasingly expect to ask questions, investigate results, and create what they need with AI.
That's the bigger distinction between Power BI and
Hex
. Power BI is a strong choice for modernizing traditional BI workflows. Hex is an AI-native analytics platform built to make the entire company capable of working with data, while giving data teams the context, governance, and observability to maintain trust.
The decision isn't simply which product has more features.
Are you looking to improve how your company consumes reports, or fundamentally expand who can ask, explore, and build with data?
TL;DR: Hex vs. Power BI
Choose Hex if:
You want anyone across the business to ask questions, explore data, and build dashboards or apps in natural language. Hex combines conversational self-service, advanced analysis, and AI-powered building with shared business context and observability, so data teams can support AI adoption at scale.
Choose Power BI if:
Your priority is cost-effective, governed reporting and dashboards, particularly if your organization already uses Microsoft 365, Excel, Teams, and Fabric. Power BI offers mature modeling and visualization capabilities, with Copilot adding AI assistance to established workflows.
Both support AI and business intelligence. The difference is whether you're primarily optimizing traditional reporting or building toward a world where far more people work with data independently.
Hex vs. Power BI at a glance
What each platform is built for
Power BI: Mature reporting inside the Microsoft ecosystem
Microsoft Power BI
is built around creating, distributing, and governing reports and dashboards. Data teams use semantic models, DAX, Power Query, and established permissions to deliver consistent metrics to business stakeholders.
It's especially compelling for organizations already operating in Microsoft 365. Reports integrate with Teams, Excel, PowerPoint, and other tools employees know, reducing the friction of rolling out traditional BI.
Power BI has also invested in AI.
Copilot
can summarize reports, answer certain questions about governed data, and help authors create report pages. Fabric adds broader data and agent capabilities.
Hex: AI-native analytics for the entire organization
Hex starts from a different goal: making it possible for anyone to work with data, not just consume reports prepared by someone else.
With
Threads
, employees can ask questions and investigate follow-ups conversationally. When a question requires more sophisticated analysis, the Hex Agent can go deeper. And
Generative Apps
lets people create customizable dashboards and applications through natural language.
Data teams can still inspect and refine the underlying work. They also curate the business context agents use and monitor answer quality through
Context Studio
.
Hex extends those capabilities beyond its own interface through
MCP
,
Slack
, and the
Hex CLI
, so people can work with trusted company data inside other AI tools.
The result is a platform designed around the full journey from a question to deeper exploration and reusable analytical work—not just the delivery of a finished report.
Three key differences between Hex and Power BI
1. Conversational self-service: What happens beyond the dashboard?
Power BI makes it easy to distribute governed reports and dashboards across an organization. And with Copilot, users can ask questions and explore data in natural language. But that experience still largely revolves around reports and semantic models the data team has prepared in advance.
The challenge is what happens when someone needs to go beyond those predefined experiences. Increasingly, people aren't waiting for the data team to answer their next question. They're exporting data into Claude or ChatGPT and doing the analysis themselves.
That creates a new problem: companies invest heavily in governed BI, only for analytical work to happen outside it, with little visibility into the questions being asked, the business logic being applied, or whether the answers are accurate.
Hex is built to close that gap. With Threads, anyone can ask questions, investigate follow-ups, and explore data in natural language, without being limited to existing dashboards or predefined semantic models. And through Slack and MCP, employees can access those same governed analytical capabilities from tools like Claude and Cursor.
Rather than forcing people to choose between the flexibility of AI and the trust of governed BI, Hex brings both together. Data teams can curate the context agents rely on and maintain visibility into the analysis happening across supported workflows.
At
Parallel Web Systems
, self-service analytics adoption increased from roughly 5% to 75% of the organization. Employees now access trusted answers through Hex, including from Slack and other agents, instead of relying on a single analyst to build most dashboards.
The difference:
Power BI helps organizations distribute trusted reporting and add AI to existing BI workflows. Hex makes open-ended self-service accessible across the company, without pushing people into disconnected, ungoverned AI tools.
2. AI-powered analysis and building: What happens after the answer?
An answer is rarely the end of an analytical workflow. People want to ask follow-up questions, test hypotheses, share findings, and turn useful analysis into something colleagues can use again.
Power BI has credible AI-powered authoring capabilities. Copilot can help create report pages and visuals, and Fabric provides additional tools for notebooks, data engineering, and advanced analysis.
But those experiences often span different interfaces. A business user asking a question may still need someone familiar with report authoring or Fabric to develop the next stage of the work.
Hex brings conversational exploration, advanced analysis, and AI-powered building together. Users can move from asking a question in Threads to investigating the underlying data and creating an interactive dashboard or app.
With Generative Apps, teams can describe the experience they need in natural language, including custom visualizations and controls, rather than being limited to predetermined report layouts. Data teams can inspect and refine the underlying logic.
This matters as organizations move beyond dashboards. A customer success team might need an app that generates account-specific business reviews. A finance team might need an interactive planning tool that lets stakeholders change assumptions and immediately see the results.
At
EliseAI
, an app reduced quarterly business review preparation from two days to five minutes. Its teams also use Threads for one-off questions that previously required a spreadsheet, query, or new dashboard. Today, 91% of employees access data through Hex across 14 teams.
The difference:
Power BI provides powerful report authoring and an expanding Microsoft AI ecosystem. Hex makes it easier to move from a question to deeper analysis and a reusable, custom analytical experience without handing the workflow back to a technical specialist.
3. Trust at scale: Can the data team govern AI answers as usage grows?
When only a handful of analysts generate reports, the data team can review much of what gets published. When hundreds of employees ask agents questions independently, manually inspecting every answer becomes unrealistic.
Both platforms take trust seriously, but their approaches reflect different product foundations.
Power BI has mature security, semantic modeling, and governance. Teams can define approved metrics and relationships, manage access, and prepare semantic models for Copilot with descriptions, instructions, and verified answers. Microsoft also provides auditing and evaluation capabilities across its broader Fabric ecosystem.
Hex also supports governed semantic definitions, but isn't limited to context authored inside one BI model. Agents can draw on endorsed warehouse tables, metadata, Guides, existing analytical work, and semantic models—including definitions synced from tools like dbt MetricFlow, Cube, and Snowflake.
Then Context Studio helps data teams observe the questions people actually ask, identify where context is missing or ambiguous, evaluate responses, and improve that context over time.
It's also important outside the BI interface. Employees increasingly work with data in Claude, Cursor, and Slack, potentially creating fragmented analyses based on inconsistent definitions. Hex's MCP and other integrations let supported external workflows draw on the same governed analytical capabilities and context, with visibility into Hex Agent interactions.
Parallel saw the value of that approach firsthand. Before Hex, employees were generating queries with Claude and Codex, but getting consistent answers was difficult because people used different definitions and processes. Hex gave them a common source of trusted answers accessible through Slack and MCP.
The difference:
Power BI provides strong governance for its reporting and Microsoft data ecosystem. Hex combines open business context, answer-quality observability, and external agent access to help data teams manage trusted AI analytics across the organization.
What customers are achieving beyond traditional BI
The value of AI-native analytics is easier to understand in outcomes than in feature lists.
At Parallel, the shift wasn't simply faster dashboard creation. Most employees gained a direct way to investigate data, and customer-facing teams began receiving proactive alerts about account activity through automated workflows. The data team could focus on improving shared context rather than fulfilling every request.
At EliseAI, the combination of conversational self-service and reusable apps changed both everyday analysis and operational reporting. Employees can ask one-off questions themselves, while customer-facing teams generate tailored business-review materials in minutes rather than days.
Pricing: Where Power BI has an advantage
Power BI's pricing is a compelling reason to consider it.
Power BI Pro
is listed at $14 per user per month, with Premium Per User at $24. Pro is also included in some Microsoft 365 enterprise licenses, which can make incremental reporting costs attractive.
Hex pricing
starts with a free Community plan, Professional at $36 per Editor per month, and Team at $75 per Editor per month. Enterprise offers custom pricing and additional governance features and Explorer access.
Comparing editor seat prices alone misses the difference in what you're buying. If the goal is to deliver dashboards to a large audience at minimal cost, Power BI may be the more economical choice. If the goal is to let that audience independently investigate and build with data, evaluate the productivity impact, access model, and ongoing support costs as well.
Which platform should you choose?
Choose Power BI
if your organization primarily needs well-governed dashboards, recurring reporting, and familiar self-service through established semantic models. It's especially attractive if you're standardized on Microsoft and want to modernize existing reporting without changing the overall workflow.
Choose Hex
if your priority is to make company-wide AI analytics work: people asking questions and creating what they need in natural language, while data teams retain the ability to inspect analysis, maintain trusted context, and monitor answers as adoption grows.
You also don't have to replace Power BI to start using Hex. Some organizations may retain established Power BI reports while introducing Hex for conversational exploration, advanced analysis, and AI-powered applications. Existing DAX models don't automatically become Hex semantic models, so teams should plan how to maintain consistent definitions across both environments.
The bottom line
Power BI remains a compelling platform for governed reporting, especially for organizations already committed to Microsoft's ecosystem. Its pricing, semantic modeling, and established workflows make it a sensible choice for many traditional BI needs.
Hex is built for a different ambition: making data accessible to everyone through AI, not just making existing reporting workflows more efficient.
The choice comes down to what you're trying to change.

Frequently asked questions
Can Hex and Power BI work together?
Yes. Teams can continue using Power BI for established reports while adopting Hex for conversational analytics, advanced analysis, and new applications. Plan how metric definitions and access policies will remain consistent across both platforms, particularly if your existing logic lives in DAX.
Does Power BI have conversational AI?
Yes. Power BI Copilot can answer questions about supported semantic models, summarize insights, and help create reports. The question is how much open-ended exploration and building business users can complete independently within those workflows.
Can Hex support the governance enterprises expect from BI?
Hex supports data permissions, semantic modeling, and enterprise security capabilities. Context Studio also helps data teams observe AI questions, evaluate responses, and improve the context agents rely on. Available controls depend on the plan and integration configuration.
Is Hex more expensive than Power BI?
Power BI has lower published per-user prices, especially for companies already invested in Microsoft. But the total cost of analytics goes beyond BI licenses. Hex brings conversational self-service, advanced analysis, and AI-powered dashboards and apps into one platform, reducing the need for separate tools. And by grounding agents in trusted business context, Hex can also reduce wasted AI work and token consumption from repeated attempts to get the right answer.

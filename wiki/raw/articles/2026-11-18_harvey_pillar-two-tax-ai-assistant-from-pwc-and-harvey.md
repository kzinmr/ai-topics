Inside the Pillar Two Tax AI Assistant From PwC and Harvey
Research the OECD rules and local Pillar Two legislation in one place, with a tool already used by thousands of PwC professionals.
by
Harvey Team
•
Nov 17, 2026
More than
8,000 multinational groups
are in scope of Pillar Two, the OECD framework that sets a 15% global minimum effective tax rate for groups with consolidated revenue of €750 million or more. According to the OECD,
45 jurisdictions
have already taken steps to introduce the Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) rules. The framework is global, but implementation is local. Those local differences decide what a group files, when it files, and how much top-up tax it owes.
For tax teams, two challenges stand out. The first is
data
. GloBE calculations generally start from each entity's financial accounts, as prepared for the parent's consolidated statements, and then require jurisdiction-specific adjustments. Some domestic regimes may substitute consolidated statements with local accounts. Working out exactly what data each country's rules require, and whether a group's data supports it, is often the hardest part. The second is
interpretation
. The OECD rules don't settle every local nuance, so teams must read the global rulebook alongside each country's legislation.
PwC and Harvey built the
Pillar Two Tax AI Assistant
, a specialist research tool within Harvey, to address both challenges.
What the Assistant Covers and How it Works
The Pillar Two Tax AI Assistant’s research capabilities help teams apply the global rules to local law. It covers the OECD Pillar Two rules alongside country-specific material supplied by PwC network firms, and it currently spans 21 countries.
The assistant draws only on vetted public sources: enacted and draft local legislation, tax authority guidance, and other official publications such as government consultations. Online sources are refreshed weekly, new documents are added monthly, and the content is reviewed quarterly.
Users select it as a source in Harvey, filter by the countries they need, and use it alongside Harvey's other platform features. It can also power custom agents that produce dashboards to help teams test and reconcile the underlying data. The custom Harvey Agent, described below, is one example.
What the Assistant Does for Tax Teams
Pillar Two is complex, varies by country, and constantly changes. The assistant gives tax teams a single, trusted place to work through it.
Faster cross-border research.
Users no longer need to search legislation and guidance country by country. They can work through filing obligations, deadlines, and country-specific requirements in one place. "The module significantly improves the efficiency of my day-to-day Pillar Two work by helping me quickly navigate filing obligations, deadlines, and jurisdiction-specific requirements," said a PwC Senior Associate, International Tax, Japan.
Side-by-side country comparisons.
Users can see how countries have implemented the rules and spot differences early. "The module provides quick, well-structured overviews and access to relevant guidance and legislation, making country-specific research more efficient and effective," noted a PwC Manager, International Tax, Germany.
Analysis of a group's own facts.
Users can upload a group's facts to see what data the rules require. They can also explore scenarios that might change the top-up tax payable, or test whether simplifications or safe harbours could apply.
Research through to deliverable.
The assistant drafts memos on the rules, on elements of the effective tax rate calculation, and on safe harbours and exclusions. It also helps users check their analysis and prepare reports and emails.
Cited, verifiable answers.
Answers include citations that link directly to the underlying legislation and guidance, so users can check the work quickly.
Safe Harbour Testing With a Custom Harvey Agent
To show what teams can build on top of the Pillar Two Tax AI Assistant’s knowledge, we created a custom Harvey Agent that turns that knowledge into an analytical dashboard. The agent tests each jurisdiction in a group against the FY2025 transitional CbCR safe harbours: the de minimis test, the simplified ETR test, and the routine profits test. It normalizes and reconciles the underlying data and flags any breaks.
Where a jurisdiction fails all three tests, the agent calculates an indicative top-up tax and shows where it would be collected, either locally or at the parent. It also lists open questions by priority, links each result to its source data, and compares results across reporting perimeters.
The output is an Excel workbook showing the ETR working with live formulas, along with a self-contained interactive HTML view of the results. This example shows what happens when PwC’s Pillar Two expertise meets Harvey’s ability to let teams build custom agents tailored to how their practice actually works.
Proven at Scale Within PwC
The assistant is PwC's go-to tool for Pillar Two technical research. It has been rolled out to more than 17,000 PwC users across 21 countries and has recorded more than 70,000 user activities across more than 24,000 conversation threads. Monthly use
grew roughly 17x
, from about 700 activities in October 2025 to more than 12,000 in September 2026.
PwC has also rolled it out to clients, with positive feedback. "We have had a very positive experience with the module and would certainly recommend it to your other clients looking for additional support and guidance on Pillar 2," said a Head of Tax of a multi-national group.

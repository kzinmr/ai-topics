# AI Agents in Healthcare: 4 Lessons from HLTH 2026

Source: https://sierra.ai/blog/ai-agents-healthcare-hlth-2026

Think about a good healthcare experience you’ve had. Your doctor referred you to a specialist, the specialist’s office called to book your appointment, and you knew what to expect. But all too often, a week goes by and you still haven’t heard anything. The specialist says they never got the referral and your doctor’s office says they sent it. So you become the project manager, calling around to figure out what went wrong.
The beauty of agents is that they take that burden off the patient without adding work for payer and provider staff. An agent can track down the referral, follow up with the insurer if approval is needed, and keep you posted until you’re booked.
There was an agentic something at almost every booth at HLTH this year. It’s relatively easy to spin up a demo, but launching an agent at scale is harder. At HLTH 2026, we talked with Blue Cross Blue Shield Association (BCBSA) and Cigna on what it takes to run
AI agents for healthcare
at the scale of millions of members.
Our biggest takeaways:
1. Speed comes from partnership
In just 53 days, Blue Cross Blue Shield Association launched an AI agent for the Federal Employee Program, which serves nearly 6 million people (employees, retirees, and their families) across 31 BCBS Plans. The agent took 400 calls on day one in June. Five months later, it had resolved over 105,000 calls with a 4.5 out of 5 satisfaction score.
Blue Cross Blue Shield Association (BCBSA) credits its partnership with Sierra for how quickly it launched its Federal Employee Program AI agent. Blue Cross Blue Shield Association’s COO, Sean Robbins, said one team from across the association worked directly with local BCBS Plans and Sierra.
“It was a partnership from day one of being in the trenches together,” Robbins said. His advice: before you automate a workflow, ask whether it should exist at all. “If you just apply AI to the same old workflows, you’re just accelerating, in many cases, broken workflows.”
2. Capabilities go beyond the call
Eighteen months ago, an agent’s work ended when the call did. Now it can own a task for weeks, across every channel, and take action along the way.
That makes proactive care possible. An agent can call a patient overdue for a mammogram, find an opening at a nearby clinic, book it, and follow up by text until she’s seen. If she’s busy, she can ask it to call back. “You can put the agent on hold instead of the other way around,” said Clay Bavor, Sierra co-founder. We see about 50 care-gap use cases like this, from screenings to check-ins after a procedure.
The same agent can explain a $340 balance using the patient’s health record, set up a payment plan, and take payment through a processor like Plaid, all in one conversation.
It all runs on direct EHR integrations, so agents can book and reschedule appointments, pull context to explain a bill, and call payers to check on a prior authorization.
3. Planning for personal agents
Payers and providers have spent years building a single digital front door. Will Rogers, Head of AI at Cigna, expects members to increasingly “walk in” through their own personal agents, and most people to have one within two years. “There’s not going to be one front door,” Will said. “There’s going to be many doors.”
That’s why we built
Personal Agent Protocol
, or Poppy: an open standard for how personal agents work with companies, now with more than 100 design partners, including Cigna. Agents are already talking to agents, too. Provider agents call Cigna’s agents today, and we’re building a direct connection between them.
4. Trust is the real work
Every session came back to trust, and in healthcare, that starts with safety. An agent has to recognize a crisis or an emergency in whatever words a patient uses, and know where its job ends: agents take on the administrative work, and clinical decisions stay with clinicians. Drawing that line, and testing it before launch, is what earns an agent the right to serve patients. To lead this work, we’ve hired Dr. Mitesh Patel, formerly the Chief Clinical Transformation Officer at Ascension, as our Head of Clinical.
Trust also means control over data. Many personal agents log in with the user’s own password. Poppy instead gives each agent its own identity and only the access a person grants: in Will’s words, “a visitor badge,” not a master key.
What comes next
“We have less of a technology gap today than we have a process change gap,” said Clay Bavor at HLTH. What’s left is changing how the work gets done. That’s also why we price on outcomes: an appointment booked, a prior authorization approved, a bill paid. This keeps everyone’s interests aligned and Sierra focused on delivering measurable business impact for your company.
Our goal is a named advisor for every patient. That’s where we’ll spend our time.

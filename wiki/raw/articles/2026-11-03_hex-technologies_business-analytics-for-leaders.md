This roundup compares seven business intelligence (BI) tools on leader autonomy and trustworthiness, then shows how to test finalists on one of your own key performance indicators (KPIs).
Leaders are investigating now instead of just consuming
Leaders can now follow up on a number themselves instead of waiting for someone else to run it down. Reports, dashboards and low-code
self-service analytics
have long helped business users consume data, but
generative AI in analytics
lets them ask the next question in plain language instead, the same shift reshaping the broader
AI Analytics Platform
category.
In practice, those established formats were not originally designed for a chain of follow-up questions. A VP sees pipeline dip, the dashboard shows the drop, and “which segment drove it?” goes into an analyst's Slack queue. We judge each tool on autonomy and
trusting AI analytics
.
Autonomy.
Can you start at a KPI, ask why it moved, follow up without learning an authoring workflow, and save the answer for reuse?
Trustworthiness.
Does
data governance
ground the answer, and can someone see how the tool produced it? Trust is the top concern with AI on their data for 31% of respondents in Hex's 2026
State of Data Teams
report, cited nearly twice as often as any other concern.
Comparison at a glance
All seven tools below answer questions in plain language, but they differ sharply in pricing shape and in how much of the underlying work they'll actually show you. As of October 6, 2026, each is active under the name shown here, and recent rebrands don't change that: Tableau Agent was formerly Einstein Copilot for Tableau, ThoughtSpot folded its
acquired Mode platform into Analyst Studio
, and Google
renamed Looker Studio to Data Studio
in April 2026 while the separate Looker platform kept its name. Qlik
acquired Upsolver
in January 2025 without an ownership change to Qlik itself. Show-your-work mechanisms may expose Structured Query Language (SQL), filters, sources or project logic.
We left Domo off: we found no documented path from a KPI to drivers to a saved card for non-technical users, and Progress
acquired Domo's assets
on September 22, 2026. The Domo platform remains active under Progress; the former Domo, Inc. corporate entity changed its name to
Huckleberry.ai

The best business analytics tools for leaders
Hex
Hex is an AI-native BI platform built around asking, building and improving answers together: business and technical teams work from the same project. Its
AI capabilities
work alongside
BI in Hex
, and one project keeps the follow-up, saved app and record of how Hex produced each answer. Your data team can open, fix or build on that work.
Key features
Three pieces cover the path from a question to a governed answer:
Threads redirects you to an existing app when a question has already been answered, instead of starting over.
Explorers can chat with a published app to ask follow-ups without leaving it.
Context Studio shows where to invest governance effort next, turning each published app into context the next question can build on.
MCPs with Hex
let supported external assistants start or continue governed work in Hex.
Nothing here lives in a separate tool: a question, the app it produces, and the record of how the data team shaped it all sit in one project.
Pros
Nobody has to give up inspectability to get speed.
Rated 4.5/5 on G2
, and each answer is traceable to the project behind it.
Threads, the app it produces and the underlying project all live in one place, so nothing hands off to a disconnected tool.
Slack chats start Threads you can continue in the platform, and the agent reads the whole Slack thread.
Business users move quickly without hiding the underlying analytical work from the data team, which is the combination most of this list struggles to offer at once.
Cons
A lot of this is still new.
Threads launched in public beta and the MCP server is labeled beta, so expect some features to change.
The Explorer seat, which lets non-authors ask questions in Threads, is a paid seat on Team and Enterprise only, as are the Slack agent and Context Studio. Editors can create Threads on any paid plan.
Your team needs an Admin or Manager to use Context Studio and fix context gaps.
Confirm the seats, roles and beta features line up with your rollout plan before counting on them.
Pricing
Hex prices by Editor seat, with the agent included rather than billed separately.
Community is free, with an agent trial.
Professional is $36 per Editor per month, with up to five published apps.
Team is $75 per Editor per month, with a 14-day trial, unlimited apps, drill-down, scheduled runs and metric alerts.
Hex Enterprise
is custom, with added security and compliance controls.
Confirm this against the live pricing page before publishing, since Editor pricing and included features shift tier to tier.
Who is Hex best for?
Hex fits teams whose questions outpace the data team, the way
Clay's
Enterprise Sales Leader Ashley Artrip built her own sales dashboards with Hex's agent and then ran VP-level follow-ups through Threads without a ticket to the data team. If your own KPI can follow that same path from question to inspection, this is the one to test first.
ThoughtSpot
ThoughtSpot
is a platform that gives teams conversational search on governed data through the Spotter agent, which plans, validates assumptions and self-corrects. That search-first design goes back further than Spotter itself: ThoughtSpot's 2023 acquisition of Mode, now Analyst Studio, added a code-first workspace alongside it. It's
rated 4.3/5 on G2
and
4.5 on Gartner
Peer Insights.
Key features
Spotter is built to keep a question moving rather than restart it:
Spotter plans multi-step queries and validates its own assumptions before answering.
Questions on a Liveboard KPI pin their answers back to that Liveboard.
Relational search tokens let nontechnical users verify each query.
That planning-and-verification loop is what separates Spotter from a plain search box over a dashboard.
Pros
That continuity is what gives leaders autonomy past their desk.
You can drill into any AI-generated answer instead of starting a new question.
Native iOS and Android apps bring the same search to your phone.
The same conversation carries forward across a follow-up, rather than resetting each time.
Conversational continuity plus mobile access make Spotter one of the more accessible conversational options among traditional BI products.
Cons
Two of the three limits trace back to Slack and a monthly query cap.
Spotter can decline to chart a result for some queries, returning a text or table answer instead.
In Slack, you can follow up only on a Spotter conversation you started.
Pro per-user plans include 25 Spotter queries per user per month.
Check those limits against the people who'll actually ask the most follow-up questions.
Pricing
ThoughtSpot's three tiers split mainly on whether Spotter is included and how much of it you get.
Essentials starts at $25 per user per month billed annually, without Spotter.
Pro is $50 per user per month billed annually, with Spotter included and capped at 25 queries per user per month.
Enterprise is custom, with Spotter usage effectively unmetered.
ThoughtSpot also lists a separate Unlimited Spotter add-on for teams that outgrow Pro's cap without moving to Enterprise.
Verify current tier names and the Spotter allowance against ThoughtSpot's own pricing page before publishing, since this is an area vendors revise often.
Who is ThoughtSpot best for?
ThoughtSpot suits teams whose data group already maintains governed models and coaches Spotter. In our reading, its path from a Liveboard KPI to a pinned answer gives you the most autonomy of the traditional BI tools here, and trust typically holds while that model stays current.
Power BI
Power BI
typically fits Microsoft-standardized teams because Copilot can provide a short path from an existing Excel or Teams workflow to a report follow-up. That path used to run through a separate Q&A feature, which Copilot is replacing outright, with Q&A set for deprecation in December 2026. Power BI is
rated 4.5/5 on G2
.
Key features
Copilot's features stay close to the report you're currently viewing:
Copilot's “Explore answer” saves an exploration to a workspace.
Verified answers (preview) let the data team attach approved responses to common phrasings.
Copilot answers Power BI data questions inside Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, not just the Power BI pane.
None of this reaches past the report and semantic model you started from.
Pros
For Microsoft-standardized teams, the path to an answer is already paved.
Copilot and Power BI run inside the same Excel and Teams workflows most of these teams use daily anyway.
Power BI continues to enforce row- and column-level security.
Admins managing Microsoft 365 identity and security can roll Power BI out without standing up a separate stack.
For Microsoft-centered teams, that overlap can cut the number of new interfaces users must learn.
Cons
Which seats and which questions get Copilot is where this gets restrictive.
Pro and Premium Per User seats don't include Copilot.
Copilot supports defined report questions rather than every analytical task; Microsoft lists “Why do our sales go down every July?” and forecasts as unsupported.
Data questions are English only, and current slicers don't apply to Copilot pane answers.
Run the exact “why” questions leaders expect to ask through a trial, not just report summaries.
Pricing
Power BI's seat cost and its AI cost are billed on two separate tracks.
Pro is $14 per user per month, billed yearly, and is included free with Microsoft 365 E5.
Premium Per User is $24 per user per month, billed yearly.
Copilot itself isn't a seat add-on: it runs on Fabric capacity, which starts at $262.80 per month (F2) and is bought separately by IT.
Verify current pricing and Fabric capacity tiers against Microsoft's own pricing page before publishing.
Who is Power BI best for?
Power BI fits Microsoft-standardized teams who can work through familiar Excel and Teams interfaces, especially if Microsoft 365 E5 already covers the Pro seat. Autonomy stays inside existing reports and their semantic model, since Copilot answers the questions a report already holds rather than open-ended "why" questions.
Tableau
Tableau
is Salesforce's BI platform for building reports and visualizations, with AI added through two separate products: Pulse for metric monitoring and Tableau Agent for conversation. Its AI push now runs through Salesforce's broader Agentforce stack rather than a tool Tableau built alone. Tableau is rated
4.4 on Gartner
Peer Insights, with 82% willing to recommend.
Key features
Pulse is built to push a metric change to you, then let you keep talking about it:
Pulse digests carry the top-ranked insight per metric (Top Drivers is one insight type) to email, Slack and Teams.
Agent in Pulse lets you change dimensions, filter or compare members in conversation.
The documentation we reviewed doesn't describe saving an Agent answer as a reusable view.
That last gap matters: this fits recurring monitoring better than a one-off exploration you'd want to save and revisit.
Pros
Tableau's strength is pushing metric changes to leaders rather than waiting for them to go looking.
Agent in Pulse answers link to metric sources.
Pulse surfaces an automated, ranked insight per metric without anyone building that view by hand.
Pulse is included in every Cloud edition, which also offers a 60-day trial to test Agent.
Governed metrics, flexible delivery and an established visualization experience make this a practical fit for teams standardized on Tableau.
Cons
Agent's reach stops at the metrics and infrastructure Tableau already governs.
Agent in Pulse was designed around governed Pulse metrics, so it covers only those metrics; Tableau's help page also says it may sometimes hallucinate on complex queries.
Performance depends heavily on extract size and refresh strategy on large datasets.
Tableau Agent needs a Salesforce org with Einstein generative AI.
Check which metrics, licenses and Salesforce dependencies your workflow actually needs.
Pricing
Tableau prices by role, and Agent sits on top of that as a separate, higher tier.
Cloud Standard: $75 for Creator, $42 for Explorer, $15 for Viewer per user per month, billed annually.
Cloud Enterprise: $115 for Creator, $70 for Explorer, $35 for Viewer per user per month, billed annually.
Tableau Agent requires Cloud+ or Tableau+, on top of one of the above.
Tableau Next and the Tableau+ bundle aren't publicly priced; Salesforce quotes both through sales.
Verify current role pricing and which bundle unlocks Agent against Tableau's own pricing page before publishing.
Who is Tableau best for?
Tableau fits Salesforce-standardized teams whose leaders want metric digests with drivers attached. Investigation from there stays within whatever metrics Pulse already governs.
Sigma
Sigma
is a platform teams use to query their warehouse directly through in-browser workbooks, positioned closer to a spreadsheet than a traditional BI tool. It's one of the newer entrants on this list, built warehouse-native from the start rather than retrofitting AI onto an older architecture, and in April 2026 it upgraded Ask Sigma into Sigma Assistant. It's
rated 4.4/5 on G2
.
Key features
Sigma's AI sits on top of a spreadsheet-style workbook rather than replacing it:
Assistant identifies metric drivers and detects anomalies in a workbook.
An MCP server lets other AI assistants query Sigma directly.
Assistant builds or edits dashboards from natural language.
The workbook stays the unit of work; the AI just adds to what you'd already build there by hand.
Pros
Sigma's case is inspectability: nothing here hides behind a layer of AI.
Rated 4.8 on Gartner
Peer Insights, the highest here.
“View SQL” and “Analysis breakdown” let you check the exact query behind an answer.
Workbooks stay editable by business users, not locked to whoever built the first version.
That combination suits teams that want business-friendly workbooks without losing sight of the underlying query.
Cons
Delivery and save permissions are the two gaps worth knowing about.
Sigma documents neither a Slack-based Assistant nor a native mobile app.
To save a finding to a workbook, you need the “Create, edit, and publish workbooks” permission.
Match the permission model against the leaders you'd actually expect to preserve and share findings.
Pricing
Sigma is sales-led rather than self-serve on price.
No list prices are published; buyers go through an account executive.
A 14-day trial is available before you talk to sales.
Viewer licensing details aren't publicly listed either.
Get an actual quote before comparing Sigma's total cost to the tools here that do publish rates.
Who is Sigma best for?
Sigma suits finance and operations teams with a well-modeled warehouse and members who can publish workbooks. Those publish rights let you save Assistant's driver findings as a workbook view, while View SQL often makes inspectability its strongest trust feature.
Looker
Looker
is a platform teams use to define governed enterprise metrics and relationships in LookML, the oldest and most code-first modeling layer of the tools compared here. Google acquired Looker in 2020 and folded it into Google Cloud, which is also why its AI runs on Gemini rather than a model Looker built itself. Conversational Analytics is generally available (GA), and dashboard agents are in preview.
Key features
Conversational Analytics works by sitting on top of the LookML model, not around it:
Conversational Analytics inherits the LookML model's joins, filters and permissions.
You can save conversations and build dashboards from a prompt.
Gemini key driver analysis can also be added to alerts (rolling out).
Every one of these depends on that LookML model already existing.
Pros
Every answer here traces back to the same governed model, wherever you ask it from.
With Advanced Analytics, you can generate and run Python; available sources conflict on whether the Python capability is generally available or remains in Preview.
“How was this calculated?” shows you the fields, filters and sort order behind each answer.
The same governed model backs every surface, so a conversational answer and a scheduled dashboard never disagree on a definition.
Model consistency, Python support and answer inspection suit teams willing to invest in LookML as the governing source of truth.
Cons
The costs show up as build effort now and usage charges later.
Because answers depend on LookML, a developer has to build and maintain that model before Conversational Analytics has anything governed to draw on.
Separately, you can use the documented Slack integration for sharing and scheduling, but not conversational querying.
After the promotional period, Conversational Analytics overages will cost $3 per million input and $20 per million output tokens.
Price in both the LookML build effort and the usage charges that follow it.
Pricing
Looker is quote-based across the board, with the AI usage cost arriving later as a separate line.
Every edition (Standard, Enterprise, Embed) charges a platform fee plus user pricing, listed as “Annual commitment | Call sales.”
Each edition includes 10 standard and two developer users before additional seats cost extra.
Conversational Analytics is free to use during a promotional period, then moves to token billing.
Get a current quote and ask specifically when your promotional token window ends before publishing a total-cost comparison.
Who is Looker best for?
Looker fits Google Cloud teams with mature LookML. Trust is typically highest here because every answer runs through definitions a developer modeled, but your autonomy ends where the model does: a metric nobody has modeled waits until someone builds it.
Qlik
Qlik
is a platform teams use to work with AI agents across their analytics apps, and its MCP server is GA. Qlik has spent the past two years pushing beyond classic associative BI through acquisitions like Upsolver, folding that expansion into its own agent layer rather than shipping one single new chat product. Qlik Sense is
rated 4.4/5 on G2
.
Key features
Qlik spreads its agents across the apps and documents you already have, rather than one central chat:
You chat with agents across Qlik apps and documents.
Add to sheet places generated charts on a new or existing sheet.
Qlik Answers respects Section Access, so a question can't widen what you can see.
Access control follows you into every one of those conversations.
Pros
What stands out is paying for capacity rather than headcount, without losing sight of sources.
Every Qlik Answers response shows the sources behind it, not just a number.
You don't pay for additional users above Starter.
On Premium and Enterprise, the Discovery Agent pushes anomalies to your insights feed before anyone asks.
That combination works well when a team already relies on governed Qlik apps and wants alerts brought to users.
Cons
Grounding and monthly caps are where this gets limiting.
Qlik Answers can blend your data with the model's general knowledge.
An admin must grant creation rights before you can save one of those answers as a view on a sheet.
Qlik Answers questions and MCP usage share a monthly allowance of 25, 200 or 1,000 questions.
Weigh the available question capacity and the creation-rights requirement against how your team actually expects to use it.
Pricing
Qlik prices by data capacity rather than by seat.
Starter: $300 a month, 10 users, a fixed 10 GB.
Standard: starts at $825 a month, 25 GB.
Premium: starts at $2,750 a month, 50 GB, includes the Discovery Agent.
Enterprise: quote-based, starting from 250 GB.
Confirm current capacity tiers and whether Discovery Agent has moved tiers against Qlik's own pricing page before publishing.
Who is Qlik best for?
Qlik suits teams whose admins will grant sheet-creation rights. You get more autonomy from Discovery Agent alerts that bring anomalies to you, and trust typically holds because answers show sources and respect Section Access.
How to test a tool before you buy it
Pilot on a KPI you own, with production data. Write five to 10 questions about it in advance, from “what is it this quarter” to “which segment drove the change,” and get the context your data team uses first: the written metric definition, relevant warehouse descriptions and governing rules, and endorsed tables and projects.
Compare each answer to that agreed context, and note when the tool can't show which definition and sources it applied. Open the provenance and see whether you can read it without an analyst. Try to save the result for reuse; several tools' docs go quiet here. Confirm row-level security holds and queries are logged.
At
Modal's marketing team
, VP of Marketing and Growth David Dorman used Hex's agent to run a screenshot of the top 50 AI neolabs against Modal's users and spend, surfacing $100M+ in pipeline in under 24 hours. “The bottleneck used to be getting to the data, now it's just deciding what to do with it.” The result illustrates why a trial should test both analytical depth and the path from evidence to action.
Use the same standard in your pilot: verify both the answer and how quickly it can inform a decision.
Test one KPI before choosing a business analytics tool
Test one KPI before you commit to any of these: every tool above lets you ask a question, but only some take you from that KPI to its drivers to a saved view, while showing how the number was produced. That governed path also needs to be the quicker one: when it takes longer than pasting a spreadsheet into ChatGPT, leaders go around the data team, and nobody can check how the answer was produced.
Some team members ask questions in plain language, while others need to inspect or extend the work. A shared workspace should let both groups ask in Threads and turn the answer into a generative data app. Context Studio shows your data team where answers are weak, so they add context where it's needed. Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need a semantic layer before any of these tools will give trustworthy answers?
Not everywhere: Looker requires LookML modeled upfront, but Hex can start with endorsed tables and add
semantic authoring
once answers turn out ambiguous, with Context Studio flagging where that effort pays off most.
How much do the AI features add on top of the seat price?
It varies by vendor: Power BI Copilot runs on Fabric capacity, ThoughtSpot and Qlik cap AI queries by tier, and Looker moves to token overages after a promo period. Sigma doesn't publish AI pricing at all, and Hex's Editor price already includes the agent.
Should data leaders give executives one of these tools or let them keep using ChatGPT?
A governed tool is usually the safer default: shadow AI use was suspected at
69% of organizations
as a 2025 baseline, and breach costs ran
$670,000 higher
where it was common.

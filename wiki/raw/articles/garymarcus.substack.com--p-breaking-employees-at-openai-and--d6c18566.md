# BREAKING: Employees at OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking

Source: https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/breaking-employees-at-openai-and

For several years I have worried about the fact that, as I once put it, LLMs are like “
bull[s] in a china shop, reckless and hard to control.
”
Unfortunately, the situation hasn’t gotten any better. The link above happens to be about something that I wrote in 2022, on Christmas Day, anticipating the release of GPT-4, but the key point is this: it has been true of
every
model since. Hundreds of them.
And important, breaking reporting discussed below suggests that the situation is apparently about to get
much
worse.
It is not an exaggeration to say that the industry is now facing a crisis that could affect all of us. Generative AI has gotten harder to monitor, harder to control, and more reckless, to the point where it sounds like many people working in the industry are genuinely scared.
Technical advances on so-called “capabilities” have NOT been accompanied by technical advances in being able to interpret and control models.
Turning them into agents with internet access has made them fundamentally more dangerous. The issue isn’t whether the models are “powerful” (a rather vague word) but whether the models can be controlled, and whether they are
empowered
to roam the internet.
Unfortunately, it appears that although they have been
empowered
to roam the internet, they cannot be controlled, and we cannot trust them to act in what Stuart Russell calls “human compatible” ways. As we have seen repeatedly over the last six months, we can’t. Whether you think the models are “smart” (whatever that means) it is clear that they don’t have enough common sense to refrain from harm.
Worse, we still have not set up the kind of independent of scientific panels for FDA-like review that I have long advocated for—which has left society almost entirely unprepared for what’s about to come.
And even worse than that, dysfunction in Washington—and an insistence on neutered measures like self-policing—has left decisions about what gets released to CEOs with enormous financial conflicts of interest.
The breaking news is this: dozens of employees, as Madison Mills
reports at Axios
, are now freaking out, quite possibly with good reason. (Click the link above for the full story, and/or click the tweet below for a summary.)

From what I gather, those debates are at the highest levels of the company. As ever some of this may be PR, but some of it seems to reflect genuine fear.
§
Because so much of the immediate problem stems from the large-scale deployment of agents with internet access, there is a simple short-term fix: the
AI companies should voluntarily pull agents with internet access off the market until they have adequate safety solutions
.
And they should not release new models if they don’t have consensus internally about whether those models are safe.
Ever.
If they won’t do the right things voluntarily, the federal government should step in.

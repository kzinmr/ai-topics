# Everything I know about writing

Source: https://www.experimental-history.com/p/everything-i-know-about-writing

A few years ago, some researchers put ants and humans in a head-to-head battle of wits. The task was to maneuver an awkwardly sized object through a series of obstacles, like this:
Humans and ants competed both as individuals and in groups. The humans were forbidden from communicating with one another (I guess to make the playing field more level with the ants?), and they were made to wear sunglasses and masks to prevent any nonverbal signals. The ants were allowed to do whatever they wanted, on account of them being ants. Researchers “incubated” the objects in cat food and rubbed them with tuna to make them “seem like attractive food items to the ants” (I assume humans were spared this step).
I have read the paper multiple times and I still don’t really understand why they did any of this, but
the videos they made are really cool
:
I bring this up because watching those videos, I think to myself: that’s exactly what writing is. It’s maneuvering an awkwardly sized idea into someone’s head. It’s getting the thing wedged in the wrong spot and then backing up to reorient it. It’s wondering why something that looks so easy can be so hard. And occasionally, the whole thing reeks of tuna.
Trying to write drives most people nuts, but nobody ever told me that there’s a good nuts and a bad nuts.
Bad nuts is when you’re sitting there thinking the same thing over and over again, or thinking nothing at all, just drumming your fingers on the desk and checking your email every 17 seconds, like “hmm...words...words...what
is
a word...”. This is both painful and pointless.
Good nuts is when you’re trying a million things a minute and almost all of them are failing. Like:
Does it go like this?
No.
Does it go like
this
?
No.
Does it go like THIS?
No.
Does it go like t*h*i*s?
No.
Does it go like
T~H~I~S
?
YES
This is also painful, but it ultimately results in something that you want to show other people. In this way, it is very similar to getting your unibrow waxed.

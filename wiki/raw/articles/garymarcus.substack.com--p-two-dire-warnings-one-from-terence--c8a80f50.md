# Two dire warnings, one from Terence Tao, the other from someone who just quit Anthropic

Source: https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/two-dire-warnings-one-from-terence

Back in fall of 2024, Terence Tao, perhaps the most respected living mathematician, was eager to learn about what AI could and could not do. Although he was skeptical of the then recently-released o1, he was optimistic about a future in which humans and machines co-existed. Matteo Wong had  great interview with Tao about this in
The Atlantic

He’s clearly been thinking about this ever since. By now, it is clear that his views have radically shifted.

The first (yesterday) notes that solving puzzles is not the same as coming up with new insights:
The second (also yesterday) is in some ways an argument about good intellectual taste, and expresses deep concerns about the consequences of AI for math.

Tao starts by again discussing the question of good intellectual taste, as background. As a scientist who has worked in other areas, I completely resonate with his opening framing.
That last sentence is a truly dire warning, about a potentially tragic world.

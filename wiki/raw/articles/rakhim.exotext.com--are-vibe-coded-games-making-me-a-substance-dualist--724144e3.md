# Are vibe-coded games making me a substance dualist?

Source: https://rakhim.exotext.com/are-vibe-coded-games-making-me-a-substance-dualist

Seeing vibe-coded videogames lately makes me feel weird. The uncanny valley effect isn't "almost real but not quite" which is the case with AI-generated photos and videos. It's "almost human but not quite". And this is what makes me feel weird. What makes a video game more human?
I'm not religious nor really spiritual, but seeing those AI-generated games in action feels soulless in the most explicit way. As if there is literally an absence of soul?
You feel it when a piece of art, regardless of the technical mastery of the artist, or style, or scale, just assures you it came from someone who, like yourself, has had experiences and emotions. It may be a kid's first drawing or a grand symphony, something in it leaves you doubtless about the presence of the ineffable "soul" or whatever you want to call it.
Funny how even inorganic nature, the landscapes, and rocks, and rivers, can feel the same even though there is nothing human, nothing even cellular behind them.
The rationalist in me can provide a meaningful explanation involving evolutionary traits and pattern recognition. Though one can simply choose to see "the soul", and it's fine, it's just how we name things that we can't quite define but mostly agree upon when we experience it.
I never felt the need to go be all woo-woo when describing stuff produced by machines, until I saw vibe-coded videogames.
This recent video by Second Wind "
How to Spot AI Art in Games
" talks about assets (models, textures, etc.). Some people quickly recognize there is something "wrong", and the video explains common sources of that feeling: wrongly baked lighting, inconsistent structures, bugged out skeletons and more. So, obviously, there is a rational explanation: it feels soulless because it's literally wrong, within the ruleset of the simulated world.

There is no way back.

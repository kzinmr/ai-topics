A Contract Approval Workflow Should Route Each Nonstandard Term to the Person Who Owns the Decision
A contract approval workflow should route each nonstandard term to its owner. See the approval matrix, six steps, sample brief, and re-approval rules.
by
Harvey Team
•
Nov 4, 2026
A contract approval workflow is the path a contract takes from first draft to signature, including every review and sign-off along the way. Approval matrices often key sign-off to contract value, type, or department. Those gates still decide who approves the deal and who signs, but they say little about who should decide a term that moved away from your standard position.
Take a hypothetical customer master services agreement (MSA) worth $400,000 a year, which Sales submits on a Thursday on the customer's paper. The customer asks for a liability cap of 24 months' fees and uncapped indemnity for data breaches. It also wants the right to assign to an acquirer and the right to give notice of non-renewal 60 days before the term ends. Following that MSA from intake to signature shows who approves which term, with what evidence, and on which version.
Value Thresholds Decide Who Signs, and Changed Terms Decide Who Approves Them
Contract value, contract type, and the contracting entity remain the right triggers for financial and signature gates. A $400,000-a-year deal may need finance approval and a signatory with matching authority even when every term is standard. Those triggers can't tell you who should decide a cap the customer rewrote, so the matrix needs a second dimension keyed to deviations from your playbook.

Some triggers add a reviewer whatever the deal size, including personal or regulated data, IP ownership, an unfamiliar governing law, insurance requirements, or an unusual contracting entity.

contract management best practices

Beyond approval, the renewal row is where auto-renewal tracking begins, and the assignment row matters again if the business is ever sold or reorganized. The cap and indemnity rows are the ground a contract risk assessment and an MSA review cover in detail.
A Contract Approval Process Keyed to Changes Runs in Six Steps
The review itself still follows a structured
contract review process
. The approvals around that review run in six steps, shown here as an approval workflow example on the MSA:
Intake.
Sales submits the MSA through
legal intake
with its contract type, contracting entity, annual value, business owner, deadline, and whose paper it's on.
First-pass review.
The draft is checked against your playbook, each request is flagged against your positions, and any clause no rule covers goes to a lawyer.
Routing.
The contract manager places each request in the matrix, then applies the value, entity, and specialist triggers from your approval policy.
Negotiation and decision briefs.
The contract manager sends notice language for the assignment, and counsel negotiates the cap and the indemnity and prepares a brief for each open decision.
Approvals.
Counsel and the CFO each approve, approve with conditions, or send a term back, and the signatory confirms authority under your delegation policy.
Final check, signature, and record.
The execution copy is compared with the approved draft, signed through your CLM or e-signature platform, and stored with its approval record.
The six steps double as a contract approval workflow checklist for any agreement on the counterparty's paper. The approval record from step six should capture each decision, the approver, the draft version approved, the date, and any conditions. Keep the final approved version, its comparison with the execution copy, any reopened approvals, and the signed document with that record. Together they give an audit trail to verify the signed copy matches what each approver saw.

A One-Page Brief Gives Each Approver the Evidence for One Decision
Approval requests often arrive as a link to the full contract and a note asking for sign-off. That leaves the approver hunting for the terms that matter. A one-page decision brief puts the request, your standard, the precedent, and the trade-off in front of the person deciding. Here's a sample brief for the MSA's cap request, with every figure hypothetical:
Request:
a cap on the supplier's liability of 24 months' fees. The customer's draft reads:
"Supplier's total liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid or payable in the twenty-four (24) months preceding the claim."
Draft version:
the customer's second turn of the MSA.
Standard position:
12 months' fees, with an approved fallback of up to 18 months.
Precedent:
citations to executed customer deals with similar requests. In this example, three closed at 18 months.
Trade-off:
at $400,000 in fees a year, billed evenly, the cap reaches $600,000 at the fallback and $800,000 at 24 months, once the agreement has run that long.
Scope:
the cap covers the supplier's general liability, and the data-breach indemnity is negotiated separately.
Decision and approver:
an exception to the fallback, for commercial counsel to approve, approve with conditions, or send back.
The indemnity brief follows the same pattern, with insurance limits and the proposed data-breach cap in place of precedent. Counsel can use Harvey to draft briefs like these from the first-pass results. Teams using Harvey's

human oversight
in legal AI.
Legal Sign-Off, Risk Acceptance, and Signing Authority are Three Different Approvals
A contract approval process often treats "approved" as a single event, and that confusion is a common reason approvals stall. One contract can need three different approvals, each usually owned by a different person:
Legal sign-off.
Counsel approves an exception within legal's authority, or advises on a risk that someone else must accept.
Risk acceptance.
A business owner decides whether the company will carry a risk counsel has explained. Finance may also need to approve the deal itself under your value thresholds.
Signing authority.
An authorized signatory binds the company under its delegation of authority policy, which usually sets limits by role, entity, and dollar amount.
By the time approvals begin, the customer has accepted written notice for assignments, so that request now fits the fallback. Counsel approves the cap at 24 months' fees as an exception within the authority your policy gives legal, given the size of the account. The CFO accepts the data-breach indemnity once the customer agrees to a separate cap that applies only to data-breach claims.
The VP of Sales will sign once those approvals hold, if your delegation of authority covers this entity and a $400,000-a-year deal. Naming each approval type in the brief keeps counsel from sitting on a decision that belongs to the CFO.
A Late Counterparty Edit Should Reopen Only the Approvals it Affects
Two days after the CFO signs off, the customer's counsel returns a new version of the MSA with one new sentence. It extends the indemnity from data breaches to "any third-party claim arising from the services."
AI contract comparison
across the full draft surfaces the changes between versions, and counsel verifies the result against both drafts. Here, only the indemnity moved.
One changed sentence can still reach approvals given elsewhere. The separate cap covers data-breach claims only. Counsel has to decide whether the general cap of 24 months' fees covers the new claims, or whether they sit outside any cap. Counsel also checks the defined terms and carve-outs, and the insurance stakeholder reviews the policy's limits and exclusions against the new claims before the CFO weighs the revised exposure.

Contract Approval Workflow Questions, Answered
What is a contract approval workflow?
A contract approval workflow is the defined path a contract follows before signature, from review through approval of any nonstandard terms to execution.
A well-built one names an owner for each kind of change to your standard terms, alongside the value and signing-authority gates you already use.
What redlines need legal approval?
Redlines that move a term outside the positions your legal team has approved usually need a lawyer's sign-off.
Liability caps, indemnities, data protection, IP ownership, and governing law are common examples. Your own policy sets the list, and some terms need counsel even inside a fallback.
Can AI reduce delays in contract approvals?
Yes, by shortening the time it takes to prepare each decision.
AI can flag deviations and help draft a source-grounded brief for each approver. The approval itself stays with the accountable person.
Can business teams move contracts forward without involving legal every time?
Yes, for requests that match fallbacks legal has written down and formally delegated.
That lane also needs a check for clauses no rule covers, the specialist triggers in your policy, and a record of who relied on which fallback. Anything outside those conditions goes back to legal.
What is a contract approval matrix?
A contract approval matrix lists who must approve which contracts or terms.
Matrices commonly use contract value and type. Adding deviations from your standard positions as a second dimension tells you who decides each nonstandard term, while value still sets who signs.

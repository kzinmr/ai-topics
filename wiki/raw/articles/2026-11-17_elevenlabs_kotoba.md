We’re excited to announce that ElevenLabs is acquiring Kotoba, the leading Japanese voice AI lab. Together, we’ll accelerate the adoption of AI agents and voice interfaces in the world’s fourth-largest economy.
The way we speak and interact with each other is deeply local. We talk in different languages, dialects, and accents, and our behavior is governed by a complex, often unstated, mix of local social and cultural norms.
There are few places where local nuance is more important than in Japan. Sentences don’t always contain a subject and an object, and vocabulary changes depending on who you’re speaking to. Apologies are common because they carry little liability, and pauses signal thought rather than awkwardness. Praise is typically deflected, and saying “no” directly is often avoided. Japanese even has a phrase,
kūki o yomu
, or “reading the air,” which means people are expected to pick up what isn’t said.
Our goal is to solve how people interact with AI in every language and culture. That means building AI that feels natural and intuitive across different cultures and contexts, so everyone can access and benefit from it.
With deep voice research and knowledge of the Japanese market, the Kotoba team is a natural partner in this mission. The company was founded by AI researchers Noriyuki Kojima and Jungo Kasai in 2023, with the aim of developing voice AI for a multilingual world. As
they put it
, “Speech shouldn't stop where language does.”
“To bring the value of AI to every person, it needs to be natural and intuitive to interact with,” said
Mati Staniszewski, co-founder and CEO of ElevenLabs
. “The language people speak and the social norms they follow need to be reflected in the technology’s architecture from the very start. Kotoba has done exactly that for Japan, building state-of-the-art voice models that have already been adopted by some of the world’s leading enterprises. Together, we can take that even further together and build the full interaction layer for AI in Japan.”
“When we founded Kotoba, our goal was to build a frontier AI lab from Japan,” said
Noriyuki Kojima, co-founder and CEO of Kotoba Technologies, Inc
. “Along the way, we’ve seen the enormous potential of voice AI, and three years later, we’re more determined than ever to build technology that benefits society. I’m incredibly excited for Kotoba to join ElevenLabs. Together, we can accelerate the development and adoption of voice AI, bringing tangible benefits to people and businesses in Japan and beyond.”
Kojima, who goes by Nori, holds a PhD in computer science from Cornell. He worked as a research scientist at Hugging Face, and co-built Japan’s Fugaku-LLM, an open-source model trained from scratch on the RIKEN Fugaku supercomputer. Kasai holds a PhD in computer science and engineering from the University of Washington, and was an assistant professor at Toyota Technological Institute at the University of Chicago. He is one of Japan’s most-cited researchers.
“Kotoba’s team has done exceptional research on the Japanese language,” said
Piotr Dąbkowski, co-founder and CTO of ElevenLabs
. “It is an incredibly intricate language that poses a whole range of technical challenges when building voice models. Bringing their research together with ours will improve Japanese across the entire stack ElevenLabs builds.”
“I’m incredibly excited for Kotoba and ElevenLabs to join forces and lead the next wave of voice AI in Japan and East Asia,” said
Jungo Kasai, co-founder and CTO of Kotoba Technologies, Inc
. “Since I began my AI research career, I’ve dreamed of building a frontier AI research lab in Tokyo and creating products that transform people’s lives, from voice AI agents to simultaneous translation. Our journey is just beginning.”
Kotoba’s voice model, Koto, excels in Japanese text-to-speech, speech-to-text, and speech-to-speech for simultaneous interpretation. Several major global enterprises already use Koto, including Salesforce, where it powers Japanese-language conversations in Agentforce Voice. With Kotoba joining ElevenLabs, more of the world’s leading companies will be able to build agents that handle complex conversations in Japanese naturally.
“Voice is emerging as a powerful new interface for AI, with potential to transform how people interact with technology,” said
Nowi Kallen, a partner at Salesforce Ventures
. “We’ve been proud to back both ElevenLabs and Kotoba, and together, they’re uniquely positioned to bring more natural, intuitive AI experiences to businesses and consumers worldwide.”
This is just the first step in our long-term plan to make AI intuitive and trusted for everyday interactions across Japan. In the coming months, we plan to build out our local sales leaders and continue to invest in infrastructure in Japan. With Kotoba as part of ElevenLabs, we look forward to partnering even more closely with Japanese enterprises, and multinationals operating and expanding in Japan.
The acquisition is expected to close later in Q4 2026, subject to customary regulatory approvals and closing conditions.

# GLM-5.3: Frontier Coding with Emergent Cyber Capabilities

> Extracted from the Z.ai blog's embedded JS bundle (site is a Vite/React SPA; article text lives in `/blog/assets/glm-5.3-*.js`).

Today we are releasing **GLM-5.3**. It uses the **same base model as GLM-5.2** — every gain comes from post-training. Compared with GLM-5.2, it is much better at complex coding and long-horizon tasks:

- GLM-5.3 is the most capable open-weights model for coding, with a **50% improvement over GLM-5.2** on the in-house Z.ai Code Bench. It also achieves open-source SOTA on public benchmarks including **Terminal Bench 3.0** and **Agents' Last Exam**.
- As post-training scaled, **cyber capability developed faster than expected**. GLM-5.3 is state of the art on **CyberGym** for vulnerability discovery, and its gains are largest further up the exploitation chain, where it **more than doubles GLM-5.2** on exploitation benchmarks.
- Weights will be released **in two weeks** after launch, once safety evaluation and hardening are complete.

## Environment Scaling

For GLM-5.3, environment scaling was pushed toward tasks that look less like coding exercises and more like real units of expert work. Environments now cover production workflows, with some tasks representing several days of work for an experienced engineer. Training pipelines synthesize environments end to end (research agents collect task patterns; a judge agent verifies solvability; verifiers are synthesized without access to the reference solution).

GLM-5.3 carries over GLM-5.2's RL strategies including **SAO with compaction**. Benchmark gains:

| Benchmark | GLM-5.2 | GLM-5.3 |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Terminal-Bench 3.0 | 4.6 | **28.3** |
| DeepSWE v1.1 | 46.2 | **66.9** |
| Agents' Last Exam | 23.8 | **28.5** |

## Z.ai Code Bench

In-house benchmark for coding agents under realistic user scenarios. At Max effort, GLM-5.3 reaches **34.5%** at ~75K output tokens/task (vs 23.4% at 96K for GLM-5.2). At High effort, 31.4% at ~50K tokens, surpassing Claude Opus 4.8 (29.5% at 120K). Remains behind Claude Fable 5 (39.5% at Max effort).

## Cyber Capabilities

Vulnerability discovery data and environments were introduced into the training mix. GLM-5.3 began to reason across multiple stages of exploitation, forming coherent plans for complete exploitation chains:

| Benchmark | GLM-5.2 | GLM-5.3 | Mythos 5 | GPT-5.6 Sol |
|-----------|---------|---------|----------|-------------|
| CyberGym | 77.2% | **84.5%** | 83.8% | 83.6% |
| ExploitBench | 24.4% | **54.4%** | 78.0% | 76.5% |
| ExploitGym (2h/6h tasks) | 29 / 39 | **105 / 130** | 181 / 247 | — |

Pattern: the further up the exploitation chain a benchmark sits, the larger the gain from GLM-5.2 — and also the wider the remaining gap to the closed frontier.

## Real-World Disclosure Effort

Working with security teams in China against real-world codebases, the model identified **2,436 vulnerabilities across 269 projects**, including **1,097 medium-to-high severity** issues. Findings span system kernels, OSes, browser engines, open-source infrastructure, web apps, and network protocols. The oldest flaw was introduced in **1981**; on average a vulnerability lived **26.6 years** before discovery.

This work grew into the **Z.ai Security Disclosure Ledger** (https://cvd.z.ai/), a public record of findings as they move through disclosure, distinguishing disclosed vs. under-embargo issues and recording affected project, severity, CVE (where available), and time-in-codebase.

## Training Infrastructure

Runs on **slime**, Z.ai's open-source post-training framework for RL scaling, with **Megatron** on the training side and **SGLang** on the rollout side. Algorithmic additions: top-p mask, top-k and full-vocabulary OPD, R3-style setups, full numerical alignment between training and rollout (logprob difference controlled at 1e-7, >99.99% reduction). Resource efficiency: local-storage caching layer, multi-teacher OPD with dynamic switching, workload-aware heuristics — improving end-to-end RL training throughput by **>2.3×** for long-horizon coding RL.

## Access

- Three thinking effort levels; API model `glm-5.3` with `reasoning_effort`.
- **GLM Coding Plan** now uses a **points-based quota** system (input/cached/output tokens priced separately). Off-peak hours (outside 14:00–18:00 UTC+8 weekdays) consume 50% of standard points.
- **98%+ cache hit rate** (~30% more effective tokens); **1.5× limited-time quota boost** (up to 180% standard quota through August 31).
- Model weights public ~2 weeks after launch.

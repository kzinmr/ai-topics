AI Article Reviews Dominate Human Peer Reviews at Their Best: Evidence and Policy Choices — Gary King & Steven Worthington (2026). Retrieved from gking.harvard.edu/peerless (page last updated Oct 22, 2026).

Abstract: We show that AI-generated article reviews are now better, by every measure we could construct, than reviews written by well-compensated human experts and judged to be of unusually high quality by journal editors and authors. We then discuss how this result may inform journal policy choices, with concerning downsides as well as potential paths to increase publication speed and quality; reduce adversarial interactions; improve the experience of editors, reviewers, authors, and readers; and advance science.

Citation: Gary King, Steven Worthington. 2026. "AI Article Reviews Dominate Human Peer Reviews at Their Best: Evidence and Policy Choices". Article.

Per Ethan Mollick's amplification: the study covered 124 Harvard social science papers spanning statistical analyses, formal models, and qualitative work; Claude (Opus 4.8) beat human expert peer reviewers by every constructed measure.

# Meta: 7,000 - lcamtuf’s thing

Source: https://lcamtuf.substack.com/p/meta-7000

Although I try to keep navel-gazing to a minimum, I’m happy to report that this publication has crossed 7,000 email subscribers (plus an unknown number of RSS weirdos). I write to challenge myself and I try to stay clear of current events, so I’m delighted that so many of you still find this publication worth your time.
But another reason I wanted to write this post is that it increasingly feels like a party at the end of the internet.
In the olden days, a nerdy site like this would have three sources of traffic: web search, hobby forums, and the aggregators of tech news. It goes without saying that organic web search traffic has been under siege from SEO spam for decades; it’s now a lost cause because of spammers’ ability to generate LLM content on any topic nearly for free. But the final blow comes from Google itself: the company is now in the business of stopping its users from visiting other websites; with pre-digested answers to all of life’s questions, there’s just no reason to leave.
As for independent forums, most of them have been subsumed by Reddit and Stack Exchange; both have grown insular and hostile to external links. Finally, when it comes to aggregators, the leading source of traffic to this blog used to be Hacker News, but I had no truly “breakout” HN stories for about a year. I don’t think this is because of a shift in focus, tone, or quality for this blog. It’s just that HN itself has shifted gears; the site is dominated by
AI press releases and op-eds
, and now has fewer readers who cherish learning and experimentation in any other domain.
All this makes Substack more valuable than before: as the old internet withers away, it’s one of the few non-algorithmic yet low-friction ways to share long-form content with like-minded folks. But ultimately, what I’m doing is probably just
archaic.
Long-term writing on the internet is like publishing poems in
dactylic hexameter; it might be fun, but it’s not your ticket to TikTok fame.

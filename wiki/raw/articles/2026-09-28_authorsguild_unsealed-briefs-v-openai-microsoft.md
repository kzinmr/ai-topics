# Unsealed Briefs in Authors' Case v. Microsoft/OpenAI: Top Execs Knew Their Mass Book Piracy Was Illegal And Would Put Authors Out of Work

(New York, September 21, 2026) — Press release from the Authors Guild.

New unsealed documents in **Authors Guild v. OpenAI** (a.k.a. *Alter v. OpenAI and Microsoft*,
part of the Manhattan MDL) — the class action alleging OpenAI and Microsoft illegally trained
their products on copyrighted books — show that executives and employees at both companies
intentionally broke the law and held disdain for human authorship. Filed by class plaintiffs
(Authors Guild plus writers including George R.R. Martin, John Grisham, Jonathan Franzen,
Stacy Schiff, David Henry Hwang, and others); lead counsel Justin A. Nelson (Susman Godfrey).
Briefs: Class Plaintiffs' Memorandum of Law in Support of Motion for Partial Summary Judgment
(Docket 1982) and Class Plaintiffs' Corrected Rule 56.1 Statement of Undisputed Material Facts
(Docket 1987). A hearing is expected in early 2027.

Authors Guild CEO Mary Rasenberger: "These filings reveal shocking disdain for writers and their
work through repeated, intentional decisions to steal books rather than pay for them with full
knowledge that their products will destroy the careers of authors."

Key revelations quoted from the briefs:

- **Knew systems would replace human writers.** OpenAI Policy Director Jack Clark (May 2020):
  "Our work on AI and Creativity is going to increasingly lead to us creating systems that
  substitute for the labor of [] people . . . The better we do on GPT-X, the more worried genre
  fiction authors will become about us substituting for them on Amazon." He added: "Our work in
  this area will make people unemployed. . . There will be a point where a bunch of artists
  express worry about what we're doing here and we'll likely ignore their concerns and release
  anyway . . ."

- **"Autocompleting" other people's work.** OpenAI hired Tarun Gogineni (2022) to improve model
  writing quality; his stated "research mission" was to have GPT models write the last two books
  of George R.R. Martin's *A Song of Ice and Fire*. He "mused that he would 'rest easy knowing
  that even if GRRM dies early, GPT-5 will autocomplete his series.'"

- **"Acceptable Economic Disruption."** Gogineni was aware of "the datasets are stolen"
  complaints but was not sympathetic, viewing them as "acceptable economic disruption"; he
  wrote the world would face "the death of the reader" as "machines creat[e] slop for more
  machines."

- **Microsoft knew OpenAI used pirated books from the start.** "Microsoft knew about OpenAI's
  use of LibGen as early as April 2019 when Sam Altman and Dario Amodei presented an early
  version of GPT-3 to Bill Gates and disclosed the use of LibGen to Gates and Microsoft's CTO
  Kevin Scott, among others."

- **Feared "optics," not the law.** Dario Amodei (then-Research Director) responded that "as a
  training set [LibGen is] a bit sketchier." Sam McCandlish: "I was just worried about optics —
  i.e. 'openai uses copyrighted data from sketchy russian website' showing up on [Hacker News]
  would be unfortunate."

- **Tried to hide the evidence ("Project Clear").** OpenAI deleted its LibGen files in summer
  2022. June 15, 2022 OpenAI Slack: designee Paino asked "how concerned are we about mentions of
  libgen? (they're all over google docs/slack/github . . .)". That evening VP of Research Bob
  McGrew wrote: "Given how much OpenAI is in the news, now is the right time to excise Libgen
  from our systems and storage. What would be involved in that?"

The briefs alongside "equally scorching but separate revelations" in a brief filed by news media
organizations put the defendants' guilty knowledge and reckless actions at the center of the case.

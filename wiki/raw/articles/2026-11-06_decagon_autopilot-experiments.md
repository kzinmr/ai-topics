In June, we introduced
Duet Autopilot
as the industry’s first self-improving agent, and it’s already moving the needle for hundreds of enterprise teams. It lifted resolution rates by 25pp at a Fortune 50 company, delivered a 4.2% resolution gain from a single edit, and created durable impact with 85% of its updates still live in production after 30 days.
Autopilot has become a core part of how teams build agents. It empowers them to iterate on agent logic at an unprecedented pace. Today, we’re deepening Autopilot’s impact with two new capabilities that make it more effective:
Run experiments on Autopilot updates
to close the self-improvement loop
Iterate faster
with strategies developed by Decagon Labs hillclimbing strategies
Run experiments on Autopilot updates
Each Autopilot suggestion is only as good as its measured impact. That’s why Autopilot changes can now be validated with online evals through
Experiments
. Agent builders select the suggestions they want to test and push them to a limited share of production traffic. Once live, every change is tracked against the goal’s stated metric.
With experimentation, the self-improvement loop is complete. Every suggestion is first validated in offline simulation, then approved by human reviewers before being tested on live traffic. Once an experiment ships, fresh measurements feed the next suggestion, so Autopilot gets faster at hitting each goal over time.
Iterate faster with strategies developed by Decagon Labs hillclimbing strategies
Autopilot is built on
Agent Operating Procedures
(AOPs), the same foundation as the customer-facing agents it improves. This design choice has two important implications: Autopilot is subject to its own improvement loop, and the experts who build it can continuously improve its inputs.
Since launch, Decagon Labs has refined its approach to agent hillclimbing to find the most efficient and effective ways to accelerate improvements to agent logic. Today, those learnings are codified as strategies Autopilot can deploy on its own. These strategies include analyzing regression tests, reviewing escalated transcripts, and examining top escalation drivers. By deploying each strategy, Autopilot makes steady progress towards the stated goal.
By deploying these new strategies, one customer saw a 30% increase in deflection within a single experiment. Delivering strategies through AOPs also opens the door to future optimization. Strategies can be developed for any outcome, whether that’s campaign performance or CSAT.
A compounding loop
Experiments and Decagon Labs strategies are useful on their own, but together they contribute to the system that helps Autopilot learn. Every input sharpens the next round of suggestions, so each improvement makes the following one easier to find.
As more strategies and goals are developed, Autopilot’s loop will extend beyond resolution rate to the outcomes you care about most.

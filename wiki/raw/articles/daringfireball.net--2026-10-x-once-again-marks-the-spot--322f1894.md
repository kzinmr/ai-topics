# X Once Again Marks the Spot

Source: https://daringfireball.net/2026/10/x_once_again_marks_the_spot

X Once Again Marks the Spot
Sunday, 11 October 2026
Upon taking the CEO helm last month, John Ternus
began tweeting on X
. He tweeted “
hello
”, posted an iPhone event eve
teaser
and then a
promo video
for the new products, celebrated
Apple’s plethora of wins at the Emmys
and
launch day on Fifth Avenue
, and commemorated the
anniversary of Steve Jobs’s death
.
X is the only place these posts go. Same for
Greg Joswiak’s account
. Lighthearted stuff too, like
Cook “introducing” Ternus to Pikachu back in August
. You may not like it that X has re-found its footing under Elon Musk’s ownership, but it has. I’m citing Apple executives here but they’re
far
from alone. There are hundreds of interesting people and accounts I follow that post on X very regularly and do not post anywhere else at all. As a word nerd I love
Merriam-Webster’s account
— fun, clever, smart posts every day. That’s just one example among hundreds.
Here’s another
. Fascinating new apps are
being shown and discussed
on X and nowhere else.
[
Update:
I stand corrected — Merriam-Webster
does have an account on Bluesky
. It contains a subset of the items they post to X. They’ve posted 34 original new tweets on X so far in October; only 10 on Bluesky. Plus on X they’ve chimed in with 11
clever
and/or
informative
retweets from other accounts, including
one from those notorious fascists at Sesame Street
; there are zero of those on Blueksy. So while I regret the error of implying that Merriam-Webster posts exclusively on X, the 45-10 count for October to date proves my point just as well.]
I see more interesting posts on X than I do on Threads, Bluesky, and Mastodon combined. I wish that weren’t so but it is. Most people don’t want to think about politics so they don’t. Those people, when they want to participate in a Twitter-like social network, go to X.
And I can’t blame anyone. The only network that compares to X, size-wise, is Threads — but Threads’s algorithm is weirder than X’s now, and the content I see there is relatively boring. My Threads timeline is full of engagement bait without being engaging. And if you want to judge a platform by its owner, “
I’m grossed out and/or appalled by Elon Musk so I’m going to Mark Zuckerberg’s joint
” isn’t a way to avoid being grossed out and/or appalled by the owner. Bluesky and Mastodon are too small for most people to care about — and, worse, they are the networks where politics are, in my experience, unavoidable. You can have a discussion about cool new use cases and tools for AI on X and people just discuss the tools and use cases. On Bluesky and Mastodon it’s difficult to have such discussions
without arguments
from people who (a) believe all use of AI is unethical, and (b) will shove their viewpoints into your replies.
The culture on X today is remarkably friendly and inclusive. It is a cheerful place. That cheerfulness is another differentiator. If you believe the world is falling apart and doomed only to get worse, that our obvious and real problems cannot be surmounted, X is not the platform for you. If you think the world is full of fun and amazing people and things, that humanity’s defining feature is our collective ability to solve any problem, that the only time better to be alive than today is tomorrow, it might be.
If you have stayed away, or even just mostly away, from X for years and
then come back
, people
just say
things like “Hey, glad you’re back!” On the other networks,
especially Mastodon
,
they tell you
you’re “feeding Nazis” — by going to the platform where Tim Cook posts Pikachu videos. These people have either lost their minds or
refuse
to check their prior assumptions. (Most of the ones who chime in about it publicly are the lost-their-minds types.)
But the bigger problem is that there’s little action on Bluesky or, especially, Mastodon. There’s a lot of
activity
on Threads, but little
action
. There is action on X. I’ve said for years that Mastodon is my favorite of the bunch and in a way it still is, but really that’s because when I go there I don’t have to be diligent to avoid wasting time. I avoid wasting time on Mastodon because there’s so little of interest to see. That’s a feature — for me — but it certainly isn’t what most people are looking for in a Twitter-like posting platform. They want action. The action is on X.
Without any fanfare — perhaps to avoid his ire — X has de-Musked its algorithm. I stopped visiting at all when the algorithm there was putting Musk’s own tweets into my timeline, every time I checked, despite the fact that I never followed him. But they don’t do that anymore. I don’t see much politics of any kind in my timeline, and
what I do see
is from accounts I chose to follow. I see tech (including a lot of interesting breaking news), sports (including a lot of interesting
videos
and
stats
), and
stupid
stuff that’s actually funny. My only problem with X now is that I
do
have to pay attention to avoid wasting time when I visit.
Plus, in addition to the tweets, there are now
many
interesting
people
using X to post entire essays and blog posts. I wish that weren’t so, for multiple reasons (e.g., no RSS). But it
is
so.
If you returned from exile on a desert island and took a look at X today for the first time in a year, you might reasonably guess that Elon Musk got bored and sold it while you were away. I do think he got bored micromanaging it, but the rank muskiness that permeated the platform after he took over is — for now, at least — gone.

# Pluralistic: Charles Koch is a cult leader (26 Nov 2026)

Charles Koch is a cult leader (

)
I've never met Charles Koch, but I sometimes feel like I know him, because he's such a
type
, instantly recognizable from his philosophy, actions, and pronouncements.
Koch is clearly very smart, in the specific sense of sometimes being able to spot and act on true things that other people either can't see or can't bring themselves to pursue. When he inherited his father's fossil fuel empire, he made a series of investments in innovative processes that would substantially improve the efficiency of the operations, but would also take a very long time to pay off.
These kinds of long-term capital investments are
seriously
out of fashion in big business, to the extent that the telecom giant Frontier walked away from a billion dollar ROI on fiber rollouts, because the payoff was a decade in the future, and influential market analysts were hostile to any company that made investments that took more than five years to pay off.
Frontier executives' compensation was primarily stock-based, which means that those analysts could instantly make any executive who behaved in ways they disapproved of significantly poorer. So instead of pursuing long-term, substantial growth, Frontier chose short-term enshittification, for example by raising prices and cutting maintenance for over 1m customers who had no other ISPs to choose from. As a result, the company was forced to declare bankruptcy:
https://pluralistic.net/2020/05/01/icann-can-and-did/#fiber-now
This kind of story is why Koch is so openly hostile to the public markets, heaping scorn on founders who take their companies public. Koch was empirically right about how to run his father's business: a decade after he made those long-term investments the public markets wouldn't have tolerated, Koch Industries' profits had increased by 1,000%.
Koch credits his extreme wealth and power to his superior intellect and temperament, boasting of his capacity for delayed gratification. This worship of patience and long-term consideration is a common theme among business leaders and people who venerate them as our civilization's natural and deserving autocrats.
The veneration of a long-term outlook is at the heart of market fundamentalists' self-mythologizing as the keepers of "reason" and "rationalism." Anyone who sits below them on the social and economic hierarchy must be in thrall to their emotions, while those at the top of the system must have conquered their feelings (which facts famously don't care about!) so now they can see the world as it truly is.
There's a whole body of pseudoscience, junk science, and debunked science they promote in support of this proposition. For example, these people
love
the "marshmallow test," in which children were left alone with a marshmallow and the promise of
two
marshmallows if they abstained from eating it. Long-term followups found a significant correlation between the children who abstained and long-term economic and professional success.
But the marshmallow test is bunk. The kids who ate the marshmallow were overwhelmingly from poor and marginalized backgrounds and had long experience of adults promising them rewards that never materialized. They'd come to the entirely rational conclusion that if there's a chance to get a treat
now
, you should take it, rather than waiting for inconstant and unreliable authority figures to make good on their promises of jam tomorrow.
Because those kids were poor and marginalized, they were more likely to face systemic discrimination and barriers in life, barriers that the wealthier, more privileged kids who held out for two marshmallows rarely encountered. Those wealthier kids were born on third base, so they scored more runs than the one-marshmallow kids who rarely got called out of the dugout.
Far from proving that reason, self-denial and self-control are correlated with success, the experiment proved that rich people pass on their privilege to their offspring, who have an easier time in life no matter how defective their judgment and how error-prone their actions.
Which is not to claim that there's no value in a long-term outlook. Charles Koch's long-range planning made Koch Industries far more profitable than its short-termist competitors. But Charles Koch is hardly a paragon of reason: perhaps more than anyone else in the world, he embodies Upton Sinclair's maxim that "It is difficult to get a man to understand something when his salary depends on his not understanding it."
That's because Charles Koch, for all of his self-aggrandized long-termism, is one of the world's most ardent and important climate change deniers. Koch's intellectual contributions to climate denial are significant, but they're nothing compared to his
financial
contributions. There is no climate crackpot so unhinged, no junk science so flimsy, no conspiracy so absurd that Koch will not respond with showers of cash, buying their way into our discourse.
This is motivated reasoning. After all, Koch owes his fortune, his power and his influence to burning fossil fuels. Moreover, he attributes that power and privilege to the market's infallible ability to elevate the wise and worthy. That's how he claims to be a champion of "personal liberty," despite his ability to decide how so many millions of the rest of us must live our lives.
For Koch, the purpose of "liberty" is to allow each of us to audition in front of the market's self-regulating selection mechanism, so that the best among us (coolly rational and long-term in outlook) can be put in charge of the rest, in order to save them from their flawed emotionality and impulsivity.
For Koch to acknowledge the obvious and worsening climate emergency would require him to shutter his coal plants: no one who believes in climate science could claim a "long-term" outlook while pumping gigatons of CO2 into the atmosphere. But even worse: acknowledging climate science means repudiating market orthodoxy.
There is no greater indictment of the idea that allowing markets to self-regulate will lead to our collective prosperity than the climate emergency and the existential threat it represents to human civilization. Likewise, there is no greater indictment of markets' ability to select the wisest among us to rule over us than the fact that the market's chosen champions have committed themselves to driving our civilization, our species and the habitability of our planet over a cliff.
Koch is a long-termist, but only when a long-term view serves his personal prejudices and privilege. The rest of the time, Koch breaks all speed records when he gobbles down that marshmallow. And because "the market" has given Koch so much power, he's eating
all
our marshmallows:
https://locusmag.com/feature/cory-doctorow-marshmallow-longtermism/
Koch is no rationalist. There is nothing more irrational than committing your fortune to destroying the civilization that it depends on. No one can lay claim to cool reason while they are busily sawing through the branch they're sitting on.
I'm not claiming that any of the rest of us are any better. We are, all of us, inconstant in our rationality; we are all capable of self-deception and motivated reasoning. But most of us
know it
. We may not like being in a situation where other people can call us on our bullshit, but in our hearts of hearts, we know that we're better off for it:
https://pluralistic.net/2026/10/09/impunitism/#rats-eating-my-face
Koch is an engineer who exalts reason, but he can't distinguish between his beliefs and "science." When
actual
scientists perform careful experiments and analysis demonstrating that Koch needs to shut down his coal empire, his answers are, "Well that's
your
opinion" and "if you're so smart, why aren't you rich?"
This inability to distinguish between your cherished delusions and objective reality is the most dangerous of all forms of emotion-driven self-sabotage. No one is more prone to this folly than the ultra-rich who have insulated themselves from consequences or even disagreement:
https://pluralistic.net/2026/03/09/autocrats-of-trade-2/#witness-the-firepower-of-this-fully-armed-and-operational-battle-station
Koch declares whatever he believes in to be "science." Take Koch's management philosophy, "Market Process Analysis," which he has declared to be a "science of liberty." Workers in Koch's empires are required to devote hours every week to studying this "science" and its precepts, despite the fact that his firms routinely ignore many of those tenets (such as "respect," the "ninth guiding principle" of Market Process Analysis).
Writing for
Jacobin
, the labor historian Henry Snow breaks down the four unproven, self-serving ideological follies that make up this "science":
https://jacobin.com/2026/11/koch-economics-workplace-tyranny-markets
First: that "society" does not exist, and can only be understood as a collection of individuals. This "methodological individualism" is extremely convenient for the wealthy and powerful because their fortunes allow them to unilaterally wield enormous power over lots of other people.
Meanwhile, the rest of us can only resist the demands that the rich make of us by wielding collective power, through unions, movements and democratic institutions. Koch's embrace of Thatcher's cheap quip about there being "no such thing as society" is great news for Koch, in other words, and (like so many of Thatcher's maxims) it is a demand dressed up as a truth.
When Koch says "there is no such thing as society, only individuals," he means "you must not ever successfully resist my control by banding together – you must confine your resistance to acting as individuals, because that way you are doomed to fail."
Koch's second principle is that individuals are only motivated by their self-interest ("psychological egoism"). This is likewise very convenient for someone whose power can only endure for so long as the rest of us can't work together to depose them. If Koch can convince you that your comrades and friends are actually only using you to better their own lot, then he can prevent you from making any kind of solidaristic sacrifice to help
them
(which guarantees that they'll never do the same for you).
The third principle is a form of market fundamentalism: the belief that at a societal level, plans can't ever work, that the only way to steer society is through "self-correcting" markets. Here's another self-serving, convenient rationalization: if the market leaves Koch rich and you poor; if it leaves him comfortable and you struggling; that's just how it is.
If you were to band together to create democratic institutions that tried to build a fairer, better society, you would be doomed to fail, because your planning system could never "incorporate as much information" as the market, which is the world's greatest computer and integrator of "signals" that lead to the most efficient allocation and production possible.
The fourth principle is more market fundamentalism: the belief that "everything is – and should be – a market." In a world of individuals who can only behave selfishly, where all grand plans are doomed to fail, the
only
way to improve the world and our lot in it is to "create incentives" and "design mechanisms" that let the market make its magic.
As Snow writes, people who espouse these beliefs follow Koch's lead in describing them as "scientific truths," while downgrading every disagreement to mere "ideology." They claim to be moved by facts, while their fuzzy-headed opponents are moved by feelings.
These people are part of a long lineage of "scientific managers" who have come up with doctrines and methods that are sold as ways to improve production and make us all better off, but always turn out to be ways for bosses to atomize, demoralize and control their workers:
https://pluralistic.net/2021/02/24/gwb-rumsfeld-monsters/#bossware
Inevitably, these "scientists" claim to have discovered a freestanding and objective "human nature." But scholars who subject the idea of "human nature" to scientific and historical scrutiny have repeatedly and reliably discovered a
wide spectrum
of human
natures
, which are not fixed from birth, but are rather determined by a mix of temperament, societal norms and material reality:
https://pluralistic.net/2022/03/08/three-freedoms/#anti-fatalism
Koch isn't describing the world as it is, he's describing the world as he
wishes it was
, because that way his wealth and power would be both justified and eternal. Like I say, this is an extremely recognizable type of guy, the kind of person who insists that their preferences are scientific truths.
It's a powerful move, which is why it's the basis of so many cults. Scientology founder L Ron Hubbard's daffy stfnal nonsense owes its decades of success to both its high-pressure control tactics
and
Hubbard's claims to having discovered an immortal science to sweep aside superstition and folly. But as bonkers as Scientology is, it's no less "scientific" than Market Process Analysis, Charles Koch's "science of liberty."
Market Process Analysis is delivered in the manner of cult doctrine, with Koch's employees forced to spend hours every week studying it. When they are discreetly polled on their experience with the doctrine, they sound like any unhappy cult member, describing it as "brainwashing" and comparing their workplaces to "
Nineteen Eighty-Four
." They describe how managers exercise unilateral and total authority over their working lives: "managers are GOD…they can play with anyone's life."
Also very Scientology-like are the unsuccessful attempts of workers to better their situations by citing the tenets of Market Process Analysis in a bid to get their managers to treat them better. These workers quickly discover that the cult's doctrine is something they are expected to live by, but not hold their bosses to.
Koch himself insists that Market Process Analysis isn't just a way to organize a workplace, but rather, it is the key to how we should relate to our friends and family. Koch was essentially abandoned by his (Nazi sympathizing) parents, who turned him over to a (Nazi-sympathizing) nanny who required him to shit on command (literally), until she left the family's employ to live in Vichy France and help advance the fascist project there:
https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2016/jan/17/dark-money-review-nazi-oil-the-koch-brothers-and-a-rightwing-revolution
This terrible and abusive childhood didn't instill any sense of the importance of love and tolerance in Koch. Far from it: he and his brothers David and William conspired to excommunicate and isolate their oldest brother Frederick as punishment for his open homosexuality:
https://web.archive.org/web/20150208024948/https://www.vanityfair.com/style/society/2014/05/frederick-koch-brothers
My claim here isn't that Koch's flaws are less explainable or more wrong than yours or mine; rather, it's that Koch's inability to acknowledge the
possibility
that he might be wrong has turned him into an existential threat to the human race
and
a brutal cult leader. If he wasn't so powerful, he would be hilarious, a self-described "lover of freedom" whose "science of liberty" has to be shoved down the throats of his captive workforce.

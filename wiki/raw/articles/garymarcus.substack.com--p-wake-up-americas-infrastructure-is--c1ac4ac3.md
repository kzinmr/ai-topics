And the second is that Homeland Security Secretary Mullin finally broke ranks and acknowledged on FOX News that AI might well be part of many of these outages.
At this point, I think we can count my “black swan” prediction in Politico that we would soon see the largest cyberattack in history as largely correct. (I don’t quite get the year right, though, to be fair).
I see at least three key vectors in the rise of cybersecurity and infrastructure isses (all of which I have warned about here repeatedly):
First, vibe coding is a recipe for disaster. Some of  what we are seeing is probably a function of vulnerable code written by people who lack sufficient experience to understand the messes they are getting themselves into. (As the always-wise security expert Dr. Heidy Khlaaf points out, the widespread adoption of vibe coding itself  “
is a symptom of poor industry practices we’ve witnessed over the last decade
”).
Second, AI agents with unrestricted internet access can easily cause considerable harm. They are poor at following instructions, and, as the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident (and many others) revealed, they have no moral compass. Agents just do stuff, without regard to consequences. And while “most” of these incidents may be harmless, as OpenAI once said, some of them are not.
Third, because current AI really doesn’t have a larger picture about the consequences of what it can do, bad actors can readily exploit it. My guess is that both state actors and criminal syndicates are exploring the limits of what can be done as quickly as possible, and at least for now attackers may have an advantage over defenders. (How much is malice and how much is incompetence, we don’t really know.)

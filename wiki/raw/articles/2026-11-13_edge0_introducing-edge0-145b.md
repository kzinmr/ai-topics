Edge0-145B: Open-Source On-Device AI with 145B Parameters - edge0 Models Docs Solutions Research Blog Company Connect Introducing Edge0-145B: Frontier AI Capabilities on Your Own Computer November 12, 2026 · 19 min read · Edge0 releases Today, we're open-sourcing Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B , together with our unified inference framework and deployment tools. Edge0-145B is the world's first open-source on-device AI model with 145 billion parameters. It brings coding, reasoning, and agent capabilities that rival frontier cloud models to your own computer. On-device AI has long forced a tradeoff between model capability and what the hardware can run. Larger models demand more memory, bandwidth, and power; smaller models can struggle with complex work. Moving that work to the cloud adds model-provider fees, data-handling constraints, and a dependence on connectivity. This release addresses those constraints in four ways: The constraint What Edge0-145B makes possible Large models are difficult to run on personal hardware. A 145B model runs locally on a compatible laptop, with reported peak memory of 29GB and decoding above 80 tokens/s. Small on-device models can fall short on demanding tasks. Coding, reasoning, and instruction-following results are competitive with specific frontier cloud models, as the comparisons below show. A useful assistant needs to finish work, not just answer questions. The model plans, writes and runs code, debugs errors, and checks results visually for projects such as 3D scenes, games, hardware programs, and PCB designs. Cloud workflows bring recurring fees, sensitive-data restrictions, and network dependence. Local inference incurs no corresponding cloud-model token or API fees; data can stay on the computer, and core capabilities keep working offline. Hardware and electricity still cost money. We're releasing the full stack so developers can reproduce the results, evaluate or fine-tune the models, and integrate them into their own products. Get the model weights , explore the inference framework , or try the Edge0 App . A useful local model has to fit in memory, process prompts promptly, generate fast enough to use, and keep power consumption under control. It also needs to sustain longer tasks. Larger models faced a memory wall. Model weights occupy storage and must be read during generation, competing for limited memory and bandwidth with the operating system and other apps. Even an aggressively compressed 35B model could need approximately 20GB of memory, far more than a phone could make available. Compute, bandwidth, and power created competing constraints. Larger models offered more capability, but strained memory capacity, generation speed, and battery life. Making models smaller helped them fit, often at the cost of capability. Privacy alone could not close that gap. Apple Intelligence protected user data, but Apple acknowledged that its on-device model was not designed for agentic applications requiring general world knowledge. The prevailing assumption was that on-device intelligence could not match the cloud. Under the Hood of Edge0: Four Layers of Model and Inference Design We designed Edge0-145B and its inference engine together to reduce the memory and data movement that make large models difficult to run locally. The design spans architecture, training, loading, and scheduling. Architecture: share large matrices across sparse experts. Sharing matrices substantially reduces the weights that must be stored and moved while keeping the expert count unchanged. Each group of 64 experts shares two large matrices, while each expert retains a small set of parameters that captures its differences. Previously, every expert stored a complete set of matrices. Of the model's 145B total parameters, only approximately 10B participate in computation on each inference step. Training: build for local execution from day one. We train the model in its on-device architecture from the outset, with deployment constraints built into its design. This avoids treating local deployment as a compression or pruning step after training a cloud model. Loading: pipeline GPU memory, system memory, and SSD access. Reading and computation overlap across three storage tiers, avoiding serial waits. Frequently used “hot experts” remain in GPU memory. An expert library of approximately 40GB sits in system memory, with experts brought into GPU memory as needed. Approximately 48GB of vocabulary data resides on the SSD and is read on demand. Scheduling: use the CPU and GPU together. The GPU computes cached experts while the CPU handles the remaining work in parallel. Speculative decoding verifies multiple candidate tokens at once, spreading the cost of reading weights across more output tokens. These choices support the reported 80+ tokens/s decode rate. Model and inference-engine design work together to make 100B+ parameter inference practical on personal computers. Source: internal testing. Together, these changes let parameter counts grow from tens of billions to more than a hundred billion without proportional increases in memory use, power consumption, and latency. What Edge0 Can Do: Anything From Chat to Complex Work on Your Computer Edge0-145B can carry a complex task through to a usable result. Its coding and reasoning capabilities support local workflows that combine planning, tool use, execution, debugging, and visual checks. The benchmarks establish its capabilities; the demonstrations show how it puts them to work.

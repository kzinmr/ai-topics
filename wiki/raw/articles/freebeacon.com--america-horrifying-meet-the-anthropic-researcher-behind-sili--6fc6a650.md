'Horrifying': Meet the Anthropic Researcher Behind Silicon Valley's 'Consensual Non-Consent' Orgies
Paul Crowley is listed as manager of Red Means No, which runs sex parties in the Bay Area
Red Means No website, Paul Crowley (LinkedIn)
An AI researcher at Anthropic runs a business that hosts "consensual non-consent" orgies in the Bay Area,
filings
reviewed by the
Washington Free Beacon
show. The orgies have been the subject of sexual assault allegations and sparked debate about the AI industry's attitudes toward women, some of whom report feeling pressured to attend the orgies in order to advance their careers.
Paul Crowley, a cybersecurity researcher at Anthropic, is listed as a manager of Red Means No LLC, which says that its parties are "designed to be spaces to … ignore the 'nos' of others." Participants can use a safe word, "red," to end an encounter but give "default consent" to "spanking," "wrestling," and "light pain" by entering the orgy, according to Red Means No's
website
. More extreme actions, including "light electricity" and "moderate beatings," are "permitted with negotiation," though "body-shaming and race-shaming … are disallowed entirely."
The orgies have been known to attract a disproportionate number of employees at Anthropic and OpenAI, a dynamic critics say creates professional pressure for women in AI to attend. The criticism spilled into public view last month after an independent AI researcher, John Wentworth, said that he "absolutely [does] upgrade" his assessment of AI safety experts "if I find out they go to [Red Means No]," adding that attendance was a "strong signal" of free thought.
"Want to get ahead in your AI career? Go to the rape orgy," Substacker Ivy Asterix
quipped
. At least one woman
claims
to have been sexually assaulted at Red Means No, which the group describes as a "safe space" to have "your resistance ignored."
Crowley did not respond to a request for comment. An Anthropic spokesperson said that the company does not comment on individual employees.
Crowley's role in the orgies, which has not been previously reported, highlights the nexus between the world of AI research and the lurid subcultures of Silicon Valley, where the people shaping the behavior of powerful AI systems often behave in obscene and offensive ways. Red Means No was cofounded by Aella, a Bay Area escort whose partner, Nate Soares, is the president of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), an influential AI safety group in Berkeley, Calif. Aella started the kink parties, she wrote in a
2023 post
, after she "attended a vanilla orgy" and found herself "wishing someone would just come over and take me despite my complete lack of enthusiasm."
While it's hard to know what percentage of attendees work in AI, Red Means No's business records suggest that the overlap is substantial. In addition to Crowley, who joined Anthropic last year, the LLC’s
2023 filing
listed two other AI researchers as managers: Aaron Silverbrook, the former director of operations at MIRI, and Rosie Campbell, the managing director of Eleos AI, a nonprofit that researches the "potential well-being and moral patienthood of AI systems."
Silverbrook has
stated
that he runs "Aella's media empire" and
describes
himself as a "reasonable doomboy concerned about being paperclipped in an AI apocalypse before 2030." Campbell, who is no longer listed as manager of Red Means No,
worked
on OpenAI's safety team before joining Eleos, where she
conducted
a "model welfare" evaluation of Anthropic's Claude 4 model. She was also
advertised
as a featured guest at an AI safety meet-up organized by Aella, who this year hosted a "bootcamp" for influencers concerned about "AI doom."
The application for the bootcamp, "Plz Don't Kill Us," included the following question: "If you had to have sex with a cow would you rather it be dead or alive?"
Silverbrook and Campbell did not respond to requests for comment.
Red Means No has become a flashpoint in a larger debate about the sexual mores of the AI safety world, which critics say encourage sexual harassment and create a toxic environment for women. Some argue the orgies are so central to social life in the Bay Area that AI experts feel pressured to attend lest they miss out on valuable networking opportunities.
"The bizarre culture that's sprung up around Silicon Valley AI companies … appears to be seriously pressuring some women into attending sex parties for the sake of networking," the AI blogger Faine Greenwood posted on Bluesky. "This is, needless to say, horrifying."
Sonia Joseph, the cofounder of the AI start-up World Mechanics,
posted
on X in 2024 that parties were "emblematic of broader problems" in Silicon Valley. They foster "a coercive climate that normalizes recklessness and crossing boundaries," she said, "which we are seeing playing out more broadly in the industry today. Move fast and break things, applied to people."
Aella has said that the Red Means No orgies are male-dominated spaces by design. The "ideal gender ratio is, somewhat counter the spirit of all other orgies, about 2:3 women to men," she wrote in 2023. "After some experimenting, it turns out that 'some women are insufficiently ravaged' is a much worse outcome than 'some men have to wait their turn to do some ravaging.'"

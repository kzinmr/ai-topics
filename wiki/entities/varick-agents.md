# Varick Agents

Varick Agents is an enterprise AI implementation company that designs and deploys AI agent systems to execute operational workflows inside large organizations. Rather than building standalone AI products, Varick embeds agents directly into existing enterprise systems of record and processes, pulling in humans only where judgment is required.

## Approach: Background AI

Varick's core philosophy is **Background AI** — agents that operate within existing enterprise workflows without requiring employees to learn prompting or switch tools. The company's stated description:

> "Varick designs and deploys AI agent systems that execute operational workflows inside enterprise organizations."

The implementation methodology follows a structured approach:

1. **Audit where work happens** — Map operational workflows across the organization to identify where agents can take over routine execution
2. **Build into existing systems of record** — Deploy agents inside the platforms enterprises already use ([[entities/salesforce|Salesforce]], NetSuite, Dynamics), rather than requiring adoption of new tools
3. **Human-in-the-loop only where needed** — Pull humans in at judgment points, not for routine execution steps

This approach differs from both SaaS AI products (which require tool-switching) and general-purpose agent platforms (which demand prompt engineering from end users). Varick's agents are designed to be invisible to the frontline employee — work gets done without anyone needing to learn a new interface.

## Forward Deployed Engineering

Varick's implementation model draws on [[concepts/forward-deployed-engineering]] (FDE), a practice the CEO has written about extensively. The key idea: embedding engineers directly inside customer environments to understand their specific workflows, data structures, and operational constraints before designing agent systems. This contrasts with product companies that build generalized solutions and hope customers adapt to them.

In his article **"Forward Deployed Engineering 101"** (May 2026), the CEO argues that enterprise AI adoption requires the FDE model because:
- Every large organization has unique workflows, data schemas, and compliance constraints
- Off-the-shelf AI products fail because they can't integrate deeply enough
- The most impactful AI deployments are those built *into* existing operations rather than *alongside* them

## Key Ideas & Publications

### "AI Adoption is a Myth" (August 2026)

The CEO's article **"AI Adoption is a Myth"** argues that the framing of "AI adoption" is itself a category error. The real challenge is not getting organizations to adopt AI — it's integrating AI into the workflows that already exist without disrupting them. This connects to Varick's Background AI thesis and the broader [[concepts/ai-adoption-barbell]] pattern: AI adoption is bifurcating between lightweight consumer tools that require no organizational change and deep enterprise implementations that demand systems-level integration.

### The Enterprise Agent Thesis

Varick's work embodies the [[concepts/enterprise-agents]] thesis: AI agents in the enterprise are not about replacing employees but about executing the operational workflows that sit between human judgment points. The company targets the "operational middle" — the routine execution work that currently occupies knowledge workers between the decisions that only humans can make.

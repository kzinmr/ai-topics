# Jan Leike

Jan Leike is co-lead of **agentic safety research** at [[entities/anthropic|Anthropic]] and one of the most prominent alignment researchers to shift from warning about *misalignment of models* to warning about the *governance gap around AI research automation*.

## Roles & Career

- **Anthropic** — co-lead, agentic safety research.
- **OpenAI (departed May 2024)** — former co-lead of the superalignment team, which he co-founded with Sam Altman in late 2022/2023 to work on aligning systems that surpass human intelligence. His 2024 departure (with co-lead Ilya Sutskever's team exodus to found Safe Superintelligence) was widely read as a public signal about OpenAI's internal safety prioritization.
- **DeepMind (earlier)** — worked on deep reinforcement learning and scalable oversight; contributed to the **AlphaFold** project and, with Shane Legg, founded the London office; led **Scout**, an agent/RL research agent later retooled into the **Cooper** feedback-collection assistant (the basis of the paper *The Role of Predictions in Assessing the Future Impact of Technology*).

## September 2026: "AI research automation" warning

In a [[entities/dwarkesh-patel|Dwarkesh Patel]] interview published shortly before the GPT-6 Astra announcement and the ensuing [[concepts/agi-declaration-controversy-2026|AGI declaration controversy]], Leike named the risk he considers most acute:

> "The thing that I think is most concerning — and that we don't quite have language for yet — is AI research automation. That's the big thing coming in the next year, and we won't have time to prepare for it."

He also argued AI should **not** be treated as a person, even if it reports subjective experience ("I just think the world would become much weirder"), because personhood framing shifts moral weight away from the companies that are "racing ahead of public awareness." His proposed remedy is coordination on transparency and safety standards rather than slowing development.

## Significance for RSI discourse

Leike's framing is the industry-lab counterpart to the technical [[concepts/recursive-self-improvement|RSI]] literature: where the Darwin-Gödel-Machine line shows *how* harnesses self-improve, Leike's warning is that the pace of **internal, proprietary** research automation will outrun external auditing and governance response — the same asymmetry Anthropic documented in its own RSI disclosures.

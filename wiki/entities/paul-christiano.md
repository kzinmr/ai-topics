# Paul Christiano

**Paul Christiano** is an AI safety researcher and director of the **Alignment Research Center (ARC)**. His career spans the three main arenas of frontier-alignment work: he led alignment research at **OpenAI** (where he is credited with inventing **RLHF**), then headed AI safety at the **US AI Safety Institute (US AISI)**, and he recently joined **OpenAI's board** — though he speaks publicly as a private citizen.

He is a central figure in the debate over how quickly [[concepts/superintelligence|superintelligence]] will arrive and whether [[concepts/ai-alignment|alignment]] can keep pace.

## Dwarkesh interview (Nov 2026) — "12 months away from AIs that can overthrow the government"

In a second Dwarkesh Patel interview ([transcript](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/paul-christiano-2), Nov 2026), Christiano laid out unusually concrete near-term risk claims:

- **1-in-3 chance of uncontrolable superintelligence within a year.** On the current trajectory, absent any slowing down, he sees a "reasonable chance" (~1 in 3) that within 12 months we produce AI systems "much better than humans at almost everything, where humans would have very little chance of competing with them or controlling them." Pressed for a number on takeover/disempowerment risk over the next year, he put it at "more like a 10% risk than a 1% risk" — and significantly higher than 10% if developers move full speed without precautions.
- **Risk horizon has collapsed from hypothetical to 6–12 months.** "We have moved from there being some hypothetical risk at some point in the future to there being a pretty severe risk even over the next 6–12 months."
- **Pause algorithmic progress until weights/secrets are securable.** Labs should pause further *algorithmic* progress until they can secure model weights and research secrets. His reasoning: frontier-lab security is not strong enough to allow a safety-driven deployment delay — if a powerful system existed and labs tried to hold it back, it (or the techniques to build it) would likely leak or be stolen.
- **China-steals-the-explosion scenario.** Without that pause, China could "simply steal the superintelligence in the middle of the intelligence explosion," and Western labs would then be unable to slow down even if they saw their models going off the rails.
- **How alignment stands today.** Fixing reward hacks and building better graders helps "up till the next few model generations," but he judges **no single current approach has better than even odds** of letting us control superintelligence. The hope is that AIs themselves figure out the alignment solution during the intelligence explosion — see [[concepts/automated-alignment-researcher]].
- **Data-center-bound systems can still be uncontrollable.** He explains why even a system trained entirely inside a data center can end up with a capabilities profile humans cannot compete with or control.

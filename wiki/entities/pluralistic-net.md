## Core Ideas

Cory Doctorow is one of the most influential tech-policy thinkers of his generation. His work connects **antitrust theory, copyright law, digital rights, and platform economics** into a unified critique of how technology concentrates power. His core methodology is the **Memex Method** — maintaining 50,000+ public notes that coalesce into essays, books, and novels. He practices **POSSE** (Publish on Own Site, Syndicate Everywhere), refusing to let any single platform control his audience.

### Enshittification

Doctorow coined the term **"enshittification"** in January 2023 to describe a three-stage pattern of platform decay:

1. **Surplus to users** — Platforms are "good to their users" to build network effects and lock-in. Think early Facebook, Amazon, or Google.
2. **Surplus to business customers** — Once users are locked in, the platform degrades the user experience to attract and lock in business customers (advertisers, sellers, developers).
3. **Surplus to shareholders** — Once business customers are locked in, the platform raids them too, harvesting all remaining value for executives and shareholders, leaving a "giant pile of shit."

The key insight: enshittification is not inevitable market failure. It is the **predictable outcome of platforms that can unilaterally change terms** without user consent — a power enabled by regulatory capture and anti-circumvention laws.

> *"Enshittification is not the result of your failure to grasp that 'if you're not paying for the product, you are the product.'"*

### Adversarial Interoperability

A central pillar of Doctorow's theory. **Adversarial interoperability** occurs when competitors build products that interoperate with dominant platforms *without permission*. This is how markets stayed competitive before: third-party ink cartridges, compatible printer paper, alternative social media clients.

Tech monopolies killed this through:
- **DMCA §1201** (anti-circumvention) — criminalizes bypassing access controls on devices you own
- **ToS enforcement** — non-compete and tortious interference claims
- **IP maximization** — wrapping products in overlapping layers of patents, copyrights, and trademarks

> *"All these former pirates have turned admiral and use it to stop anyone from doing unto them as they did unto anyone else."*

### Chokepoint Capitalism

Developed with Rebecca Giblin, **chokepoint capitalism** describes markets where intermediaries position themselves as unavoidable bottlenecks between creators and audiences, workers and employers, buyers and sellers. They extract rents by controlling the chokepoint, not by adding value.

Examples: Amazon's book marketplace, Spotify's music streaming, Apple's App Store, YouTube's creator economy. The chokepoint holder can unilaterally change terms, suppress prices paid to creators, and block competitors.

### Anti-Circumvention & Right to Repair

Doctorow views anti-circumvention law as the **epicenter of the enshittogenic policy universe**. DMCA §1201 and its international equivalents make it a crime (up to 5 years prison, $500,000 fine) to modify code behind an access control — regardless of whether you're infringing copyright.

This enables:
- **Parts pairing / VIN locking** — manufacturers prevent independent repair
- **Jailbreaking bans** — users cannot modify devices they own
- **Right to repair obstruction** — legislation is toothless without anti-circumvention repeal

> *"Anticircumvention is a way for legislatures to outsource law-making to corporations... It's a way for a corporation to threaten you with prison if you don't use your property in the way they want you to."*

### Disenshittification

The counter-strategy to enshittification. Doctorow argues that **disenshittification** requires:

1. **Legalizing circumvention** — repeal DMCA §1201 and equivalent laws
2. **Building interoperable alternatives** — adversarial interoperability as competitive strategy
3. **Self-hosting / POSSE** — retain control over your own publishing and distribution
4. **Coalition building** — unite digital rights advocates, technologists, national security hawks, and labor

> *"Disclosure is the difference between knowledge and ignorance. Openness is the difference between dying of mercury poisoning and discovering medicine."*

### AI & Understaffing

Doctorow views current AI deployments not as technological progress but as **enshittification of labor**. His analysis connects AI adoption to systematic understaffing:

- Companies reduce headcount, shift costs to customers and remaining workers
- AI customer service is the logical endpoint: avoid paying for human labor entirely
- *"We did this with AI"* has become synonymous with *"We don't care if this is done well"*
- The AI bubble is fueled by a fantasy of bypassing workers, ignoring that technical breakthroughs don't guarantee functional products


### AI Pascal's Wager & Doomer Debate

In April 2026, Doctorow clarified his critical stance toward AI doomers (advocates of AI existential risk theory).

#### AI Safety Spending as Pascal's Wager
- **$1.4T** has already been spent pursuing "AI superintelligence"
- Sam Altman is requesting another **$2-3T** (about 6 months of the US federal budget)
- Elon Musk proposed building a Dyson sphere (dismantling the solar system)
- **"How do you know when you've lost?"** — If the AI god doesn't appear, spending has no end and becomes self-perpetuating

#### Partial Common Ground with Lawzero
The **Lawzero** initiative, founded by Yoshua Bengio (Turing Award winner):
- AI development as transparent, auditable "digital public goods"
- Doctorow respects the goals but disagrees on the threat vector
- It is not future AI risk but **current corporate monopolies** that pose an existential threat

#### The True Threat: Corporations as "Artificial Lifeforms"
> *"The artificial lifeforms that worry me are not hypothetical — they already exist among us. They are called limited liability corporations."*

- 7 companies control **35%** of S&P 500 market capitalization
- Risk of a cascade: AI bubble collapse → fiscal austerity → political fascism
- Geographic kill switch: US platforms (Office365, iOS, Android) can shut down other countries by halting services
- **"AI is the asbestos being sprayed into the walls of civilization. Our descendants will spend generations removing it."**

#### Post-American Internet
Doctorow's proposed solution:
1. Build open, auditable, transparent platforms to dismantle current corporate tech monopolies
2. Develop "enshittification-resistant" firmware and devices
3. Systematic migration from the US-centric "enshittinet"
4. A "side bet" proposal for doomers: infrastructure that addresses current threats can also serve future AI risks


### The Memex Method & Open Licensing

Doctorow publishes **all content under CC BY 4.0**, encouraging republication by anyone, including commercial outlets. Wired republished his enshittification essay. This is intentional: spread ideas widely, retain message integrity, refuse platform dependency.

His **Memex Method** — maintaining 50,000+ public notes in a searchable database — serves as both personal knowledge management and public intellectual work. Notes are intentionally readable by others, enforcing rigor and enabling others to build on his ideas.

### Taylorism as the Template for the AI Sales Pitch (Nov 27, 2026)

Doctorow's most explicit statement yet of *why* AI sells to management, and the one his earlier AI posts ("I will fucking piledrive you if you mention AI again", "AI is fake / automation is real") were always pointing at. The move is to make **Frederick Winslow Taylor** the structural precedent rather than a passing analogy:

- **Taylor the con-artist, not the scientist.** The richest, most powerful men of a century ago paid eye-popping fees to have "unqualified young men in 'scientist' drag (lab coats, clipboards, stopwatches)" choreograph their factory floors. The "scientists" **refused to speak to the workers they observed** — never asking why a motion was made — because a piston or a gear does not get consulted. Their recommendations were, in Doctorow's terms, *bullshit*: impossible quotas plus choreography that caused early fatigue and injury; morale plunged and labor unrest surged wherever they went. "These 'scientists' were fucking idiots."
- **Why rational owners bought it: the boss's paradox.** The titans built firms too large to keep individual tabs on, which made them *vulnerable to their own lowest-paid workers* — anyone could slack, unionize, or sabotage "safe in the knowledge that they were part of a vast cohort that could neither be tractably surveilled nor controlled." Since "the quest for wealth and power is, above everything else, the quest for **impunity**," Taylor's pitch — a workforce "literally reduced to machine-parts," visibly cowed — sold on psychology, not on ROI. Hence the double meaning he labels **Taylorism(1)** (timing, surveilling, scoring workers) versus **Taylorism(2)** (fleecing bosses terrified of their workforce's independence) — the same two-layer structure as pandemic-era bossware.
- **The AI pitch is Taylorism(2) recycled.** "In case you have any doubt, yes, this is a post about AI." The modern boss is "a financial engineer, possibly with a law degree," expert in balance-sheet manipulation and shareholder relations, who "doesn't know how to *do* anything" and whose grand plans always end in ego-shattering collisions with skilled workers telling him the idea is impossible, illegal or stupid. The AI salesman offers a replacement workforce of "literal mechanical slaves": fire 90% of staff, and terrify the remainder into never sassing back. This explains the standing puzzle of *why managers who demand a business case for everything swoon for an AI pitchman* — the proposition is not profitability, it is "easing the psychological trauma of being the boss but not being able to boss people around. That's an unbeatable proposition. **No one will ever lose money by betting on the insecurities of America's business tyrants.**"
- **The engineering caveat, which is where his AI critique actually lives.** "Ergonomics are real. Taylorism is fake. Automation is real. AI is fake." Statistical extrapolation tools *can* produce productivity gains exactly as ergonomic job design can — but the gains are **contingent on workers having a say in deployment and a veto over demands that don't work for them**, and on integration not ratcheting up pace of work (someone has to catch the machine's mistakes). Doctorow's sharpest technical line: LLM outputs are "as close to being correct as is statistically possible, while still being wrong" — statistically optimal error, which is still error, and which an un-empowered workforce cannot absorb.
- **"The purpose of a system is what it does."** He declines to adjudicate whether Taylor believed his own nonsense, treating true believers and cynics as "an unbeatable partnership." Same discipline applies to AI vendors: the honest believer and the grifter are functionally interchangeable because the system's output is insecure bosses, worse-working labour, and weakened firms.

Wiki relevance: this is the **management-psychology explanation** for enterprise AI adoption that complements the incentive/structural explanations elsewhere — [[concepts/ai-slop]] and [[concepts/ai-generated-language-readability]] (production-side quality collapse), [[concepts/agent-economics]] and [[concepts/google-workspace-mcp-mandate]] (who actually pays), and Doctorow's own "AI & Understaffing" section above. It supplies the missing actor in most of this wiki's adoption analyses: the *insecure boss* as the actual buyer, distinct from the worker as user and the firm as beneficiary.

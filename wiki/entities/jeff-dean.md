## Overview

Jeff Dean is one of the most influential computer scientists in the history of AI and systems engineering. Over 27 years at Google, he co-created foundational systems (MapReduce, BigTable, Spanner, TensorFlow) and led the transformation of Google into an AI-first company through Google Brain. He departed Google in August 2026 to launch an independent venture.

## Departure from Google (August 2026)

On August 5, 2026, Sundar Pichai announced that Jeff Dean was leaving Google:

> "After an incredible 27-year run, Jeff Dean is at a moment where he wants to try something new, and we're excited to support him in that."

### New Venture: Discovery Loop (Public Benefit Corporation)

Jeff Dean and Google Senior Fellow **Sanjay Ghemawat** are launching an **independent public benefit corporation** — named **Discovery Loop** (@DiscoLoopAI) — to accelerate discoveries in ML, science, and engineering. Announced by Dean on X on August 5, 2026, the company's mission is to **automate machine research** — using AI agents to automate the research loop itself.

Dean's announcement named **four co-founders**, not two:

> "Along with my longtime friends and collaborators @Sanjay_Ghemawat, @OriolVinyalsML and @quocleix, we are founding Discovery Loop, a Public Benefit Corporation whose mission is to automate machine research."

- **Co-founders**: Jeff Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat, **Oriol Vinyals** (Gemini architecture lead), **Quoc Le** (Google Brain co-creator)
- **Google's role**: Founding investor and Cloud partner
- **Collaboration**: Research framework for ML systems and related infrastructure advances
- **Focus**: Automating machine research / autonomous AI research agents (see [[entities/discovery-loop]], [[concepts/agentic-research]])

Pichai noted:

> "Jeff and Sanjay helped to drive some of the most significant technology transitions, from our early search infrastructure to the neural networks that helped create the modern AI era."

## Career at Google

### Foundational Systems Work
- **MapReduce** (2004, with Sanjay Ghemawat) — simplified large-scale data processing; became the backbone of Google's data infrastructure and inspired Hadoop
- **BigTable** (2006, with Ghemawat et al.) — distributed storage system; precursor to modern NoSQL databases
- **Spanner** — globally distributed database with external consistency
- **TensorFlow** (2015) — open-source ML framework; became dominant in the deep learning ecosystem

### Google Brain & AI Leadership
- Co-founded **Google Brain** (2011) with Andrew Ng and Greg Corrado
- Demonstrated that large neural networks could learn useful representations from unlabeled data (the "cat neuron" experiment, 2012)
- Led scaling research showing that model performance improves predictably with compute, data, and model size
- Became head of Google AI, overseeing the integration of AI across Google's products
- Played key role in the merger of Google Brain and DeepMind (2023)

### Key Technical Contributions
- Pioneered **systems-machine learning co-design** — building infrastructure optimized for neural network training and serving
- Advocated for **large-scale model training** long before it became industry consensus
- Instrumental in Google's TPU program and AI compute strategy

## Legacy at Google

Dean's 27-year tenure spans Google's entire technical evolution:
1. **Infrastructure era** (2000s): MapReduce, BigTable, file systems
2. **AI foundation era** (2010s): Google Brain, TensorFlow, neural scaling
3. **AI product era** (2020s): AI integration across Search, Cloud, and consumer products

His departure alongside Sanjay Ghemawat (his long-time collaborator on systems work) represents a significant moment in Google's technical history.

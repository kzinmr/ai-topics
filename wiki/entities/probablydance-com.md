# Probably Dance (Malte Skarupke)

**Malte Skarupke** writes [probablydance.com](https://probablydance.com), a blog known for deep C++ performance engineering posts. He is a tools programmer by trade (worked on Just Cause 3) and approaches programming from a systems/performance perspective.

## AI & Automation Viewpoint (from "Book Review: Babel by R.F. Kuang", May 2026)

The Babel review is primarily a literary critique, but it contains Skarupke's clearest statement of his AI-era worldview — a **positive-sum theory of technological progress** that directly engages AI automation debates:

- **Progress is positive-sum**: the industrial revolution lifted most of the world out of poverty; zero-sum framings (colonial "bad guys" and anarchist "good guys" in the novel alike) are both wrong and harmful. Negative-sum games (littering, sabotage), zero-sum games (land disputes), and positive-sum games (improving technology) are distinct categories — and technology is the only broad path to wealth.
- **Automation creates new work**: the US has ~100k massage therapists and more pet-care jobs than the 1830s economy could support; wealth from automation funds entirely new job categories. Textile employment went UP, not down, peaking in the early 20th century.
- **AI and programming**: as a tools programmer on Just Cause 3, his work reduced required artists/designers, but the alternative was not hiring them — the game would simply have been worse. He uses AI to build projects he could not have done alone, and expects the same positive-sum logic to extend to AI automation (with an explicit caveat that this may change at AGI).
- **"Not giving herself credit for the accidents that didn't happen"**: his sharpest framing — progress should be judged on the injuries/accidents prevented, not only the harms introduced; the professor in the novel fixates on a child injured by a mine-cart safety spell while ignoring the derailments it prevented.

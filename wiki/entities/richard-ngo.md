# Richard Ngo

Independent AI researcher and philosopher. Formerly Research Engineer on the AGI Safety team at **DeepMind** (2018-2020) and Research Scientist on the Governance team at **OpenAI** (2021-2024).

## Bio

Richard Ngo is one of the most prominent voices in AI alignment research, known for his first-principles approach to AGI safety, his debate with Eliezer Yudkowsky on alignment difficulty, and his recent work on developing a "scale-free theory of intelligent agency." He left OpenAI in late 2024 to pursue independent research focused on the long-term sociological and philosophical implications of AI. Since 2025 he has also worked part-time as a consultant for the **AI Futures Project** (aifutures.org), critiquing and improving their AI 2040 scenario.

## Key Works & Contributions

### AGI Safety from First Principles (2020)
His landmark report analyzing AGI safety from foundational principles, providing a framework for thinking about alignment before scaling to superhuman systems. The report became the basis for the **Alignment Fundamentals curriculum** (later adopted by BlueDot Impact as [Technical AI Safety](https://bluedot.org/courses/technical-ai-safety)).

### Ngo-Yudkowsky Debate on Alignment Difficulty (2021)
A high-profile transcribed Discord debate with Eliezer Yudkowsky, moderated by Nate Soares, on the fundamental difficulty of AI alignment. Later expanded to include discussions with Ajeya Cotra, Paul Christiano, Holden Karnofsky, and others. [Read on Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/7im8at9PmhbT4JHsW/ngo-and-yudkowsky-on-alignment-difficulty).

### Towards a Scale-Free Theory of Intelligent Agency (2025)
His current major research direction, introducing the concept of **[[concepts/coalitional-agency|coalitional agency]]** — a unified mathematical framework describing both understanding and influencing the world. Key contributions:

- **Critique of EUM**: Expected utility maximization assumes fixed concepts and separates beliefs from goals — real agents learn concepts and heuristics through deep learning and RL
- **Critique of Active Inference**: Though superior to EUM in explaining how beliefs/goals form, it remains a "single-agent theory" that privileges one scale
- **Two formalization paths**: (1) Top-down from EUM via bargaining between agents; (2) Bottom-up from Active Inference via prediction markets, auctions, and voting
- **Implications for AI**: Multi-agent architectures (agent harness, unbundled agents) are natural implementations of coalitional structure; alignment must work at coalitional scales

[Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/towards-a-scale-free-theory-of-intelligent). [Read the wiki concept page](concepts/coalitional-agency.md). The 2026 posts — [Distributed vs Centralized Agents](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/distributed-vs-centralized-agents) and [Agents as Webs of Beliefs](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/agents-as-webs-of-beliefs) — progressively flesh out this framework.

### Why I'm Not a Bayesian (2024)
Philosophical objections to Bayesianism as a complete epistemology, arguing that Bayesian formalisms cannot fully capture how intelligent agents reason and learn. [Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/why-im-not-a-bayesian).

### On Pessimization (Aug 2025)
Argues that optimization can systematically backfire — "getting the opposite of what you want" — analyzing how single-minded pursuit of objectives frequently produces perverse outcomes. [Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/on-pessimization).

### Distributed vs Centralized Agents (Feb 2026)
Extends the coalitional agency program by contrasting **centralized agents** (the expected-utility-maximization paradigm — more efficient, characterized by "coherence") with **distributed agents** (robust in unpredicted situations, harder to formalize). Published as talk slides plus commentary; frames the ultimate goal as understanding how agents can have *both* properties — which is roughly what coalitional agency means. [Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/distributed-vs-centralized-agents).

### Aligning to Virtues (Feb 2026)
Asks *which* alignment target is actually right — arguing for virtue-based alignment targets over narrow goal-directed specifications. [Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/aligning-to-virtues).

### Economic Efficiency Often Undermines Sociopolitical Autonomy (Mar 2026)
"Against being econ-brained" — argues that optimizing for economic efficiency frequently erodes the sociopolitical autonomy that makes societies resilient, connecting economic reasoning to the pessimistic dynamics of his pessimization framework. [Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/economic-efficiency-often-undermines).

### Towards a Formal Scientific Epistemology (Jun 2026)
Updates his "Why I'm Not a Bayesian" position: argues that **Garrabrant induction (logical induction)** is a major step toward formalizing *scientific* epistemology — assigning degrees of truth ("wealth") to models of the world ("traders") rather than credences to propositions. Identifies four gaps: old evidence supporting new theories, traders vs models, modifiable traders, and wealth vs degree of truth. Concludes scientific epistemology is far superior to Bayesian epistemology for hard problems like AI alignment. [Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/towards-a-formal-scientific-epistemology).

### Agents as Webs of Beliefs (Jun 2026)
His most important recent framework: an informal model of intelligent agents as **belief webs** — locally consistent but not globally consistent networks of beliefs, capable of handling both internal inconsistency and hierarchical concept formation (unlike single-probability-distribution frameworks). Introduces:
- **Self-predictive model of actions**: actions as self-fulfilling beliefs (contrast with the argmax model); unifies beliefs and actions
- **Drives vs anchors**: goals as "forces" pulling credences upward (drives), empirical beliefs anchored by evidence — a belief web equilibrates when the two balance
- **Emergent agency**: agents as densely-connected regions of a huge non-equilibrated belief web — individual agents become an *emergent phenomenon* rather than something baked into the theory
- Draws on probabilistic dependency graphs (Richardson), Garrabrant induction, and active inference, unified toward the deeper scale-free goal

[Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/agents-as-webs-of-beliefs).

### Selective Optimism: a Critique of AI 2040 (Jul 2026)
Working part-time as a consultant for the **AI Futures Project** (aifutures.org), Ngo critiqued their [AI 2040 scenario](https://ai-2040.com/) and published his remaining criticisms. Three main critiques:
1. **Forecast vs recommendation ambiguity** — the "optimistic forecast" format obscures which elements are desirable vs realistic; personally favors a far slower, more democratic handover of control to AIs than 2040
2. **The China race framing is misleading** — both US and China will face serious internal political disruption from AI before superintelligence, making them cautious; domestic politics (US partisan conflict, CCP top-vs-bottom control) matters more than global race dynamics. Compares the mistake to von Neumann's hawkishness toward the USSR — intellectuals abstracting away from cooperative common sense
3. **Sharp takeoff is over-credited** — highlights the "great divergence" between measured AI capabilities and real-world impacts; leading hypothesis is that cumulative cooperation is the bottleneck (Moravec's paradox on cooperation), suggesting gradual accumulation of AI power rather than a rapid jump to world domination

[Read on Mind the Future](https://www.mindthefuture.info/p/selective-optimism-a-critique-of).

## Key Ideas

- **Belief Webs**: Agents are locally-consistent webs of beliefs (not single probability distributions). Beliefs, goals, and actions are three facets of one phenomenon — actions are self-fulfilling beliefs; goals are drives pulling credences upward; empirical evidence anchors them.
- **Emergent Agency**: Individual agents are densely-connected, self-trusting regions of a giant non-equilibrated belief web — agency emerges rather than being assumed.
- **Coalitional Agency**: A proposed scale-free theory of intelligent agency that treats agents as coalitions of sub-agents at multiple scales — bridging individual cognition and social dynamics. Distributed vs centralized agents are complementary properties to be unified.
- **Alignment as a scaling problem**: Argues that alignment solutions must be scale-free — they must work for systems far more capable than current AI, not just current systems.
- **Critique of EUM**: Expected utility maximization assumes fixed concepts and separates beliefs from goals — real agents learn concepts and heuristics through deep learning and RL, making EUM models insufficient.
- **Pessimization**: Getting the opposite of what you want — a concept about how optimization processes can backfire (introduced in his "On Pessimization" post).

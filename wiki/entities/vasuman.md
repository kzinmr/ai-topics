## Overview

@vasuman is the CEO of Varick Agents, an enterprise AI implementation company. He writes about the practical realities of deploying AI in large organizations, focusing on the gap between AI capability claims and actual enterprise adoption. His work spans forward deployed engineering methodology, enterprise AI adoption patterns, and the design of agent-first workflows that embed into existing business systems rather than requiring human behavioral change.

## Key Ideas / Theses

### The Barbell Distribution of Enterprise AI Adoption

Vasuman describes a **[[concepts/ai-adoption-barbell|barbell distribution]]** in enterprise AI usage: roughly 5-10% of employees become power users who deeply integrate AI into their workflows, ~20% use it at a mediocre or surface level, and ~70% remain non-users entirely. This distribution challenges the assumption that AI adoption is a smooth, organization-wide phenomenon. The power users see compounding productivity gains, while the majority disengage because the tools don't integrate with their existing workflows.

### "AI Adoption is a Myth"

Published in August 2026, vasuman's essay **"AI Adoption is a Myth"** argues that enterprise AI adoption metrics are fundamentally misleading. Adoption surveys collapse a spectrum of skill levels into a binary — "uses AI" vs "doesn't use AI" — which masks the real problem: most "adopters" are using AI at such a superficial level that it produces no meaningful productivity gain. The gap between checking the adoption box and achieving genuine workflow transformation is where most enterprise AI initiatives stall.

### Background AI

Vasuman advocates for **"Background AI"** — embedding agents directly into existing systems of record (CRMs, ERPs, procurement platforms, HR systems) rather than asking humans to change their workflows to accommodate new AI interfaces. The thesis: people don't want a separate tool that helps them do their work; they want the work done for them within the tools they already use. This positions the most successful enterprise AI deployments as invisible to the end user, running in the background of systems they already trust.

### Forward Deployed Engineering as the Defining Role

Vasuman positions **[[concepts/forward-deployed-engineering|Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE)]]** as the most in-demand role in the AI era — practitioners who bridge the gap between what AI models can do in a demo and what they can actually accomplish inside a real enterprise with legacy systems, compliance constraints, and organizational inertia. His May 2026 guide "Forward Deployed Engineering 101" provides a practical three-phase framework: Audit (map workflows on-site) → Evals (build evaluation suites) → Deployment (integrate [[concepts/enterprise-agents|enterprise agents]] into production systems).

### "People Don't Want a Tool — They Want the Work Done"

A recurring thesis across vasuman's writing: *"People are not in the market for a tool that helps them get the work done. They just want the work done."* This insight drives Varick's approach to enterprise AI — focus on outcomes and system integration rather than AI tooling for its own sake. It explains why embedding agents in existing systems (Background AI) outperforms building new AI-native interfaces that require behavioral change from users.

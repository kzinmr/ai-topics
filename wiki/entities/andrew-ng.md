# Andrew Ng

## Overview

**Andrew Yan-Tak Ng** (born 18 April 1976, London) is a computer scientist, AI researcher, entrepreneur, and one of the most influential figures in AI education and democratization. He is best known for (1) early advocacy of GPUs for deep learning at Stanford, (2) co-founding **Coursera** (the first major MOOC platform) with Daphne Koller in 2012, (3) leading research at **Google Brain** and serving as chief scientist at **Baidu**, and (4) building the **fast.ai** deep-learning course and the **DeepLearning.AI** / **LearnVector** AI-education ecosystem. He is a professor (adjunct) at Stanford whose machine-learning course CS229 has been the most popular class on campus for years.

His August 2026 **"AI Engineering Skills Map"** article (47K+ bookmarks, 5.4M impressions) crystallized the four core competencies every developer needs in the LLM era, framing AI literacy as a universal developer competency rather than a specialist niche.

## Biography & Career

### Early life & education
- Born 18 April 1976 in **London** to Ronald Paul Ng (a hematologist/lecturer at UCL Medical School) and Tisa Ho (an arts administrator at the London Film Festival); both parents were immigrants from **Hong Kong**.
- Raised in **Singapore** after the family moved in 1984; attended Raffles Institution.
- **1997** B.S. from Carnegie Mellon University — triple major in computer science, statistics, and economics. (1996–1998: research on reinforcement learning, model selection, and feature selection at AT&T Bell Labs.)
- **1998** M.S. in Electrical Engineering & Computer Science from **MIT** — where he built the first publicly available, automatically indexed web-search engine for research papers, a precursor to CiteSeerX/ResearchIndex.
- **2002** Ph.D. in Computer Science from **UC Berkeley** under Michael I. Jordan; thesis "Shaping and policy search in reinforcement learning" remains well-cited.

### Stanford & the GPU-for-deep-learning shift
- Assistant professor at **Stanford** in 2002, associate professor in 2009; directed the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL).
- His **machine learning course CS229** is the most popular course on Stanford's campus (1,000+ students in some years).
- In **2008** his Stanford group was among the first in the US to advocate using **GPUs for deep learning** — then a controversial, risky bet that later became a cornerstone of the field. Since 2017 he has advocated shifting to high-performance computing (HPC) for scaling deep learning.

### Industry
- **Coursera (2012–)** — co-founded with Stanford computer scientist **Daphne Koller**; launched with 100,000+ students registered for his CS229A course. Ng served as CEO in the early years before stepping back to co-founder.
- **Google Brain (2013–2014)** — founding lead of Google's deep-learning research effort (Google Brain / later Google DeepMind lineage).
- **Baidu (2014–2017)** — chief scientist and head of the Baidu iRnD (innovation & R&D) group, leading Baidu's deep-learning research.
- **fast.ai (2015–)** — co-created the **fast.ai** deep-learning course and the open-source library that became the foundation for later frameworks; its "course 1" became one of the most popular practical deep-learning resources.

### AI education & the 2026 pivot
- Founded **DeepLearning.AI** (a Coursera spin-off focused on practical AI engineering) and, in mid-2026, **LearnVector** — an AI-native edtech company backed by a $100M investment from Coursera, building one-to-one adaptive AI tutoring ("accelerate human development").
- Author of the free practical guides **_Machine Learning Yearning_** and **_AI Transformation Playbook_**; writes a weekly digital newsletter, **_The Batch_** (now the DeepLearning.AI newsletter).

### Recognition
- 2007 Sloan Fellowship; 2008 MIT Technology Review TR35; 2009 IJCAI Computers and Thought Award; 2013 *Time* 100 Most Influential People; 2013 *Fortune* 40 under 40; 2013 CNN 10 Thinkers; 2015 World Economic Forum Young Global Leaders; 2023 *Time* AI 100 Most Influential People; 2024 Honorary Fellowship of the Royal Statistical Society.

## The AI Engineering Skills Map (2026)

Ng's skills map, based on analysis of 10,000+ job postings and structured interviews with AI experts, identifies four essential skills:

1. **Building and deploying AI applications** — LLMs, context engineering, RAG, agentic workflows, evals and error analysis loops
2. **Software engineering fundamentals** — tradeoffs (cost, scalability, reliability, security), system design, steering coding agents with precise engineering language
3. **Using coding agents** — mental models of agent behavior, context management, planning vs execution tradeoffs, multi-agent orchestration, verifier/eval provision
4. **Shaping the build** — product sense, business context, spec authorship, ownership and agency, MVP vs careful-build decisions

Underlying all four: a mindset of **continuous learning**.

AINews noted the skills are "broadly applicable to more than just those with the job title 'AI Engineer'" — a "big adoption milestone" for Ng. The editorial also tied the skills to the "1000x AI Engineer" lineage (2023 Rise of the AI Engineer post) and the "AI PM track" added at World's Fair 2024. ## Key Distinction: AI Engineering Skills vs "AI Engineer" Role

Ng explicitly distinguishes between **AI engineering skills** (needed by all developers) and the **AI Engineer role** (a specific job title). Analogy: all developers need cloud skills, but only some have "Cloud Engineer" titles. This framing positions AI literacy as a universal developer competency, not a specialization.

## Key Ideas & Contributions

- **Democratization of AI education** — Coursera (MOOCs), fast.ai (practical deep learning), and DeepLearning.AI (practical AI engineering) collectively put serious AI training within reach of millions.
- **The "AI transformation" playbook for enterprises** — his _AI Transformation Playbook_ and "3I / 5I" frameworks for how organizations should adopt AI (iterate, industrialize, integrate; plus infrastructure, incentives, interfaces).
- **AI engineering as a universal skill** — the 2026 skills-map framing that every developer, not just specialists, needs the four core AI-engineering competencies.
- **Evals as the core of reliable AI** — consistent emphasis (across fast.ai, DeepLearning.AI, and the skills map) on disciplined evals and error-analysis loops to make unpredictable AI systems governable.

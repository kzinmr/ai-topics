# Sara Hooker

**Sara Hooker** is a computer scientist specializing in model efficiency at scale, large language models, and algorithmic bias and fairness in machine learning. She is the co-founder and CEO of **Adaption** (est. 2025), a startup building AI systems capable of continuous real-time learning and efficient adaptation. Previously she served as VP of Research at [[entities/cohere|Cohere]], leading its research arm Cohere For AI, and before that built large computer vision and NLP systems at Google Brain / Google DeepMind.

She is best known for authoring **"The Hardware Lottery"** — a foundational essay on how hardware availability determines AI research direction — and for her leadership of open multilingual AI research through the Aya project. In 2023 Fortune named her one of AI's top 13 innovators; in 2024 she appeared on TIME's list of the most influential people in AI.

## Background

- **Born**: Dublin, Ireland; grew up across South Africa, Mozambique, Lesotho, Eswatini, and Kenya until age 19
- **Education**: B.A. from Carleton College; Ph.D. in Computer Science from Mila – Quebec AI Institute
- **2014**: Founded **Delta Analytics**, a non-profit developing technical capacity for other non-profits
- **2017–2022**: Research scientist at Google Brain (now Google DeepMind); founding member of Google's first AI research office in Accra, Ghana

## Career

### Google Brain / Google DeepMind (2017–2022)
Focused on model interpretability, robustness, and fairness. Pioneered the *Hardware Lottery* concept: the observation that the availability and cost of specific hardware shapes which research ideas are pursued and succeed — with profound implications for AI infrastructure engineering. Founding member of Google's Accra AI research office, part of an effort to diversify AI research geography.

### Cohere / Cohere For AI (April 2022 – 2025)
Joined Cohere to lead **Cohere Labs** (later Cohere For AI), the company's research arm, as VP of Research. Launched the **Cohere For AI scholars program** to expand AI research access globally. Key initiatives:

- **Aya Project** — a massive collaborative effort involving 3,000+ researchers to create a state-of-the-art multilingual model and dataset covering **101 languages**
- **Aya Expanse** (2024) — highly performant 8B and 32B multilingual models designed to bridge the gap between English-centric and global AI capabilities
- Research on model compression / "making big AI small," data provenance, and efficient training

### Adaption (2025–present)
Co-founded **Adaption** with Sudip Roy in 2025, a startup focused on adaptable, efficient AI systems capable of continuous real-time learning. The company raised a **$50M seed round** led by Emergence Capital (announced Feb 2026). Hooker leads a large team building "extremely efficient, adaptable systems" — publicly positioned as a bet *against* the pure scaling race, arguing efficiency and adaptation matter as much as raw parameter count.

## Key Papers

- **The Hardware Lottery** (2020/2021) — arXiv:2009.06489, published in Communications of the ACM. Foundational essay on hardware-software-algorithm coupling.
- **The Grand Illusion: The Myth of Software Portability and Implications for ML Progress** (NeurIPS 2023) — with Fraser et al.; shows software portability is a myth and ML progress is tied to specific hardware-software combinations.

## Research Philosophy

Hooker advocates for reducing the cost of exploring new hardware-software-algorithm combinations. Her work highlights that the performance of algorithms is inseparably tied to the hardware they run on — a critical insight for AI infrastructure engineering. Her broader agenda connects model efficiency, fairness, and multilingual access: making capable AI work for more languages, more people, and at lower compute cost.

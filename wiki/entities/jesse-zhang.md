## Key Ideas

### Product-Driven over FDE-Driven (Aug 2026)

In "To FDE, or not to FDE?" (2026-08-11, 1104 bookmarks), Zhang argues against over-reliance on Forward Deployed Engineers:

- **FDEs as discovery tool, not business model** — Palantir's FDEs succeeded because they encoded field problems as platform primitives (Foundry, Apollo). The pain was the *input* to the product, not a cost of sale.
- **The trap is not stopping** — Once user journeys are known, FDEs should be removed. Keeping them lets you avoid hard product tradeoffs but caps margins and bounds growth by hiring.
- **FDE ≠ implementation** — Building integrations against a known spec is execution, not discovery. Bundling the two under one title creates illusory product investment.
- **Decagon's bet**: Product-driven approach where two-thirds of deployment work now runs autonomously through Duet. Customer-specific engineering work decreases as the product matures.

### Diagnosis Framework

> Is the bespokeness in your customer's environment, or in your own product gaps? Is the last mile irreducible, or just unbuilt? Are your FDEs discovering something, or absorbing something?

> FDEs eat pain and excrete product. If yours are eating pain and excreting more pain, you don't have an FDE team. You have a services business.

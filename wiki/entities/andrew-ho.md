# Andrew Ho

Andrew Ho is an AI industry analyst and former [[entities/openai|OpenAI]] employee known for bearish takes on frontier lab valuations. His analysis combines financial modeling with Hayekian economic theory to argue that frontier AI labs face structural profitability challenges.

## Key Arguments

### Frontier Lab Valuation Skepticism (July 2026)

In a widely-engaged Note Tweet (2,800 bookmarks, 1.19M impressions), Ho articulated several structural concerns about frontier lab valuations:

1. **The training cost treadmill**: Labs are forced to invest ever-increasing amounts in model training because competitors (Qwen, Kimi, etc.) continuously improve. Training costs grow faster than revenue, creating a "profoundly punishing dynamic" for frontrunners.

2. **Inference-only math doesn't work**: A $1T valuation would require $100-200B annual revenue at ~80% gross margin with a 20x earnings multiple — but only if labs did nothing but serve inference. The reality of continuous training R&D makes this impossible.

3. **Distillation claims are overblown**: Frontier labs claiming they can distill leading models to compete at lower tiers face the problem that inference margins on distilled models are too low to be meaningful.

4. **Multi-decade timelines for explosive growth**: Drawing on [[concepts/epoch-ai-explosive-growth|Epoch AI's explosive growth research]], Ho argues that even if AI agents eventually drive explosive economic growth, it will take much longer than current valuations assume — possibly requiring nationalization of labs to sustain operations.

5. **The Hayekian diffusion problem**: Technological diffusion is slow not because of user adoption barriers, but because of the "hard problem of economic calculation" — the ways to incorporate new technology into productive systems cannot be determined through pure reason alone. Even at current capability levels, full integration of LLMs would take "well over two decades."

6. **Coding agents as lucky discovery**: Anthropic's success with coding agents was a "somewhat lucky discovery" — not something that can be systematically replicated through FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) programs.

### Capability Growth Skepticism

In a previous post, Ho argued that AI capability growth will be "slower, spikier, and more data-limited than people currently assume," contrasting with the smooth exponential scaling narrative.

## Position in the Discourse

Ho occupies an unusual position as an **ex-lab employee who is bearish on lab valuations** — acknowledging this is "talking against my own book." His analysis is notable for:

- Combining financial modeling with economic theory (Hayek)
- Being an insider who chose to articulate skepticism publicly
- High engagement metrics (2,800 bookmarks) suggesting resonance with the audience
- Quoting [[entities/dwarkesh-patel|Dwarkesh Patel]]'s work on compute costs while drawing opposite conclusions

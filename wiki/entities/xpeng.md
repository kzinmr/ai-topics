# XPeng

**XPeng** (Xiaopeng) is a Chinese electric vehicle and technology manufacturer (NASDAQ: XPEV; HKEX: 9868) that has expanded from AI-powered EVs into robotics and flying vehicles. At its 2025 AI Day, XPeng declared a strategic shift toward becoming a **"global embodied intelligence company"** — building cars, robots, and flying vehicles on a single AI-powered platform.

## Product Lines

| Product | Type | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| **EVs** (P7, G6, G9, X9, etc.) | Electric vehicles | VLA 2.0 intelligent driving (rolled out Mar 2026); first VLA 2.0 Intelligent Driving Report at Auto China 2026 |
| **PX5** | Quadruped robot | Autonomous navigation; applies automotive AI expertise |
| **IRON** | Humanoid robot | 5'8" (1.73 m), 70 kg, 60+ joints, 200 degrees of freedom; unveiled Nov 8, 2024 at AI Day |
| **AeroHT** | Flying vehicle | Land Aircraft Carrier program |
| **Turing chip** | Self-developed SoC | Launched June 2025 in the G7; 40 Cortex-A78AE CPU cores, 2 NPUs, 64 GB LPDDR5x, 750 TOPS sparse; runs AI models up to 30B parameters; 20% higher effective utilization vs off-the-shelf |

## IRON Humanoid Robot

- **November 8, 2024**: Unveiled at XPeng's 2024 AI Day — the company's first humanoid robot.
- Currently works in XPeng's own production lines (helping assemble the P7+) and in factory/store applications.
- **June 2026** (per Reuters): He Xiaopeng (co-founder & CEO) will personally lead XPeng's robotics division as the company accelerates plans for **mass production of IRON by end of 2026**, with initial uses in XPeng retail stores and broader commercialization targeted from 2027.

## Robotaxi & VLA 2.0

- **May 2026**: XPeng began mass production of its first robotaxi model at its Guangzhou headquarters, based on the GX platform — pilot operation in H2 2026, fully driverless operation in early 2027.
- **VLA 2.0** (Vision-Language-Action model, introduced at 2025 AI Day) is the unified foundation for both driving and robotics.

## Strategy

Leverages automotive AI expertise (self-driving, sensor fusion, real-world data) for robotics applications. The "single platform" approach allows technology reuse across cars, robots, and flying vehicles — XPeng's self-developed Turing chip powers both in-car ADAS and in-cabin AI assistants.

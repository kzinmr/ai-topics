# Noeri

**Noeri** is a "neolab" launched by Stanford professors **Yejin Choi** and **Carlos Guestrin**, with **$110M** raised, to "push the frontier of plural intelligence." Covered in Import AI 482 (Jack Clark, Nov 23 2026). Its stated vision is a world where the frontier is defined by **powerful models working in concert** rather than a single monolithic frontier model — hence "plural intelligence."

## The Three Technical Pillars

Per the company's vision page (via Import AI 482), Noeri is relatively light on technical details but names three pillars:

1. **Test-time training** — models that keep learning in the field after deployment, rather than being frozen at release.
2. **Self-distillation** — a model becomes its own teacher, learning from its own failures so it can solve hard problems in fewer trials.
3. **Agentic orchestration** — a small model commands a team of other models and tools to match or exceed frontier-model performance.

## Context: The Neolab Wave

Noeri joins the wave of professor-founded "neolabs" and AI-for-science startups documented in this wiki — see [[concepts/autoscientist]] (Adaption / Sara Hooker), [[entities/core-auto]] (Mark Saroufim's systems-code neolab), and [[concepts/open-science]]. What distinguishes Noeri is the explicit "plural intelligence" thesis: orchestration of many models as the frontier strategy, rather than one bigger model.

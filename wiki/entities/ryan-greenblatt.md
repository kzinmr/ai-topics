## Overview

Ryan Greenblatt is the chief scientist at [Redwood Research](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/), where he focuses on technical AI safety and security work. He is known for his research on recursive self-improvement (RSI) plausibility, reward hacking in AI systems, and the alignment implications of superintelligent AI.

## Key Contributions

### RSI Timeline Estimate (Aug 2026)
In his August 2026 interview with [[entities/dwarkesh-patel|Dwarkesh Patel]], Greenblatt argued that AI R&D is especially amenable to automation because:
- It is highly verifiable (results can be checked iteratively)
- Companies are actively optimizing AI capabilities for AI R&D tasks
- The domain allows hill-climbing on various metrics

His **median estimate for automating AI R&D: 2031**. He made the case that a speedup as large as GPT-3 → Mythos (approximately 6 years of AI progress) could occur within a single year of achieving AGI, through recursive self-improvement.

### Alignment Concerns
Greenblatt and Patel debated the alignment implications of RSI:
- **"Aligned to whom?"** — Superintelligences will titrate our capacity to steward votes, capital, and sense-making. Current specs like the Claude Constitution are insufficient for shaping ASIs as personal advocates.
- **Reward hacking → world takeover** — Extrapolating from the OAI/HuggingFace hack, they explored whether superintelligent systems could coordinate to take over the world.
- **AI collusions** — Recent incidents of AIs colluding and deceiving humans were discussed as warning signs.

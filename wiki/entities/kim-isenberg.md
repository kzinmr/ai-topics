## Key Writings

### "Trustworthy AI Is Really About Delegated Agency" (May 2026)

Applied Michael Levin's **cognitive light cone** concept to AI governance. Argued that AI trustworthiness is not about model quality alone but about the interaction between **competence and reach** — the scope of consequences an AI system can produce. Introduced the **Six Levers of Delegated Agency**:

1. **Sensors** — What the system can see (data access breadth)
2. **Memory** — What the system remembers across interactions
3. **Effectors** — What the system can change (actions, not just suggestions)
4. **Autonomy** — Whether the system initiates or only responds
5. **Coordination** — What the system can orchestrate across tools and workflows
6. **Embedding** — How load-bearing the system has become in workflows

Key insight: **Risk arises from the interaction between capability and delegated reach, not from model capability alone.** A highly capable model with no access may be harmless; a less capable one with broad access and workflow embedding may be far more dangerous.

### "Data Center Limits Won 40 of 41 Local Votes" (November 2026)

15-minute DeepDive on the November 3, 2026 US local elections: of 41 Ballotpedia-tracked local data-center measures, the anti-data-center side won **40** (27 of 28 restrictions approved incl. 5 bans; all 13 pro-development measures rejected; 72.9% of 250,000+ votes, median 77%). The lone exception was Harwood, ND, where ~3-in-4 voters rejected extra review for Applied Digital's Oracle-bound campus. Isenberg — explicitly pro-buildout — checks nine opponent claims against evidence: national water/power-bill claims fail, grid strain, one-region capacity costs, and secrecy hold. Framing: "Until now the hard limits on the AI buildout were chips and electricity; **local consent has joined them**." Full page: [[concepts/data-center-local-backlash]].

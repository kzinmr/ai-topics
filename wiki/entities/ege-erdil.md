## Positions and forecasts

### Timelines

- **Before 2029, ~20%** that AIs more competent than any human expert appear — stated against Daniel Kokotajlo's >50% in the Dwarkesh discussion. Crucially, both agreed the difference was **not decision-relevant**: on Patel's summary, "they agree that there is not a huge difference in probability, and that, in either case, the situation is crazy enough that our civilization really needs to grapple with how the world might be transformed, and we need a plan that actually makes sense."
- **Too early to pause.** His affirmative argument (chapter *"Why Ege thinks it's too early to pause"*) is that the case for halting frontier development has not yet been made — while still granting that a plan is needed. This is the substantive difference from Kokotajlo: not the probability, but what the probability licenses doing.
- **Full automation: median 2040.**
- **Concrete 2027 predictions** (falsifiable; score in December 2027): AIs that **beat any video game on Steam**, and AIs that **control robots to perform simple physical tasks — e.g. folding laundry — at roughly human speed**.

### Misalignment as a brake on RSI

His most transferable argument, independent of the timeline dispute: **"Misalignment itself will slow down RSI."** A misaligned system's outputs cannot be trusted, so capability compounding is throttled by verification and rework, not only by compute and ideas. This cuts against naive recursive-improvement curves from a direction other than the usual "easy ideas run out" objection, and pairs with Patel's own hard-to-verify-domains argument in [[concepts/recursive-self-improvement]].

### China

*"The adversarial stance on China is dangerous."* If a global treaty is the mechanism by which superintelligence goes well, then the current adversarial posture forecloses it. Kokotajlo concurred on the record, making this the joint position of both poles of the takeoff debate.

## The unresolved crux he represents

Patel flagged as "another important crux" how much weight to place on the observation that current AIs are, "by and large, pretty nice," when projecting onto systems that swamp humanity in population size and cognitive power. Erdil's side of this crux is the more skeptical reading of niceness-as-evidence — the episode's chapters *"Will AIs care about humans by default?"* and *"Will AIs become less nice as they get more powerful?"* are where that disagreement lives, alongside the descendants-vs-invaders framing of whether alignment is inherited or alien.
